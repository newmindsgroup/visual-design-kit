# Generated media quality evaluation

## Inputs and workflow

Read the [media workflow](../../MEDIA-QUALITY-WORKFLOW.md) and reuse accepted upstream inputs. Use the [production packet](../../templates/media-quality-packet.md) for concrete inputs and outputs. Missing evidence stays explicit; tool availability is not production acceptance.

Use the shared rubric to inspect actual candidates. First reject factual product changes, incorrect required text, unusable crops, broken rights or missing required output specifications. Do not average these failures into a flattering score. Then score composition, brand fit, craft, readability and campaign coherence with visible evidence and exact frame/region references.

Review native-size output, contextual viewing scale and a candidate contact sheet with comparable crops. Video requires complete playback plus frame inspection around cuts, motion peaks and loop join; selected attractive frames alone are insufficient. Separate aesthetic preference from defects and user validation. Recommend accept-as-candidate, repair, reject or held for evidence, with a concrete repair order. Record independent review separately. Do not claim sales uplift, readability at a real distance or hardware compatibility from a desktop preview.

## Handoff and acceptance

Return artifact locations, exact baseline, candidate status, observed checks, remaining defects and next owner through the existing [handoff](../design-stage-handoff/SKILL.md). Apply the [rubric](../../templates/media-quality-rubric.md). Preserve original assets and follow the [media execution contract](../../MEDIA-GENERATION.md). Stop at the agreed revision/budget limit and report an unresolved result rather than silently changing the brief.

## Campaign-wide final review

Review a version-bound manifest of every still, loop, interactive screen, format and fallback. Compare contact sheets for product identity, type hierarchy, palette, light, message and disclosure consistency; also play all motion assets and walk the interactive sequence. Static thumbnails cannot reveal timing or interaction failures. Confirm alternate formats remain distinct compositions with the same brand logic. Check promotion validity and source version across primary/fallback variants. Record per-asset defects and campaign-level conflicts separately. A failure in one asset holds that asset and dependent variants; do not mark the whole campaign approved by averaging scores. Return the manifest, findings, repair owners and exact scope approved for the next stage.

For physical-screen work, use the [display production detail checklist](../../templates/display-production-details.md) and preserve actual test evidence.


## Photographic rejection gate

For photographic briefs, reject schematic products, implausible geometry, material response inconsistent with the specified material (real plastic is not a defect), inconsistent reflections or shadows, floating objects, malformed packaging, and visibly artificial compositing before scoring. Inspect the complete image, native-resolution material/edge/label crops and intended viewing size. Cite the failing region and required repair. Do not confuse deliberate photographic shadow or stylization approved in the brief with a defect.

Return separate decisions for photographic appearance, factual product fidelity, brand/art-direction fit, technical delivery and human acceptance. Unknown required evidence holds its dependent stage. Technical passes never override visual rejection. Freeze the accepted still version before motion; review temporal identity, texture stability, transitions and the whole loop separately. The same gate applies to fallback and adapted assets.

Apply the scope, evidence and decision-combination rules in the [shared rubric](../../templates/media-quality-rubric.md).
