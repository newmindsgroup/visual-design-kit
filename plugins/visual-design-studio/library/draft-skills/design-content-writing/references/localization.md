# Localization and resilient content

Keep stable message IDs and separate language text from presentation. Record locale, content owner, draft/reviewed status and terms that must not change. Translating preserves meaning; transcreation may change expression to serve the same intent and needs explicit scope. Never fabricate research or mark an AI-authored translation language-reviewed.

First use real target strings when available. Otherwise show authored translations as drafts in the specimen and handoff. Add pseudo-localized stress strings for expansion, diacritics, long words and short alternatives; pseudo text tests layout, not language. Test both growth and shrinkage. Percent expansion is a stress input, not a universal rule or guarantee.

Use logical alignment and spacing for direction changes. Isolate embedded URLs, identifiers and numbers with appropriate bidirectional markup. Mirror directional relationships when meaningful, not logos, photographs, media controls or every icon blindly. A synthetic RTL fixture can verify ordering but cannot establish Arabic shaping, reading quality or translation. Those require target-script fonts, real language samples and qualified review.

Check wrapping, truncation, language attributes, accessible names, plural/number/date conventions and altered emphasis at selected widths. Reuse the typography owner's exact-font/fallback evidence. Attach string IDs, source meanings, draft status, fit results and remaining reviewer to the current work/handoff. Never solve translation overflow by silently omitting conditions or reducing all type.
