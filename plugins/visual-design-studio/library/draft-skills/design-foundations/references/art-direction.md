# Image art direction

Start with the image's job: demonstrate a real product, explain a relationship, establish atmosphere or help recognition. A photograph suggests factual specificity; a diagram can simplify relationships; illustration can explain a concept without inventing a photographed event. Do not generate a false product feature to improve a composition.

Choose between photography, illustration, collage and schematic treatment against the same brief. For each direction record subject and action; viewpoint and perspective; light direction and softness; material/texture; palette roles; background complexity; crop-safe subject bounds; space reserved for copy; realism/factual constraints; and intended family behavior. For photography specify a shot list rather than only a mood adjective. For illustration specify shape, edge, stroke, perspective and detail conventions. Treat collage as separate licensed elements with coherent scale and lighting intent.

Build a small contact sheet before final production. Compare whether the subject reads at the smallest use, whether its shape competes with text, and whether siblings belong together without becoming duplicates. Reject candidates for factual drift, ambiguous subject, inconsistent light/perspective, impossible anatomy when relevant, illegible embedded text or excessive detail. Use a different crop when the subject is lost; do not merely make it smaller.

Handoff through design-work: direction ID, image-role/shot IDs, source references and rights, invariants, selected proposal, rejected alternative/reason, subject-safe region, production route and artifact versions. Selection by the AI is a recommendation, not brand approval. Production owns editable masters and exports. Use the laboratory's two directions and three illustrations as examples of family reasoning, never mandatory styling.
