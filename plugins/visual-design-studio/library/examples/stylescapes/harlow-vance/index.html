An appeal is not a second trial. The court reads the record that was made below, and the brief tells it what that record means. We are retained early, while that record is still being written, so that the questions that decide a case are preserved, briefed and argued by the same partner.

Our associates draft; our partners argue. Clients receive a written assessment of the appeal within ten business days of instruction.

vi.

Imagery & iconography

Architecture and paper, never gavels.

Photography is black and white, shot in raking daylight: courthouse stone, stair rails, a bound volume with its spine cracked, a partner at eye level with no props. No gavels, scales, handshakes or skylines. Where a photograph is not available, use a line engraving in brass on library green.

Icons are drawn on a 24px grid with a 1.25px stroke, butt caps and mitred corners, like a ruled form. Every icon is paired with a label.

vii.

Motion

Deliberate. Nothing bounces; the rule is drawn, the verdict is set.

Motion behaves like a pen on paper: rules draw downward, lines of text arrive in reading order, and outcomes appear without flourish. It is used on first view and on state change only, never on scroll loops.

ix.

Rationale

Why the record, and not the skyline.

Why it fits

Appellate clients are general counsel and trial firms buying judgment. The pleading-paper margin and Caslon (the face of the 1776 Dunlap printing of the Declaration of Independence) borrow the authority of the court document itself, so the Ruler archetype comes from order and restraint rather than gold and crests.

The ledger turns the firm’s strongest evidence, outcomes, into the hero, with the required past-results disclaimer built into the layout instead of hidden in the footer.

What it risks

  • Antiquarian drift: without the hard grid and plain copy it becomes a costume. Keep the modern 12-column structure and short sentences.
  • Outcome numbers are attorney advertising. Rules on past-results claims and disclaimers vary by state (New York requires “Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome”); confirm with ethics counsel before launch.
  • Caslon Display hairlines fail below 40px and on low-DPI screens; the system switches to Caslon Text there.

The expected alternative

Navy and gold, a skyline or glass-tower photograph, a scales-of-justice icon and the headline “Experience. Integrity. Results.” It is safe and indistinguishable from the other 200 firms a general counsel will shortlist. Corporate-modern sans with a single bold claim would be the adjacent option; this direction keeps that clarity but owns a document-native signature.
