03 / palette

Grey is the brand. Color is an event.

Following high-performance HMI practice (ISA-101): normal states are greyscale, so the three alarm colors can be found in a glance across a 40-inch screen. Ratios computed with WCAG 2 relative luminance.

No green for "running".A screen full of green lights trains people to stop looking. Running equipment is dark grey; stopped is hollow and dashed.

Shape plus number plus color.Every alarm carries all three, so red-green color vision deficiency (roughly 1 in 12 men) never hides a priority.

Red is never decoration.Not in the logo, not in charts, not in marketing. If it is red, someone should be walking toward the line.

06 / applications

Read from 10 meters. Act from a pocket.

The andon board above the line shows three numbers and one problem. The maintenance tech gets the same alarm on a phone with the evidence attached.

07 / imagery

Symbols an engineer already reads.

Equipment is drawn as simplified P&ID symbols (in the spirit of ISA-5.1): 1.5px strokes on a 24px grid, no perspective, no gradients, no 3D renderings of machines. Marketing photography, when used, is real plants at real shift times with real PPE.

The texture of the brand is graph paper: a 16px grid that shows up behind diagrams and in empty states.

09 / voice

Finding first, then evidence, then action.

Write like a good shift lead: the conclusion, the number that proves it, the next step. No exclamation marks, no "Oops", no blame.

Headline

Line 3 is running 4% below plan. Capper C-304 is why.

States the finding and the cause in under 12 words; the trend carries the proof.

CTA

Acknowledge alarm

The ISA-18.2 verb operators already use. Never "Dismiss" or "Got it", which imply the problem is gone.

Error

Lost connection to PLC-3 at 14:02:11. Values shown are last known and marked STALE. Retrying every 5 s.

When it happened, what you are looking at now, what the system is doing about it.

10 / rationale

Why grey wins on the plant floor.

The direction, what it risks, and the default we chose not to make.

Why it fits

  • Operators watch for 8-12 hours. Low-glare grey with sparse color reduces fatigue and makes the abnormal impossible to miss; this is the core of ISA-101 high-performance HMI guidance.
  • Sage archetype: the product earns trust by explaining (finding, evidence, action), not by looking futuristic.
  • Expert users get density, tabular numbers and shortcuts instead of oversized cards.

What it risks

  • Grey demos poorly to buyers who expect a "command center". Show the alarm scenario in sales demos, not the calm state.
  • Operators trained on colorful legacy HMIs may read grey as "broken"; plan an onboarding note and a transition period.
  • Density needs good monitors; the 1280px layout must stay usable on older control-room screens.

Category-expected alternative

A dark "mission control" dashboard with neon gauges, 3D renders of the plant, green status lights everywhere and red used for branding (style.dark-tech-developer). It sells in a boardroom and fails at 3 a.m. when everything glows the same.

Validate alarm priorities and colors with the site's alarm philosophy document (ISA-18.2 / IEC 62682) and the plant's own HMI standard before rollout.
