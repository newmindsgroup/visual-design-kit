07 · ImageryTidewater Collective

Imagery & iconography

Documentary, wide and wet. Named people at work in their own landscape; data drawn as part of the place.

Photograph volunteers and residents mid-task, at low tide, wide enough to show the coast they are protecting. Name every person and place in the caption, with consent. Never use flooded houses as spectacle, a lone polar bear, or a seedling in cupped hands.

Illustrations are cross-sections and maps in the palette’s naturals, always with a labelled sea-level line and a source. Icons use a 1.75px round stroke on a 24px grid with one Life-ring detail where it carries meaning.

10 · RationaleTidewater Collective

Rationale

Why a field magazine, and not another green leaf.

Why it fits

Climate donors are fatigued by alarm and suspicious of vague impact. An editorial system lets every appeal arrive as reporting: a named place, a dated event, a sourced projection and a price per foot. The Hero archetype lives in the residents and volunteers doing the work; the donor joins them.

The cross-section turns the sea-level projection into the key visual, so the most important fact on the page is also the most beautiful thing on it.

What it risks

  • Editorial pace can bury the ask. The donation module stays in the first viewport on desktop and directly under the headline on phones.
  • Data must stay honest: projections need the scenario, baseline and source next to the line, and audited figures must be current.
  • Life-ring red reads as alarm if it spreads. Keep it to 3% of the surface.
  • Donation forms carry legal text (501(c)(3) status, recurring-gift terms, state solicitation disclosures); confirm with counsel.

The expected alternative

Leaf-green gradient, a seedling in cupped hands, a sad photograph of a flooded street and “Together we can make a difference” over a $1-a-day ask. It reads as worthy and interchangeable. The adjacent option is a purely documentary photo site; it is strong, but needs a photo budget this brief does not assume.
