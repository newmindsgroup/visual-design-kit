06Applications

The debit card is the brand's billboard in every bar and bakery; stories and push notifications are where Gen Z actually meets the bank.

07Imagery and icons

Drawn, not photographed. The brand looks like a sticker sheet, never like a stock photo of a smiling couple at a laptop.

Icons are drawn on a 24px grid with a 2.5px stroke, square caps and mitre joins, and they sit inside outlined tiles filled with one palette color. Illustration is flat sticker art: thick ink outline, no gradients, no 3D coins, no shields or padlocks as decoration.

When photography appears (campaigns, not the app) it is flash-lit phone photography of real friend groups in real places in São Paulo, Recife and Belém, cropped hard and placed inside an outlined frame with a hard shadow.

  • One fill per icon tile; never two saturated colors touching without ink between.
  • Money metaphors stay literal: a pote, a split, a receipt. No piggy banks, no rockets.
  • Stickers rotate between −12° and +12°; UI elements never rotate.

09Voice

Talks like a friend who is good with money: "você", "grana", short sentences. Never jokes about debt, fraud or a failed payment.

HeadlineSua grana, sem pedágio.

"Your money, no tolls." Brazilians know the pain of toll booths; the metaphor needs no explanation.

CTAAbrir minha conta

"Open my account." First person, verb first, no "Saiba mais".

ErrorEsse Pix não saiu. Sua grana continua na conta. Tenta de novo em 1 minuto ou fala com a gente.

"This Pix didn't go through. Your money is still in your account. Try again in 1 minute or talk to us." Says what happened, that money is safe, and what to do.

Say the number."Tarifa: R$ 0,00" beats "sem custos". Every fee in reais, before confirmation.

Use "você", never "o cliente".Informal second person is normal for youth banking in Brazil; keep slang light so it ages well.

Drop the volume on money screens.Review, confirm and error states are plain and calm; the loud voice lives in marketing and stickers.

10Rationale

Why this direction, what could go wrong, and what we deliberately did not do.

Why it fits

  • Gen Z in Brazil already banks on their phone; the differentiator is attitude and transparency, not features. Neo-brutalism reads as "nothing hidden": every box is outlined, every fee is printed.
  • Value tier plus everyperson archetype: cheap-to-produce flat color, no photography budget needed, works on entry-level Android screens.
  • Lime, coral and sky stand apart from the purple, orange and yellow that dominate Brazilian digital banking shelves.

What it risks

  • Can feel unserious when balances grow. Mitigation: transaction review, Pix confirmation and errors use calm white surfaces, no stickers, no rotation.
  • Neo-brutalism is fashionable; it may date in 3-4 years. The 3px outline and lime are the only must-keeps; the stickers can retire.
  • Hard shadows double visual weight on small screens; cap them at 4px inside the app.

Category-expected alternative

A purple or navy flat-minimal neobank (style.corporate-modern + archetype.ruler): rounded sans, soft cards, a glowing card in 3D. Credible, but indistinguishable from the incumbents Vibra is positioned against.

Before production: confirm Banco Central disclosure wording, LGPD consent flows and the Pix brand-usage rules for the "Pix" name and mark with counsel.
