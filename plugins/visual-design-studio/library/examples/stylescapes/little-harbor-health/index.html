Harbor night for reassurance, lamplight for the one thing to do next, sea-glass teal for every action in the product. Ratios computed with WCAG 2 relative luminance.

7Imagery and icons

A small harbor world: lighthouse, boats, buoys and waves. Doctors are shown as real photographs on profile pages, where trust beats charm.

Illustration uses round joins, no outlines on large shapes, and a two-step wave rhythm. Characters have minimal faces and a range of skin tones; no anthropomorphic germs, no syringes, no frightening red crosses.

Icons sit on a 24px grid with a 2px stroke, round caps and joins, inside a 72px tint circle. Symptoms are drawn as the body part or the sensation, never as a sick, crying child.

8Motion

Slow and buoyant. Things float in and settle; nothing snaps, flashes or shakes. Waiting is the most animated state, because waiting is when parents worry most.

10Rationale

Why this direction, where it could go wrong, and the safe option we chose not to make.

Why it fits

  • The real competitor is "wait until morning". A night-harbor world says "we're awake" faster than any headline.
  • Playful shapes give kids something friendly to look at during the call; the clinical system (Atkinson, 56px inputs, one action per screen) keeps tired parents from making mistakes.
  • Caregiver archetype: lamplight is a promise of someone watching over, without hands-and-hearts clichés.

What it risks

  • Illustration can read as less clinical. Real doctor photos, credentials and licensure states must appear on doctor profiles and pricing.
  • Night palette could be mistaken for a sleep app; daytime pages flip to Fog and Sea foam with the same shapes.
  • Kid-facing stickers must not become engagement mechanics; no streaks, no collecting pressure.

Category-expected alternative

White and medical blue, a stock photo of a smiling doctor on a laptop and a mom holding a child, a red cross icon (style.clinical-calm + style.corporate-modern). Trustworthy, forgettable, and slightly scary to a four-year-old.

Before launch, confirm with counsel: HIPAA for visit data, COPPA for anything a child interacts with directly (accounts belong to parents), state telehealth licensing and prescribing rules, and claims like "board-certified" and wait times.
