# Choreograph a message for the actual screen

Version-Timestamp: 2026-09-27 14:20:29 AST

Use for passive screen content, a campaign loop or a purposeful interface animation. Inputs: approved message/assets, actual canvas and player limits, viewing/dwell assumptions, interaction mode, product truth, motion purpose and permitted changes. This is original authored guidance; the miniature is fictional, with no rendered or field-tested result.

## Make the decision

1. Start from what a person can understand in the viewing context. Identify message, supporting proof and next action, including a viewer arriving midway through a loop. Treat dimensions, safe regions, panel seams and operator limits as supplied constraints on content. This card does not design fixtures, electrical systems or installation hardware.
2. Design the held composition first. A clear still establishes type, product prominence and spatial hierarchy. Check native-size artwork and a context preview; a polished perspective mockup can conceal weak flat artwork. If a factual product is shown, use approved art and inspect name, geometry, proportions and labels. A vector diagram is appropriate for explanation; it is not a substitute for a required photographic product image.
3. Give movement a specific job: reveal a relationship, direct attention between related elements, explain a transformation, or acknowledge an interaction. Reject movement that merely occupies time or competes with the message. A simple zoom can be appropriate emphasis, but does not alone establish a considered motion language.
4. Plan choreography as entry, meaningful change, readable hold and exit. Decide what moves together and why; shared motion implies a relationship. Use anticipation, stagger, continuity, easing and settling only when they support the intended sequence. Establish visual anchors so the audience does not need to reacquire every object. Avoid distorting product or logo geometry for expressive movement unless explicitly permitted.
5. Build a low-cost animatic before detailed production. Test whether the viewer can read and understand the complete claim, including qualifiers, in silence and from an arbitrary entry point. Use actual copy rather than guessed timing formulas. Shorten or split a scene before making its text unreadably small or accelerating every transition.
6. Specify behavior and verify the target. Reuse the motion record's existing interruption, fallback, reduced/static, failed-media and playback fields. For interactive work, task feedback and focus take precedence over choreography. For rendered loops, inspect the full export, join, captions where needed, product consistency and applicable flash analysis. A smooth editor preview cannot establish player performance.

## Worked miniature

Fictional reusable bottle “Tern” has an approved photograph and the supported message “Refill. Reuse.” A candidate spins a generated bottle while zooming each word. It adds activity but risks invented back-label detail and makes the copy harder to follow.

The proposed alternative keeps the real bottle anchored, introduces the words as a readable phrase, and uses a restrained graphic path to connect a supplied refill instruction to the product. The path is explicitly symbolic, not a demonstration of an internal mechanism. A held frame carries the complete meaning; motion guides the sequence. The animatic must still establish appropriate reading time and mid-loop comprehension. No candidate advances to product-fidelity acceptance without comparing actual rendered frames to approved art.

## Failure and repair

If a loop needs sound or its first frame to make sense, add a stable message anchor or recompose self-contained beats. If type, product and background all move independently, reduce competing movement and assign a clear focal handoff. If product details drift, repair the asset/composite before animating; exporting a rejected still as a video does not improve it.

## Handoff and limits

Use `display-art-direction`, `screen-storyboard`, `motion` and `screen-product-fidelity` only as selected. Reuse the [display brief](../templates/display-design-brief.md), [motion specification](../templates/motion-spec.md) and [media packet](../templates/media-quality-packet.md). They already own target constraints, timing, fallback, asset identity and execution records.

Check actual stills, full playback, preserved product facts, arbitrary loop entry, readable holds and target evidence. This card specifies no universal durations or safety thresholds. Artistic review, player verification, field legibility, flash safety and human acceptance remain separate.
