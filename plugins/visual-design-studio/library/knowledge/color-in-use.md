# Choose color through roles and relationships

Version-Timestamp: 2026-09-27 14:20:29 AST

Use when building a palette, extending an approved palette into interface states, or repairing a color decision that only works as swatches. Inputs: brand authority, semantic roles, actual copy/images, states, themes, medium and applicable criteria. This is original authored guidance; the miniature is fictional and contains no measured contrast claim.

## Make the decision

1. Separate recognition from function. An approved brand color can identify the organization without serving as body text, focus, error and chart highlight simultaneously. Preserve the primitive value and decide where it is fit for use. If a required role fails, propose an allowed pairing or functional variant instead of silently editing brand authority.
2. Define what each role must distinguish. A selected option must be identifiable beside an unselected one; an error must convey a recoverable problem; a data scale must communicate category or order. Start from those relationships, then choose candidates. A hue name does not predict a universal emotion or business outcome.
3. Compare candidates in an identical realistic composition. Inspect the large surfaces, image interaction, text hierarchy and competing accents. Change hue, lightness or chroma deliberately so you can explain what improved. More saturated accents may increase competition with product imagery; quieter surfaces may help it read but reduce brand prominence. Choose against the brief rather than a universal accent percentage.
4. Map primitives to semantic tokens, then enumerate actual pairings across relevant states/themes. Evaluate composited foreground/background values, including overlays and imagery. A passing swatch pair does not transfer to a translucent button over a photograph. If the background cannot be bounded, introduce a stable surface or change placement.
5. Add non-color meaning where color carries information: text, shape, position, pattern or an explicit state indicator. A chart legend alone may force unnecessary matching; direct labels can reduce it. Do not reuse success/error semantics for arbitrary categories if that implies judgment unsupported by the data.
6. Verify the actual medium and update dependencies. Web contrast criteria depend on the rendered role and applicable standard. Print/device reproduction depends on output conditions and proofing. A typography or token change invalidates affected pair results. Recheck those pairs, not an unrelated palette as a whole.

## Worked miniature

Fictional service “Northbank Repairs” has an approved bright yellow recognition color. The booking page initially uses white text on yellow for all actions, and yellow outlines for focus and selection. The proposed repair retains yellow in the brand area, evaluates a dark-text/yellow action pair, and gives focus a distinct, tested treatment with visible shape. Selection also gains a check and label.

The color decision lists each actual pair and its state. No pair is marked passed until measured under the applicable criterion. The proposal's reason is distinguishing actions and states while retaining recognition, not a claim that yellow makes people optimistic. A printed receipt needs its own proof because a web token does not specify ink behavior.

## Failure and repair

If individually pleasing colors become noisy together, inspect area, adjacency and competing emphasis before replacing every hue. If a palette is described as “accessible,” replace that blanket claim with the tested pairs, roles and states. If brand and functional meanings conflict, preserve the approved primitive and propose scoped semantic alternatives with the identity owner.
