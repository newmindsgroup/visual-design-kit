# Choose the graphic from the reader's question

Version-Timestamp: 2026-09-27 14:20:29 AST

Use when facts or relationships need visual explanation and several chart/layout choices seem plausible. Inputs: reader question, takeaway, source data/text, units, uncertainty, medium and required accessible equivalent. This is original authored guidance; all values in the miniature are fictional teaching data.

## Make the decision

1. Finish the sentence “The reader needs to see...” with a relationship: a ranking, change over time, distribution, location, part of a whole, or process. Choose one primary question. A visual can contain several facts, but equal emphasis on unrelated questions usually obscures the takeaway.
2. Check that the information supports that relationship. Counts do not establish rates without denominators. A before/after difference does not establish cause. Missing values are not zero, estimates are not observations, and unequal time intervals need truthful spacing. For a conceptual process, do not give shape width or area a quantitative meaning it does not have.
3. Choose an encoding that makes the comparison direct. Aligned lengths often suit comparing counts; positions over a real time axis can show change; a table may best serve exact lookup; an ordered diagram can explain sequence. Choose maps for meaningful spatial relationships, not merely because place names exist. Explain why the rejected form asks the reader to decode more or implies the wrong thing.
4. Establish hierarchy before illustration: takeaway, primary evidence, explanation, qualifications and source. Keep annotations adjacent to what they explain. Use direct labels when they remove legend hunting. Preserve contextual qualifications even if that means reducing ornament or dividing the graphic into a sequence.
5. Generate the chart, labels and accessible table from the same canonical values. Use plotting or vector geometry for quantitative marks; generated imagery may support the story but must not invent the chart geometry or exact text. If the source is an image, verify transcription before calculation and rendering.
6. Adapt the reading order to the medium. A poster's columns may need a sequential mobile layout; an unattended screen may need a simpler supported takeaway with an available complete explanation. If simplification removes an essential qualification, choose another format or revise the communication goal. Provide a text explanation of the relationship as well as data access.

## Worked miniature

Fictional repair-event counts are 18 clothing items, 12 small appliances and 6 bicycles. The reader asks which category received the most completed repairs. Ordered bars with direct labels answer that question; the values sum to 36. The specification describes counts of completed repairs, not repair success rates, because attempted repairs were not supplied.

A decorative funnel is rejected because these categories are not sequential stages. A bicycle icon three times the area of another icon is also rejected: its pictorial geometry would obscure the intended comparison. The accompanying sentence says clothing had the largest count in this fictional event. It does not claim higher demand generally or better repair effectiveness.

## Failure and repair

If a graphic looks impressive but needs a long explanation to decode, redraw the structure around the primary comparison before changing its colors. If a headline overstates the evidence, correct the headline or obtain the missing data; do not hide the limitation in tiny type. If mobile fit depends on shrinking the entire poster, reflow the sequence and preserve labels and source context.

## Handoff and limits

Use `infographics` and the existing [infographic specification](../templates/infographic-spec.md). Its element IDs bind exact text, source, units, encoding and accessible equivalents. Reuse [information design](../draft-skills/design-infographics/references/information-design.md) and the skill's existing official complex-image and contrast references.

Check source transcription, calculations, denominators, units, proportional geometry, labels, reading order and actual export size. Verify that the textual alternative explains the main relationship. Arithmetic correctness does not establish source truth, causality, audience comprehension or full accessibility.
