# Turn uncertainty into a design brief

Version-Timestamp: 2026-09-27 14:20:29 AST

Use when a design decision depends on unclear audience needs, conflicting brand sources, or an incomplete brief. Inputs: requested outcome, intended use, allowed sources, current baseline, decision owner and known constraints. This is original authored guidance; the miniature below is fictional and has not been tested with users.

## Make the decision

1. Write the choice that research must change: “Choose between a task-first and department-first navigation,” rather than “research the audience.” Name who will use the answer and what happens if it is wrong. Research without a decision often collects facts that cannot guide the design.
2. Separate locked constraints, supported facts, interpretations and open questions. “The supplied guideline requires this logo” governs use within its scope. “Several competitors use blue” is an observation. “Blue will improve trust” is an unsupported prediction. Do not promote one class into another by repeating it.
3. Prioritize questions by consequence, uncertainty and the cost of reversing the choice. Ask first about a fact that could invalidate the whole direction. Defer a minor preference that can be tested in a reversible specimen. This preserves room for exploration without pretending every missing detail blocks all work.
4. Match the method to the uncertainty. Inspect official product material for factual features, existing artifacts for consistency, task observation for interaction problems, interviews for reported needs, and comparative prototypes for the usability of alternatives. A participant's explanation is useful supplied evidence, but does not replace observing whether the task succeeds. Recruit for relevant behavior/context rather than decorative demographic detail.
5. Resolve contradictions by scope, authority and freshness. A newer campaign guide may govern only one campaign; an older master logo may remain canonical. Record the conflict and affected choice. Do not average contradictory prices, infer rights from possession, or silently choose the source that makes the design easier.
6. Convert the finding into a requirement with a visible consequence and a test. Preserve the evidence locator and limitation. Stop collecting when the named decision has enough support for its intended use, or when a specific unresolved fact holds that decision. More sources do not automatically improve the brief.

## Worked miniature

Fictional tool-lending service “Borrow Bench” requests a new home page. Its supplied task log records visitors asking how to reserve equipment; its manager prefers an “About / Services / Resources” structure. These are different inputs, not votes of equal type. The proposed requirement is “Make the reservation entry visible and describe what happens next.” A provisional task-first candidate uses “Find a tool” and a short reservation explanation. The department-first candidate keeps the same content for comparison.

The next test asks eligible participants to find and attempt a reservation in each prototype. Record task behavior and questions; do not write a persona claiming that “busy urban makers love convenience.” If participant access is unavailable, the brief can support internal concept exploration with the usability question explicitly open. It cannot claim a proven improvement.

## Failure and repair

If the report contains many trends but cannot explain a layout choice, trace each finding to the pending decision and remove irrelevant material. If one invented persona is carrying several conclusions, replace it with the supported actor/task/context and mark the unsupported conclusions as hypotheses. Follow the existing audience-input rule before dependent use; this card does not grant provisional authority.

## Handoff and limits

Use capability `research` and the existing [research brief](../templates/research-brief.md). Put the decision, prioritized questions, source register, evidence status, requirements and refresh trigger there; link [design work](../templates/design-work.md) and [UX deliverable](../templates/ux-deliverable.md) only when selected. Reuse [research contracts](../RESEARCH-CAPABILITIES.md).

Check that each material requirement traces to a source or a declared hypothesis, that contradictions have an owner, and that the next consumer can distinguish usable inputs from held ones. Desk research cannot establish observed user behavior, source possession cannot establish reuse rights, and a fictional example cannot establish either.
