## The important distinctions

A **sitemap** organizes pages or destinations. A **task flow** describes a bounded task, while a **user flow** includes relevant entry points, decisions and alternative paths. A **journey map** follows an actor's experience over time and across touchpoints. A **service blueprint** connects that experience to delivery operations. A **wireflow** combines screen structure with transitions. They can link to one another; one does not automatically replace another. These distinctions are consistent with [NN/g's glossary](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-deliverables-glossary/) and its [service-blueprint guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/service-blueprints-definition/). Naming varies between teams, so declare the actual scope and intended decision.

## Select before producing

For each candidate below record **create, reuse, omit or hold**, the decision it supports, the selection reason, current input references and next consumer. Omit when there is no relevant decision. Reuse only after checking scope, freshness and actual approval. Hold only the affected downstream use when a necessary fact is missing. An exploratory hypothesis can support a clearly provisional concept, but cannot stand in for observed research or production approval.

The brief, actor goal and inclusive-use considerations are baseline questions for relevant work; they can be satisfied by existing records and do not require three new documents. Other contracts activate on their stated triggers.

The IDs UXD-01 to UXD-30 below identify deliverable contracts, not new component inventory items. A project may combine several contracts in one document if their IDs, ownership and checks remain clear. Separate maps are justified by different actors, goals, evidence or service boundaries, not merely by alternate names such as customer versus user.

| UXD-08 Context and ecosystem map | Multiple actors, channels, organizations or physical constraints matter | Actors, environments, touchpoints, ownership, relationships and dependencies from research | Declare system boundary; validate responsibilities with owners; feeds journey, blueprint and physical requirements |

| UXD-10 Service blueprint | Delivery depends on staff, backstage systems or cross-team handoffs | Linked journey steps, customer actions, visible staff/system actions, backstage work, supporting processes, ownership, dependencies, failures and recovery | Each handoff has an owner; unknown service capability blocks only dependent promises; feeds operational and interaction contracts |
| UXD-11 Task analysis | A task is complex, safety-sensitive, unfamiliar or involves expert work | Goal/subgoals, action sequence, knowledge, tools, preconditions, variations, frequency and failure consequence | Distinguish observed current task from proposed UI steps; feeds user flows and task-based evaluation |
| UXD-12 Prioritized requirements and assumptions | Decide what to design/test next | Need and evidence references, functional/content/context/accessibility requirements, priority basis, risk, acceptance criteria, owner and assumptions | Priority is reasoned, not invented precision; each requirement supports an outcome; feeds IA, flows and evaluation |
| UXD-13 Content inventory and audit | Existing content will be reused, migrated or reorganized | Content IDs, actual location/type, owner, audience/task, freshness, quality, duplication, rights and keep/change/remove proposal | Inventory is observed existence; removal remains a proposal until authorized; feeds sitemap and content model |
| UXD-14 Content model, taxonomy and vocabulary | Repeated content entities, relationships or labels must stay consistent | Entity/field definitions, relations, ownership, controlled terms, labeling rules, locale, required/optional data and source | Domain owner resolves constraints; labels match user language evidence; feeds IA, search, screens and content production |
| UXD-15 Sitemap or destination hierarchy | A website/app has multiple destinations needing organization | Stable node IDs, parent/child relations, label, destination type, purpose, audience/need, content links, access scope and navigation inclusion | Identify roots, orphans, duplicates, broken parents and intentional cross-links; do not confuse it with an XML search-engine sitemap; feeds navigation and page inventory |
| UXD-16 IA evaluation plan and results | Grouping, labels or findability are uncertain | Card-sort/tree-test questions, tasks, sample criteria, method, observed routes, outcomes, interpretation and proposed revision | Card sorting and tree testing are methods, not mandatory separate deliverables; report actual sample/limitations; feeds taxonomy/sitemap revisions |
| UXD-17 User/task flow and state model | An interactive task has actions, branches or state changes | Actor/goal, entry/preconditions, node IDs, transitions/conditions, success, alternate, error, back, cancel, recovery, permission and data effects | Branches resolve or have explicit holds; no unsafe retry for uncertain mutation; feeds wireframes, implementation and scenario tests |
| UXD-18 Navigation, search and orientation specification | People must locate destinations or resume context | Global/local navigation, search/filter/sort needs, label source, history/deep-link/back rules, empty/error states and access rules | Every control links to real destinations or defined states; feeds screen patterns and keyboard/assistive checks |
| UXD-19 Concept sketch and storyboard | Competing solutions or physical context need early exploration | Alternatives, scenario/need, sequence, rationale, assumptions, tradeoffs and decision sought | Drawings are hypotheses, not validated solutions; select the cheapest representation that answers the question; feeds chosen flow/layout |
| UXD-20 Wireframes and screen inventory | Hierarchy, content and layout need resolution before detailed UI | Screen/state IDs, flow links, regions, content priority, real or labeled placeholder content, controls, responsive/context behavior and annotations | Every selected flow state has a representation or reason for omission; avoid implying decorative polish is usability evidence; feeds UI and prototype |
| UXD-21 Wireflow and interaction annotations | Screen layout and movement must be understood together | References to UXD-17/20, transition IDs, trigger, feedback, focus and state changes | Reuse the flow edges rather than maintaining a contradictory second graph; feeds prototype/engineering |
| UXD-22 UX content and message specification | Wording affects task success, errors, consent or comprehension | Message IDs, intent, trigger, visible/accessible wording, variables, tone source, locale and recovery action | Truthful statuses; no success before confirmation; no unresolved placeholder in adopted output; feeds screens and content review |
| UXD-23 Accessibility and inclusive-use requirements | Every selected user-facing experience | Relevant user needs, input/output modes, standards basis/version/applicability, focus/reading order, alternatives, test methods and exceptions | Plan from the start; automated or model checks never establish all conformance; feeds every design and evaluation stage |
| UXD-24 Prototype and simulation record | A concrete uncertainty benefits from interaction or comprehension testing | Hypothesis, selected flows/states, fidelity, tool/version, executable links if built, simulated data/service behavior and omissions | Verify actual built interactions; distinguish prototype specification from running prototype; feeds evaluation |
| UXD-25 Evaluation protocol and usability report | Validate a risky assumption or changed experience | Questions, representative tasks/users/context, success/severity rules defined before sessions, observed outcomes, assistance, issues and limits | Planned studies have no findings; heuristic/cognitive walkthroughs are expert reviews, not participant tests; feeds revisions |
| UXD-26 Issue, decision and retest log | Evaluation or constraints require change | Issue/evidence IDs, impact, affected requirements/states, owner, proposed fix, decision, actual change and retest result | Closing an issue needs evidence; invalidate dependent results when relevant; feeds an updated candidate and handoff |
| UXD-27 User-story map or delivery slices | Teams must stage a larger experience into coherent increments | Goals/activities/tasks, requirement/flow IDs, ordered slices, dependencies, acceptance and excluded scope | A journey map describes experience; delivery slices describe work to release. | UXD-28 Measurement and learning plan | Post-release behavior or outcome measurement will inform decisions | Outcome/question, user class/context, task/instrument, measure and metric definition/units/denominator, events, properties, privacy boundary, baseline, target and rationale, actual observed result or pending, owner and decision trigger | Do not deploy analytics or collect data by writing a plan; avoid invented targets; feeds authorized measurement and future research |
| UXD-29 Design and engineering handoff | Another person/agent must act on the work | Existing stage packet, work record and manifest; source versions, decisions, state/content/component mappings, checks, open holds and next owner | Separate readiness from approval; proposed paths are not produced files; feeds only the authorized next action |
| UXD-30 Physical/shared-device interaction specification | Displays, kiosks or public touchpoints are selected | Context/viewing/reach evidence, hardware/player constraints, touch/keyboard options, session privacy, idle/reset, interrupted/offline flows, content scheduling and fallback | Validate intended environment and device; desktop emulation cannot certify reach, glare, touch or reset privacy; feeds production and physical evaluation |

## Progression without unnecessary documents

Follow the existing six stages, reusing accepted inputs. A typical interactive project has these dependencies:

1. **Understand:** UXD-01/02/03 establish the question and evidence. UXD-04 to 11 are selected views that turn evidence into needs and context. An already accepted persona or journey need not be recreated.
2. **Establish direction:** UXD-12 states priorities and testable requirements, alongside accepted brand strategy and identity. Research or evidence gaps can send work back to Understand.
3. **Explore:** UXD-13 to 19 resolve content, task logic and alternatives. Sitemap and user flow can evolve together; the sitemap does not prove task completion.
4. **Design:** UXD-20 to 24 define screens, content, inclusive behavior and a prototype at useful fidelity. Accessibility and UX content start earlier and continue here; they are not end-stage decorations.
5. **Validate:** UXD-16 and 25/26 evaluate the actual question. Return to the earliest invalidated requirement, flow, content or layout; do not polish a disproven concept.
6. **Hand off:** UXD-27 to 29 prepare selected delivery slices and an evidence-linked handoff. UXD-30 applies across all stages for physical/shared experiences. Measurement planning begins when outcomes are defined, even if deployment comes later.

The normal chain is evidence -> need -> requirement -> flow/destination -> screen/state/content -> implementation criterion -> result. Each arrow must reference IDs and versions. A single-page informational display may need comprehension and context records without a sitemap, navigation or interactive flow. A precise label revision can reuse most upstream work and reassess only affected dependencies.

## Book-informed refinements

The source version records the private book audit and its extraction limits; that audit is not a package runtime dependency. These are method recommendations, not universal rules:

- About Face 3, source f7e223e85ed5ef35, PDF pages 144/152/154: distinguish persona priorities and context/key-path/validation scenarios. Apply the distinction to UXD-05/06/12/24/25 without inventing research or requiring multiple documents.
- The UX Book 2, source 9029cfd459ec0fb8, PDF pages 184/261/489/494: use synthesis proportionate to complexity, retain requirements beyond user stories, and define measurement before interpreting results. Apply to UXD-04/12/25/27/28.
- UXpin Process Documentation, source ae93f6b09fb9d4e4, PDF pages 44/59: IA evaluation and cross-touchpoint mapping are purposeful options. Apply to UXD-09/16 rather than require them universally.
- Seven Step UX, source 899a7df6268802b0, PDF page 66, uses journey language for task flows. Preserve source terminology in provenance, but classify its actual task/transition scope as UXD-17 rather than silently merging it with UXD-09.
- UX Strategy, source ec9b3e77b29ef1a4, PDF pages 74/118: customer/problem hypotheses and competitive opportunity work support UXD-01/06/07/12. A hypothesis is not a validated market fact.

Mental Models source 0f63ee0670e8d508 has inadequate body extraction for substantive claims in this audit. UXD-04's mental-model option remains a proposed method with limited book support here, not a verified extraction from that title. Old tool references and books do not define current native or accessibility requirements.

The Guide to Wireframing, source 1b68204df4993844, PDF pages 7 to 9, supports audience-appropriate structural communication in UXD-20/21 without implying a working prototype. Designing Brand Identity, source 3c7f1c6ff1f46f91, PDF page 144, supports positioning/message/identity comparison in UXD-07, distinct from usability benchmarking. The UX Book 2, PDF page 474, distinguishes expert inspection from participant evaluation in UXD-25. The UXpin feature-priority card sort at PDF page 27 is not treated as taxonomy-validation evidence.

Book support remains incomplete for conceptual models, standalone storyboard conventions, formal task analysis, content models, navigation systems and service blueprints. The catalog includes these as conditional original contracts supported by existing project needs and the separately identified public guidance; it does not claim that the bounded book audit established every field. Read the audit before attributing a method to a book.

## Industry guidance versus project conventions

This catalog and its field contracts are our original synthesis, not a standard mandated by a book or professional body. [GOV.UK research guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/analyse-a-research-session) separates observations, findings and actions; its [experience-map guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/creating-an-experience-map) supports maps grounded in researched experiences and iteration. Those are practical methods, not compulsory artifacts for every project.

[W3C involvement guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/planning/involving-users/) supports involving people with disabilities throughout design. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) provides testable web accessibility requirements; the selected scope and actual conformance evidence must be recorded separately. It does not prescribe our deliverable list or by itself certify native apps, physical reach, hardware or a complete service. Legal applicability and native/physical guidance need their own context-specific checks.

Public pages above were inspected on 2026-09-08. Book techniques require page/section provenance and age/context caveats from the private audit. No proprietary book templates or diagrams are copied into these contracts.
