# Creative direction and visual language

Version-Timestamp: 2026-09-09 10:37:21 AST

Original client-agnostic workflow. This extends research, reference-direction, foundations and identity; it does not create another approval authority or a universal style. Authored instructions require actual execution and visual assessment on each project.

## Start from the right brand state

| Starting point | Preserve | Explore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No identity | Accepted strategy, audience needs and factual constraints | Distinct identity territories; names and logos stay provisional until separately accepted |
| Logo or partial identity | Exact supplied marks and any approved values | Supporting type, palette roles, imagery and visual elements in the unfilled areas |
| Existing guidelines | Applicable approved rules, assets and tokens | Brand-aligned extensions for the selected campaign, channel or missing asset family |
| Existing public brand, no official kit | Verified observations with dates and uncertainty | Provisional palette/type approximations, observed layout patterns and original concepts; no redrawing of logos or wordmarks without explicit authority |
| Approved direction, narrow revision | Accepted direction and requested invariants | Only the authorized change; do not force three alternatives or reopen identity |

Research owns facts and source status. Reference-direction owns the comparison and recommendation. Identity owns approved visual language. Typography, color and motion own their technical specifications. Production owns native files and exports. Design-work owns actual approval scope, approver and evidence; the reference-decision Markdown owns comparison and links its structured constraint record. The JSON records constraint/readiness status, not a substitute for client approval. Use these existing records as the authority; the templates below are linked attachments, not competing masters. Do not add fields to the strict reference-decision JSON schema without a separate contract change.

## 1. Understand before choosing a look

Reuse relevant current research, then fill only decision-critical gaps: offering, audience/persona IDs, customer tasks, market/region, industry/vertical/niche, positioning, competitors and adjacent-category references, campaigns, channel, context of use and success criteria. Separate category conventions that help recognition from opportunities to differentiate. An industry's familiar palette is not proof that a brand must use it. Cultural associations are hypotheses requiring context, not universal color psychology.

Record what is supplied, observed, inferred, proposed or unknown, with source/date/market and rights status. Verify current trend claims through recent original examples; no date-free list is a current-trend report. Resolve conflicting approved guidance with its owner. Missing official assets can permit labeled exploration but cannot authorize logo reconstruction, copying or final delivery.

## 2. Explore ideas before decorating them

Write a small set of concept hypotheses tied to the communication problem: what should the audience understand, feel or do, and what visual idea could support that? Consider alternate metaphors, image media, composition, information density and tone. Reject unsupported product claims and stereotypes. Use [the vocabulary](VISUAL-DIRECTION-VOCABULARY.md) to explain ingredients rather than assigning a fashionable label.

Narrow to two or three meaningfully different directions when the choice is open; fewer are appropriate for a constrained extension. Keep the brief, core content, audience and target surface constant. A color swap is not a distinct concept. Explain differences in message emphasis, composition, typography, imagery or material treatment. Do not merge every favored reference into an incoherent hybrid.

## 3. Make each direction inspectable

Use [the direction-board template](templates/creative-direction-board.md). Present a named idea, rationale and intended audience response; annotated references; type specimen; palette roles; photography or illustration treatment; textures/shapes; representative layout; and motion reference when relevant. Each reference identifies exactly what to learn and what not to copy. Record source locator, checked date, license or reference-only status. Reference visibility is not production permission.

A mood board shows atmosphere and relationships. A style tile shows proposed visual ingredients together. An original application study shows whether those ingredients work for the actual content. Produce the applicable artifacts, not an unlabeled collage of attractive images. Use an accessible labeled companion with reading order and text descriptions; animation requires a static/reduced-motion alternative. Keep borrowed examples visibly separate from original studies. Private or restricted sources stay in the authorized workspace.

Use one representative application per direction and a second difficult context for the shortlisted direction: dense text, small display, narrow screen, alternate crop, localization or monochrome as applicable. Do not claim tested contrast, legibility or character consistency from a mood board alone. Inspect real renders at the intended size.

## 4. Compare, select and refine

Compare brand fit, audience/task fit, clarity/hierarchy, distinctiveness, cross-channel behavior, accessibility, feasibility, rights/cost and longevity. Define any weights before scoring; explain tradeoffs with artifacts and evidence. Scores support judgment and do not certify quality. Record rejected options, the recommended direction, unresolved conditions and the actual authorized selection. The AI recommendation is not client approval. New evidence can reopen only affected decisions.

For a requested hybrid, specify which compatible rules come from each direction, build a new candidate and recheck coherence. Do not silently treat the hybrid as an already approved option.

## 5. Define the selected visual language

Use [the visual-language template](templates/visual-language-system.md). Account for every family with create, reuse, extend, not-applicable or hold plus a reason; produce only selected families. Cover characters, photography, illustration, icons, patterns, textures/materials, shapes, typography, color, composition, UI treatments, data graphics and motion. Reference canonical tokens instead of duplicating values.

For characters include silhouette/proportion and identity locks, views, expressions, poses and relationships. For existing brands identify which elements are borrowed from approved rules, which are new compatible extensions and which need owner approval. A campaign layer is not automatically a permanent brand update. Record channel-specific adaptation without losing the core family resemblance.

## 6. Give the AI a bounded production packet

Use [the stage prompt](templates/creative-direction-stage.md) with existing stage-execution and design-work. Include actual loaded references/versions, direction and family IDs, canonical token paths, invariants, allowed variation, exact requested output, tool constraints, rights and acceptance evidence. Recheck available tools before generation. A saved prompt, seed or model name cannot guarantee consistent imagery across versions; approved visual references and output comparison remain necessary.

Explore broadly before selection; generate controlled variants after selection. When available, use approved reference images, character sheets, masks, editable components and existing assets to preserve identity. Record provider/model/version and generation settings when available, without credentials. Inspect every output for drift; reject or revise instead of explaining away a mismatch.

## 7. Quality and handoff

Apply existing [craft review](DESIGN-CRAFT.md), [production](draft-skills/design-production-interface/SKILL.md) and delivery checks. Evaluate hierarchy, balance, contrast, alignment, proximity, rhythm, scale, unity and appropriate variation against the communication goal. Check native/render behavior, crop, language, states and physical context only as applicable. Distinguish requirements from design heuristics and current trends.

Hand off approved direction ID/version, referenced visual-language families, actual assets/tokens, rights, allowed adaptations, rejected patterns, tests, unresolved holds and next owner. A new campaign, asset family, audience, product or channel triggers an impact check rather than automatic full redesign. Exact-copy revisions use the accepted system directly. No promise of exceptional quality replaces visual evidence.

### Edge-case routing

Derived logo/wordmark variants, reversed or monochrome marks and altered lockups require explicit owner authorization or an already approved supplied variant. Otherwise mark them held; a difficult-context test can reveal the need without manufacturing a new mark. New proposed lockups are proposals, not accepted extensions. Naming and logo creation route to identity and applicable naming/rights review, not automatic acceptance in this exploration stage.

A narrow approved copy change routes to content as needed and production, with a design-work delta note; no new board or visual-language record is required. Recheck changed wrapping, hierarchy, contrast at changed sizes, localization and affected outputs. Preserve other approval scope.

For generative production, record applicable provider/model commercial-use terms and output rights/likeness/trademark limitations in the existing rights record. Unknown required rights hold dependent reuse or delivery. Availability of model metadata does not waive this requirement.
