# Vocabulary for explaining visual directions

Version-Timestamp: 2026-09-09 10:37:21 AST

A non-exhaustive working vocabulary, not a trend ranking or a taxonomy of mutually exclusive styles. Names vary between practitioners. Always define what a label means in the actual candidate, with visible examples and source context. Research current popularity separately.

| Layer | Meaning | Example terms and distinctions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Communication intent | What the work should accomplish | Reassurance, discovery, precision, play, urgency; explain the audience/task basis |
| Aesthetic family | Overall formal character | Minimalist, editorial, geometric, organic, expressive, utilitarian, retro, grunge; none belongs exclusively to an industry |
| Image medium | How the image is represented | Photography, illustration, collage, diagram, 3D rendering; photorealism describes an appearance and can occur in different media |
| Technique | Construction or treatment | Gradients, grain, halftone, cut paper, duotone, line drawing, compositing, depth of field, stop motion |
| Interface treatment | How controls and surfaces communicate | Flat, skeuomorphic, translucent, tactile; visual metaphor must retain understandable interaction and accessible states |
| Composition and type | How information is organized | Modular grid, asymmetry, dense editorial hierarchy, restrained type, expressive display lettering; specify actual hierarchy and measure |
| Motion behavior | How change communicates | Reveal, transition, parallax, loop, character acting; specify trigger, purpose and static/reduced-motion fallback |
| Trend claim | A dated observation about adoption | Requires recent dated examples, scope, source and uncertainty; popularity alone does not establish brand fit |
| Art direction | The intentional combination | A project-specific set of choices, constraints and relationships, not a single adjective |

Editorial does not necessarily mean serif, luxury or sparse. Minimalism does not mean missing information. Grunge is not a license for illegibility. Gradients are a technique, not an entire identity. Skeuomorphism concerns resemblance to familiar physical objects or behaviors; decorative realism does not automatically improve usability. These distinctions prevent vague labels from becoming commands.

For each selected term record: plain-English definition; observed visual cues; exact candidate example; source/date if externally derived; role in the brand; contrast with another option; constraints; and what the term does not imply. For a trend additionally record market/channel, evidence of adoption, likely maintenance implications and a timeless alternative. Avoid unsupported claims that a treatment guarantees trust, conversion or emotional response.

Combine only ingredients that support the same idea. For example, “precise editorial” can be a proposed combination of clear information hierarchy, restrained palette, close product photography and quiet motion. Those choices need project evidence; they are not defaults, a predefined font pair or a completed direction.
