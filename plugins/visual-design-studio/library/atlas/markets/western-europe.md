# Western Europe (France, Iberia, Italy, Benelux)

## Language and script

- Latin script. Languages: French (France, Wallonia/Brussels, Luxembourg), Spanish (Spain), Catalan, Basque and Galician (co-official regionally), Portuguese (Portugal), Italian, Dutch/Flemish (Netherlands, Flanders), German (Belgium, Luxembourg), Luxembourgish. Belgium, Luxembourg and Switzerland-adjacent products often need two or three languages, each with its own entry point.
- Locale codes: `fr-FR`, `fr-BE`, `es-ES`, `ca-ES`, `pt-PT`, `it-IT`, `nl-NL`, `nl-BE`, `lb-LU`. European Portuguese differs from Brazilian (telemóvel, autocarro, "tu/você" usage); European Spanish differs from LATAM (ordenador, móvil, vosotros); Belgian French uses septante/nonante and "tu/vous" registers differently from France.
- Register: formal address is default in finance, public sector and B2B (vous, usted, Lei/Sie, u); tu/tú/jij is normal for youth brands and app UI. In the Netherlands, "je/jij" is now standard in consumer apps; "u" for banks and older audiences. Test with local copywriters.
- Characters to cover: French à â æ ç é è ê ë î ï ô œ ù û ü ÿ and Œ Æ; Spanish ¿ ¡ ñ; Catalan ŀ (l·l) and ç; Portuguese ã õ ç; Italian à è é ì ò ù; Dutch ĳ/IJ digraph (capital IJ is one letter in titles: IJsselmeer, not Ijsselmeer). Keep accents on capitals in French and Spanish, recommended by the Académie française and RAE; French typography also uses narrow no-break spaces before : ; ! ? and inside « guillemets ».
- Expansion from English: French +15-25%, Spanish +20-30%, Italian +15-25%, Portuguese +20-30%, Dutch +20-35%. German-influenced Dutch compounds need hyphenation.
- Local language laws: France's Loi Toubon requires French in advertising, consumer information and product labelling. Spanish consumer law requires Spanish, and regional rules (for example in Catalonia) add co-official language duties; verify per region.

## Typography and layout

- Use families with full Latin Extended and small caps/ligatures: Inter, Source Sans 3, IBM Plex Sans, Work Sans, Lato; serifs suited to French/Italian editorial traditions: Cormorant Garamond, EB Garamond, Playfair Display, Libre Caslon, Newsreader. Italian and French luxury and fashion typically use high-contrast Didone or Garamond-style type.
- Body 16px, line-height 1.5-1.6; French needs generous line-height for accents on capitals (display 1.15+). Measure 55-75 characters.
- Set `lang` per element; enable `hyphens: auto` (French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Portuguese dictionaries) and `text-wrap: balance` for headlines.
- Quote marks: French « text », Spanish « » or “ ”, Italian « » or “ ”, Dutch “ ” or „ ”; do not substitute straight quotes.
- Layout: the Netherlands and Belgium favor clean, direct, grid-based pages; France favors editorial storytelling and imagery; Spain and Italy accept more image-forward, warm layouts with strong display type. All expect clear price display with VAT and obvious legal links (mentions légales).
- Print: A4 everywhere; Italian addresses and invoices follow specific formats (fattura elettronica).

## Color and symbolism

- Blue-white-red (France, Netherlands flags), red-yellow (Spain), green-white-red (Italy), green-red (Portugal) are strong identity palettes; football and regional flags (Catalonia, Basque Country) carry political meaning. Do not use regional flags casually.
- White is associated with weddings and purity, black with mourning; chrysanthemums are a funeral and All Saints flower in France, Italy and Spain, so avoid them in gifting campaigns. Tuesday the 13th (martes 13) in Spain and Friday the 17th in Italy are considered unlucky, which matters for campaign dates and numbered offers.
- French luxury leans black, white, gold, deep blue; Italian design emphasizes warm earth tones and bold color; Spanish retail mixes saturated brights; Dutch design favors bold flat color and orange (the royal house of Orange-Nassau) on national days (King's Day, April 27).
- Green and blue are the default for sustainability and health claims; ensure claims comply with EU green-claims rules (see below).
- Red-white-green on Italian food packaging reads as "Italian"; use with sincerity, not as a stereotype for non-Italian products.

## Imagery and representation

- Each country is diverse; avoid clichés: berets and baguettes, flamenco and bulls, gondolas and mafia tropes, windmills and tulips and clogs. Show contemporary life: markets, cafés, cycling cities (Netherlands), apartment living, public transport, beach, work.
- Use real regional specifics (Marseille is not Paris; Andalusia is not Barcelona; Flanders is not Wallonia). Language and identity matter in Belgium, Catalonia, the Basque Country and Corsica; do not collapse them.
- Migration history shapes France (Maghrebi, West African, Portuguese communities), the Netherlands (Surinamese, Turkish, Moroccan, Indonesian), Spain (Latin American) and Italy (Eastern European, North African): include diverse faces in everyday roles.
- Body image and nudity norms are more relaxed than in the US in advertising, but the French ARPP, Spanish Autocontrol and Dutch Reclame Code Commissie police stereotypes, children and misleading claims.
- Food and wine culture: portray alcohol responsibly; some countries (France's Loi Evin) strongly restrict alcohol advertising content.
- Fashion and design audiences respond to authenticity, craftsmanship and provenance (Made in France/Italy) over generic stock.

- Week starts Monday; months and weekdays lowercase in French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch.
- Names: Spanish and Portuguese use two surnames; Dutch surnames have particles (van der, de) that are lowercase and affect sorting; French allows hyphenated given names (Jean-Pierre). Provide a flexible "Civilité" or gender-neutral titles option.
- VAT: 20% (France), 21% (Spain, Netherlands), 22% (Italy), 23% (Portugal), 21% (Belgium); consumer prices include VAT (verify current rates).
- Public holidays vary: Easter Monday, Assumption (15 Aug), Bastille Day (14 Jul), La Liberazione (25 Apr), Koningsdag (27 Apr). France's summer "congés" and Italy's Ferragosto close many businesses in August.

## Platforms, devices and payments

- Smartphone penetration is high; iOS holds roughly 25-35% of mobile traffic in Spain, Italy and Portugal and 40-55% in France, Belgium and the Netherlands (StatCounter ranges; verify). Fiber and 5G are strong; rural areas lag.
- Payments by country: **Netherlands:** iDEAL dominates online and is being phased into Wero from 2026, with completion planned by end of 2027 (verify with EPI); Tikkie for P2P; Klarna/Afterpay. **Belgium:** Bancontact (with Payconiq), cards. **France:** Carte Bancaire (CB, co-badged with Visa/Mastercard), PayPal, Apple Pay, Paylib (being folded into Wero; verify), installment payments (Alma, Oney). **Spain:** Bizum (mobile P2P and e-commerce), cards, Redsys, PayPal, Apple Pay. **Portugal:** Multibanco references (Entidade + Referência + Valor), MB WAY. **Italy:** PostePay, Satispay, PayPal, Scalapay, Bancomat Pay, cash on delivery.
- SEPA credit transfer and SEPA Direct Debit are universal; instant SEPA is spreading. Wero (European Payments Initiative) is rolling out across Germany, France and Belgium (verify current coverage).
- Delivery and pickup: Mondial Relay/Relais Colis (France), Correos, PostNL, bpost, CTT; parcel lockers and relay points are common.
- Messaging: WhatsApp dominant in Spain, Italy, Portugal and the Netherlands; Messenger and SMS in France; Telegram in some groups.
- Marketplaces: Amazon, Zalando, Vinted, Leboncoin, Bol.com, Cdiscount, Wallapop, Subito, El Corte Inglés.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **GDPR and ePrivacy:** consent for non-essential cookies and trackers must be freely given, specific, informed and as easy to refuse as to accept. The CNIL (France) requires a refuse option on the first layer and has fined Google and Meta for hard-to-refuse choices. Spain's AEPD and Italy's Garante apply similar rules and have issued detailed cookie guidelines. Avoid pre-ticked boxes and consent walls.
- **European Accessibility Act:** applies from 28 June 2025 to consumer e-commerce, banking, e-books, ticketing and audiovisual services; websites and apps should meet EN 301 549 (WCAG 2.1 AA at minimum; WCAG 2.2 AA is safer) and publish an accessibility statement. Microenterprises that provide services are exempt, and member states differ in enforcement (France's RGAA, Spain's UNE-EN 301549, Italy's AgID rules).
- **Consumer law:** 14-day withdrawal right, the "button solution" (an order button labeled to show payment obligation), clear total price, and a new EU online withdrawal function (Directive 2023/2673, applying from 19 June 2026) requiring an easy withdrawal button for online contracts with a withdrawal right. The Digital Services Act bans dark patterns on online platforms (Art. 25); the Omnibus Directive requires showing the lowest price of the prior 30 days for price reductions. A Digital Fairness Act has been proposed; verify status.
- **Language and labels:** France's Toubon law requires French for consumer information; Spanish consumer law requires Spanish, with regional co-official language duties (verify); Belgium's linguistic regions set the language of customer communications.
- **AI and transparency:** EU AI Act transparency duties (disclosing AI chatbots and labeling synthetic content) begin applying in 2026; confirm current dates.
- **Green claims:** the EU is restricting generic environmental claims ("eco-friendly", "climate neutral") under the 2024 Directive on empowering consumers for the green transition (applying from September 2026; verify); avoid unsubstantiated sustainability messaging.
- Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Localization pitfalls

- Treating the region as a single "EU" locale; payments, VAT, language and legal pages differ by country.
- Using France-French for Belgium, Spain-Spanish for LATAM, or Brazilian Portuguese for Portugal (and the reverse).
- Missing legal pages: mentions légales (France), Impressum-style company details (Belgium and Luxembourg), Aviso Legal (Spain), Privacy and Cookie policy per language.
- Prices shown net of VAT to consumers, or without the Omnibus reference price on discounts.
- Cookie banners with a prominent "Accept" and buried "Reject"; asking again every visit.
- Forgetting two-surname fields, Dutch particles, French accented capitals, and long compound words in Dutch.
- Ignoring August closures and delivery cutoffs in campaign planning.
- Ignoring regional languages: Catalan in Catalonia, Basque in the Basque Country, Flemish versus French in Belgium.
