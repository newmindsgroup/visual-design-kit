# Australia and New Zealand

## Language and script

- English (`en-AU`, `en-NZ`), Latin script, British-based spelling with local forms: colour, organise (-ise preferred by the Australian Government Style Manual), centre; Australian "program" versus NZ "programme". Use `lang="en-AU"` or `en-NZ`; do not ship US copy unchanged (cell phone → mobile, trash → rubbish, sidewalk → footpath, "Zip code" → postcode).
- Vocabulary: "arvo", "servo", "thongs" (Australian flip-flops), "jandals" (NZ), "chips" (fries or crisps), "ute", "bach" (NZ holiday home) and "crib" (NZ, South Island). Use slang sparingly and only when the brand voice is genuinely casual.
- **Te reo Māori** is an official language of New Zealand; **NZ Sign Language** is official; **Auslan** is Australia's sign language. Macrons (ā ē ī ō ū and capitals Ā Ē Ī Ō Ū) are part of correct spelling: Māori, Aotearoa, whānau, kia ora. Never strip macrons, ensure fonts have them, and keep them in search, URLs and databases (use Unicode normalization). Some iwi use double vowels (aa) instead; follow the Nation's preference. Use te reo greetings only when correct and when the brand will keep using it respectfully (avoid one-off tokenism).
- Australia has hundreds of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages; names for Country, people and language groups must be spelled as the community specifies. Capitalize "Country", "First Nations", "Aboriginal" and "Torres Strait Islander".
- Tone: friendly, direct, understated and a little irreverent; "tall poppy" culture dislikes boasting, hard sells and hype. Honest, plain language works (Australian Government Style Manual, NZ Government writing guidance). Aim for grade 7-9 reading level; be explicit about prices and conditions.
- Multilingual communities: Mandarin, Cantonese, Arabic, Vietnamese, Punjabi, Hindi, Greek, Italian, Samoan, Tongan; provide translated key information (health, government, finance) where audiences warrant it.

## Typography and layout

- Fonts: Inter, Source Sans 3, Public Sans, Noto Sans, DM Sans, Work Sans; serif for editorial: Source Serif 4, Newsreader. The Australian Government GOLD Design System and NZ Government Web Toolkit show accessible conventions.
- Body 16-18px, line-height 1.5-1.6, measure 60-75 characters. Left-aligned, sentence case headings; avoid all caps for body and legal copy.
- Macrons: choose fonts with full macron support at all weights; test uppercase Ā and combining macron fallbacks, especially in display and variable fonts.
- Layout: clean, spacious, photo-led pages with strong calls to action; retail pages can be dense with sale tags. Mobile-first, with outdoor-lifestyle imagery and local trust cues (ABN/NZBN, reviews).
- Print: A4 (ISO) in both countries; business cards 90 x 55 mm; envelopes DL. Real-estate and outdoor signage are bold and legible at speed.
- Colors in UI follow universal accessibility rules; sun glare on devices makes high contrast valuable.

## Color and symbolism

- Australia: green and gold are national sporting colors; the Aboriginal flag (black, red, yellow circle) and Torres Strait Islander flag (green, blue, black, white) are official flags of Australia; reproduction rules apply (the copyright in the Aboriginal flag was transferred to the Commonwealth in 2022 and the flag is free to use in most contexts; verify current terms). Do not alter them or use them as decoration without understanding.
- New Zealand: black (All Blacks), silver fern, and pounamu (greenstone) green; the koru (spiral fern frond), kōwhaiwhai and tukutuku patterns are taonga with cultural meaning. Do not use Māori design, tā moko or carving patterns decoratively without consultation with the relevant iwi or a Māori designer.
- Landscape palettes: ochre, red earth, eucalyptus green, ocean blue, reef teal, sand; NZ: deep green, fog grey, volcanic black. Southern Hemisphere seasons are reversed: Christmas is summer, school starts in late January/February, bushfire season (red alert palettes) is summer.
- Red is also used for bushfire and danger; avoid alarmist red in non-urgent contexts during disaster seasons.
- Green and gold sustainability palettes are common; ensure claims are substantiated (ACCC greenwashing guidance).

## Imagery and representation

- About three in ten Australians and New Zealanders were born overseas (verify latest censuses); Sydney, Melbourne and Auckland are highly multicultural, with large Chinese, Indian, Pacific, Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian communities. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples are about 3.8% of Australia's population, and Māori about 17% of Aotearoa New Zealand's (verify). Show diverse faces in everyday settings, not only "multicultural" campaigns.
- Indigenous imagery: engage First Nations or Māori creatives and communities; follow the ICIP (Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property) protocols in Australia and tikanga in New Zealand; pay and credit artists. Include an Acknowledgement of Country on Australian sites and documents for organizations that adopt one (a sincere, specific statement tied to action). Content that may show deceased Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people should carry a warning.
- Do not treat Australia and New Zealand as one country; Kiwis dislike being treated as an Australian state. Use distinct imagery, spellings and pricing.
- Lifestyle: beach, surf, bush, BBQ, sport (cricket, AFL, NRL, rugby, netball), cafe culture, utes, pets. Avoid kangaroo/koala/crocodile and hobbit/sheep clichés unless the brand is tourism.
- Sun-safe behavior is normal (hats, shade, sunscreen); show it. Bushfire, flood and drought are lived realities; imagery of disasters needs sensitivity and local review.
- ANZAC Day (April 25) is solemn; the word "Anzac" is legally protected in Australia and New Zealand, so avoid commercial use. "Australia Day" (January 26) is contested for many First Nations people (Invasion Day / Survival Day); avoid assumptions in campaign timing and copy. Waitangi Day (February 6) and Matariki (a public holiday in NZ) should be handled with consultation.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Date | DD/MM/YYYY; long form without comma | 20/10/2026 or 20 October 2026 |
| Time | 12-hour with am/pm lowercase; 24-hour in transport; AEST/AEDT, ACST, AWST; NZST/NZDT | 3:30pm |
| Number | Comma thousands, period decimal | 1,234,567.89 |
| Currency | AUD or NZD with $; prices include GST (Australia 10%, NZ 15%) | $1,234.56 |
| Address | Unit/number street, suburb, state (NSW, VIC, QLD, WA, SA, TAS, ACT, NT), 4-digit postcode; NZ: suburb, city, 4-digit postcode; rural delivery "RD 2" | 12/345 George St, Sydney NSW 2000 |
| Phone | Australia +61 4XX XXX XXX mobile, (02) 1234 5678 landline; NZ +64 2X XXX XXXX mobile | 0412 345 678 |
| Name | Given + family name; Māori and Pacific names can have macrons, multiple words and long forms | Allow macrons and spaces |

- Financial year: Australia July-June; NZ April-March. Public holidays differ by state/territory (Australia) and by region (NZ anniversary days).
- Postcode and address lookups (Australia Post PAF, NZ Post / AddressFinder) reduce errors; always allow manual entry for rural or new-build addresses.
- Distances in km, weights in kg, temperature in °C, though property sizes use square metres and hectares.

## Platforms, devices and payments

- iOS leads: roughly 55-60% of smartphones in Australia and about half in New Zealand (StatCounter; verify). High broadband and 5G in cities; regional and remote areas depend on satellite, fixed wireless and patchy mobile coverage, so keep core flows light and offline-tolerant.
- Contactless tap-and-pay is near universal; Apple Pay and Google Pay are widely accepted; eftpos is the domestic debit scheme; Visa/Mastercard/Amex. PayID and Osko (instant bank transfers via the NPP) and BPAY (bill payment) are common in Australia; **BNPL** (Afterpay, Zip, Klarna, humm) is mainstream. NZ uses account-to-account payments, eftpos, Apple Pay/Google Pay, Afterpay and Zip. Card surcharges exist; Australian regulators are reviewing and restricting them (verify). Show total price including surcharges and delivery up front.
- Marketplaces and retail: Amazon AU, eBay, Kmart, Woolworths, Coles, JB Hi-Fi, Bunnings, Officeworks; NZ: Trade Me, The Warehouse, Countdown/Woolworths NZ; property: realestate.com.au, Domain, Trade Me Property. Delivery: Australia Post, Sendle, StarTrack, NZ Post, CourierPost.
- Social: Facebook (Marketplace, groups), Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn; WhatsApp is less central than SMS/iMessage in general consumer use.
- Government digital services (myGov, MyGovID/myID, RealMe, Service NSW) shape expectations for simple, accessible, single-sign-on experiences.
- Identity verification: 100-point ID rules for finance; digital ID adoption is growing under Australia's Digital ID Act 2024.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Australia privacy:** Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) apply to most organizations with turnover above A$3m (with exceptions) and to health providers. The Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 added a statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy (from June 2025), new transparency for automated decisions (from December 2026) and a Children's Online Privacy Code to be registered by December 2026; further reform ("tranche 2") is pending (verify). Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. Spam Act 2003 (consent, identification, unsubscribe) and the Do Not Call Register.
- **Australia online safety:** under the Social Media Minimum Age law, age-restricted social platforms must take reasonable steps to prevent under-16s from holding accounts (in force December 10, 2025); the eSafety Commissioner enforces. Design age assurance carefully and minimize data collection.
- **Consumer law:** the Australian Consumer Law (misleading conduct, consumer guarantees, unfair contract terms with penalties since 2023, fake reviews, drip pricing and subscription traps under scrutiny); ACCC enforcement. NZ: Fair Trading Act 1986, Consumer Guarantees Act 1993, Commerce Commission, Unsolicited Electronic Messages Act 2007.
- **New Zealand privacy:** Privacy Act 2020 (13 IPPs, mandatory breach notification) with new IPP 3A on indirect collection in force from May 1, 2026; Māori data sovereignty principles (Te Mana Raraunga) and Te Tiriti obligations apply to Crown agencies and are best practice for others.
- **Accessibility:** Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Australia) makes inaccessible digital services potentially discriminatory; the government follows WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA (verify); NZ Web Accessibility Standard (WCAG 2.1 AA for government; verify) and the Human Rights Act 1993. Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
- **Sector rules:** ASIC rules for financial promotions and the Consumer Data Right (open banking); TGA rules for therapeutic claims; gambling, alcohol (ABAC code) and tobacco advertising restrictions; Ad Standards (AANA Code of Ethics) and NZ ASA codes.
- **Indigenous rights:** ICIP protocols and, in NZ, the Trade Marks Act's Māori Advisory Committee for marks that may offend Māori.
- Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Localization pitfalls

- US spelling, MM/DD dates, "ZIP code", "state" assumptions in NZ forms (New Zealand has no states), prices without GST.
- Treating Australia and New Zealand as one market or one voice; shipping and tax logic that groups them.
- Stripping macrons or misspelling te reo Māori; using Māori or Aboriginal art as decoration without permission; one-off "Acknowledgement of Country" without follow-through.
- Hard-sell American hype and long "limited time" countdowns that clash with understated tone and consumer-law scrutiny.
- Forgetting remote and regional connectivity; heavy pages for rural audiences.
- Seasonal mismatches (snow Christmas imagery in summer), and holiday calendars that differ by state and region.
- Underestimating regulation: card surcharges, BNPL disclosures, drip pricing, age assurance for under-16s.
