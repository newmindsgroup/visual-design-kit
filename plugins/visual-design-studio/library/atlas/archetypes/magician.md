# Magician

## Essence

- **Core desire:** knowledge of the fundamental laws of how the world or people work, to make dreams come true.
- **Fear:** unintended negative consequences.
- **Promise:** "We turn what you imagine into what you experience." Transformation in a single step.
- **Mark and Pearson group:** risk and mastery (with Hero and Outlaw). Strategy: develop a vision and live by it; make it happen.
- **Shadow:** manipulation, spectacle without substance, "magic" that hides how the product works.

Archetypes are a positioning shorthand (Mark and Pearson, 2001; after Jung), not science. Magician often pairs with Creator (tools that transform) or Sage (technology explained). Be explicit about the trick: premium magic shows the moment of reveal and never hides cost or consequence.

## Voice and tone

- Confident, evocative, sensory; verbs of becoming ("turn", "reveal", "become"). Short lines with room to breathe.
- Promise the outcome and show the mechanism in the next sentence; the audience should never feel tricked.
- Avoid exclamation points and the word "magic" itself; let the visual reveal carry it.
- Headline register: "Draft to done in one pass." / "See your home before it exists."

## Fits and tensions

- **Fits:** AI and creative tools, entertainment and streaming, fragrance and cosmetics, premium tech hardware, immersive events, games, education that transforms skills, spirits with ritual.
- **Common blends:** Magician + Creator (generative tools); Magician + Sage (science and technology explainers); Magician + Lover (fragrance, beauty); Magician + Hero (transformation through effort). Pick a primary archetype for the system and a secondary for flavor, so the brand does not become a pile of effects.
- **Tensions:** Magician vs transparency: regulated or trust-first categories (health, finance, legal) cannot promise transformation without proof. Dark UI with glow effects strains contrast and low-vision users; pair with a light, solid reading surface.
- **Price tier:** natural at premium and luxury; at value tier, drop expensive-looking effects and keep one clear reveal.

## Clichés to avoid

- Purple-to-pink or indigo-to-blue gradient blobs behind every AI product; use one named light source and a gold or teal counterpoint instead.
- Sparkle emoji, wands, top hats, crystal balls as literal icons.
- Glassmorphism on every card; limit it to one layer that the reveal actually needs.
- Particle backgrounds that cost 60% of GPU and carry no meaning.

- Disney, Apple, Dyson, Tesla: commonly cited Magician brands
- Maurits Escher and Georges Méliès for illusion and in-camera transformation
- Refik Anadol and teamLab for immersive, light-driven installations
- Bill Viola for slow, luminous video language
