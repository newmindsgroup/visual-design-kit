# Outlaw

## Essence

- **Core desire:** liberation, revolution, to break what is not working.
- **Fear:** being powerless or ineffectual.
- **Promise:** "Break the rules that hold you back." Membership in a defiant group.
- **Mark and Pearson group:** risk and mastery (with Hero and Magician). Strategy: disrupt, destroy, shock.
- **Shadow:** nihilism, cruelty, rebellion as a purchased costume.

Archetypes are positioning shorthand (Mark and Pearson, 2001; after Jung), not science. Outlaw is the archetype most often faked: defiance has to be aimed at something real (a category habit, an incumbent, a bad norm) or it reads as a pose.

## Voice and tone

- Blunt, short, a little profane or mocking where the audience expects it; address a shared enemy ("the beige default").
- Declarative headlines, no hedging; humor is dry and dark rather than cute.
- Use the rebellion for the product's truth, not for noise: say what you refuse to do.
- Keep transactional UI (checkout, errors, legal) clear and straightforward; the attitude lives in marketing, packaging and microcopy.

## Fits and tensions

- **Fits:** motorcycles and garage culture, music and festivals, streetwear and skate, craft beer, energy drinks and water brands that mock the category, challenger fintech and media, gaming, zines and independent publishing.
- **Common blends:** Outlaw + Jester (irreverent drinks and snacks); Outlaw + Hero (extreme sports); Outlaw + Creator (punk design studios, indie labels); Outlaw + Everyperson (working-class challenger brands). Choose the primary archetype for the visual system; let the secondary soften or sharpen the tone in one place.
- **Tensions:** Outlaw vs trust. Banks, insurers, clinics and government need legibility and predictability; borrow only the edge (one raw type choice, one color) and keep the grid calm. Outlaw vs scale: when a rebel brand goes mass-market, the shock must be re-earned or it becomes costume.
- **Accessibility tension:** distressed type, tiny grunge text and blinking effects fail contrast and photosensitivity rules; the attitude cannot excuse them.

## Clichés to avoid

- Skull, barbed wire, flames, leather jacket stock photos: use the real subculture's artifacts and people.
- Grunge texture overlays on a clean SaaS template: either commit to raw construction or drop the texture.
- Swearing as a substitute for a point of view.
- Black-and-red everything; try bone and acid, or concrete and orange, to avoid the default.

## Reference anchors

- Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001); Carl G. Jung's archetype theory
- Harley-Davidson, Diesel, Liquid Death, Vice: commonly cited Outlaw or Outlaw-leaning brands
- Jamie Reid's Sex Pistols graphics and *Ray Gun* magazine (David Carson) as punk and deconstructionist print references
- Barbara Kruger and Shepard Fairey for confrontational text-and-image propaganda language
- Neville Brody's *The Face* typography for experimental editorial rebellion
