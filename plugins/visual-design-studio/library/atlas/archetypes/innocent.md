# Innocent

## Essence

- **Core desire:** safety and happiness; to do things right and live in a simple, good world.
- **Fear:** doing something wrong and being punished or exposed as a fraud.
- **Promise:** "This is honest, simple and good for you." Reassurance without complication.
- **Mark and Pearson group:** independence and fulfillment (with Explorer and Sage). Strategy: keep it simple, do the right thing.
- **Shadow:** naivety, blandness, or "too good to be true" claims that read as greenwashing.

Archetypes are a positioning shorthand from brand strategy practice, popularized by Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson in *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001) and rooted in Carl Jung's ideas about recurring figures in the collective unconscious. They are not science and no study shows a brand "is" one. Use them to make choices consistent, then test with real audiences.

## Voice and tone

- Short sentences, everyday words, first person plural ("we make it simple"). Reading grade 5-7.
- Humor is gentle and self-aware, never sarcastic. Say what is in the product and what is not.
- Claims are specific and checkable ("three ingredients", "no added sugar") rather than abstract ("pure goodness").
- Headline register: "Just fruit. Nothing sneaky." Error and empty states apologize lightly and offer a next step.

## Fits and tensions

- **Fits:** food and drink, baby and family care, personal care, wellness, children's education, charities with a hopeful message, simple utilities. Strong with `style.quiet-minimal`, `style.scandinavian-nordic` and `style.handcrafted-organic`.
- **Common blends:** Innocent + Caregiver (baby care, health); Innocent + Jester (playful snacks, drinks); Innocent + Sage (clean-label science, clinical calm). Make one primary: the primary sets the type and palette, the secondary shows up in copy tone or one signature device.
- **Tensions:** Innocent against a high price tier needs restraint and material proof, or it reads as cheap; Innocent against `industry.fintech-banking` or legal work reads as unserious unless the product is simplicity itself. Against Outlaw or Ruler it is a conflict, not a blend.
- **Audience note:** for `audience.children-under-13`, add large targets and plain reading level, and follow the audience entry; the archetype alone does not make a design child-safe.

## Clichés to avoid

- White background, light-blue gradient, stock baby or smiling-family photography, a dove or leaf icon: swap for specific ingredients, real packaging, real people at real scale.
- Pastel rainbow plus rounded sans used for every wellness brand: choose one hue family and commit to it.
- "Pure", "natural", "clean" claims with no proof; replace with a number, an ingredient list or a certification.
- Over-cute mascots on adult products.
