# Everyperson

## Essence

- **Core desire:** connection, belonging, to fit in.
- **Fear:** standing out, being left out, putting on airs.
- **Promise:** "You are one of us; we treat you fairly." Practical products at fair prices and a welcoming tone.
- **Mark and Pearson group:** belonging and enjoyment (with Jester and Lover). Strategy: develop ordinary virtues, the common touch.
- **Shadow:** blandness, conformity, price-led commodity positioning with no point of view.

Archetypes are a positioning shorthand (Mark and Pearson, 2001; after Jung), not science. Everyperson is the default for broad-reach brands, which makes distinctiveness hard: borrow one memorable, owned asset (a color, a shape, a voice habit) so the friendliness is not generic.

## Voice and tone

- Plain talk, contractions, everyday examples; reading grade 6-8. Warm and direct, never folksy to the point of condescension.
- Show prices, fees and policies up front; say what the product does not do.
- Acknowledge real life ("busy week", "tight budget") without pity or jokes at their expense.
- Headline register: "Good things, fair prices." Helpful microcopy: "Need a hand? Call us. A real person answers."

## Fits and tensions

- **Fits:** retail and home improvement, furniture and household goods, grocery, banks and credit unions, insurance, telecom, utilities, public services, marketplaces, community and local business.
- **Common blends:** Everyperson + Caregiver (health plans, public services); Everyperson + Jester (mass-market food and drink); Everyperson + Hero (workwear and tools); Everyperson + Creator (DIY, craft retail). Strong brands blend a primary and a secondary archetype: the primary sets type and palette, the secondary adds one distinctive device.
- **Tensions:** Everyperson vs premium and luxury pricing, where ordinariness reads as cheap; Everyperson vs a strong challenger voice. Aim for "familiar and trustworthy" and let craft (typography, photography) lift it.
- **Accessibility:** the archetype aligns with plain-language, large-text and low-bandwidth audiences; see `audience.low-literacy-plain-language` and `audience.low-bandwidth-entry-devices`.

## Clichés to avoid

- Stock "diverse team high-fiving" and families laughing at salad: use real photography or quiet product-in-use shots.
- Gray-blue corporate "Helvetica and a swoosh" systems with no owned asset.
- Fake grassroots (hand-drawn fonts on a large conglomerate) that customers see through.
- Folksy slang that misreads regional audiences.

## Reference anchors

- Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001); Carl G. Jung's archetype theory
- IKEA, Target, Costco, Levi's, The Home Depot, Volkswagen's "Think Small" print campaign (DDB, 1959): commonly cited Everyperson or Everyperson-leaning brands
- GOV.UK Design System and Service Manual for plain, inclusive public-service design
- Dorothea Lange and the Farm Security Administration photographers for dignified portraits of ordinary life
- Paul Rand, *Thoughts on Design* (1947), for simple, friendly visual communication
