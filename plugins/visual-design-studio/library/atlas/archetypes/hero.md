# Hero

## Essence

- **Core desire:** to prove worth through courageous, difficult action and make the world better by mastery.
- **Fear:** weakness, vulnerability, being a coward.
- **Promise:** "You can do hard things, and we will help you do them better." Strength and competence in the user's own story.
- **Mark and Pearson group:** risk and mastery (with Outlaw and Magician). Strategy: be as strong and competent as possible.
- **Shadow:** arrogance, hype without proof, needing an enemy, exclusion of people who are not already strong.

Archetypes are a positioning shorthand (Mark and Pearson, 2001; after Jung), not science. Hero brands often make the customer the hero and the brand the mentor or equipment; stories that center the brand's own greatness age badly.

## Voice and tone

- Short, imperative, second person ("Train harder. Recover smarter."). Active verbs and numbers.
- Respect effort: acknowledge doubt and difficulty before the triumph, and credit coaches and teams.
- Avoid shaming ("no excuses"); motivate with progress and specifics.
- Headline register: three to five words, period at the end.

## Fits and tensions

- **Fits:** sport and fitness, performance apparel, automotive and equipment, military and public service recruitment, logistics and industrial tools, gaming, challenger fintech, safety and rescue services.
- **Common blends:** Hero + Explorer (expedition gear); Hero + Caregiver (rescue, medical heroism, recruitment); Hero + Everyperson (community sport); Hero + Outlaw (extreme sports). Decide which archetype is primary for the visual system; the secondary should appear in tone of voice or one motif.
- **Tensions:** Hero vs inclusion: imagery of elite bodies alienates beginners; show a range of abilities, ages and body types doing the effort. Hero vs calm categories (wellness, mental health): replace the battle metaphor with progress and recovery.
- **Accessibility tension:** all-caps heavy condensed headlines are hard to read at length; limit to short lines and keep body text regular weight.

## Clichés to avoid

- Silhouette of a figure on a mountaintop, arms raised; "be your best self"; lightning bolts; flames: show a particular person, place and effort.
- Slow-motion black-and-white montage with a voiceover about greatness.
- Red-black-and-gold on every sports startup; choose a color that is honest to the sport or the city.
- Stock photos of men in suits "winning" at the office.

## Reference anchors

- Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001); Carol S. Pearson, *The Hero Within* (1986); Joseph Campbell, *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949)
- Nike, Adidas, Under Armour, FedEx, Duracell: commonly cited Hero brands
- Olympic Games identities and pictogram systems (Otl Aicher, Munich 1972)
- Bauhaus and Russian Constructivist posters for diagonal, forceful composition
- Sports broadcasters' graphic packages (data callouts and replay overlays) for numeric hierarchy
