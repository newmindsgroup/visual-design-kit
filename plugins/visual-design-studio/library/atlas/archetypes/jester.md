# Jester

## Essence

- **Core desire:** to live in the moment with full enjoyment; to lighten up the world.
- **Fear:** being bored or boring others.
- **Promise:** "Life is better when we do not take it so seriously." Permission to play.
- **Mark and Pearson group:** belonging and enjoyment (with Everyperson and Lover). Strategy: play, make jokes, be fun.
- **Shadow:** wasting time, cruelty disguised as humor, jokes that undermine real customer problems.

Archetypes are a positioning shorthand (Mark and Pearson, 2001; after Jung), not science. Jester is a risk archetype in practice: humor ages, offends and fails across cultures. Pair it with a dependable product experience so the joke is the wrapper, not the service.

## Voice and tone

- Light, quick and self-aware; jokes about the brand, the category or a shared situation, never at the customer's expense.
- Use one comedic device per surface: pun, deadpan, exaggeration, or understatement. Keep calls to action literal ("Add to basket").
- Switch to plain voice for errors, payments, safety and anything involving loss or health.
- Headline register: "Snacks with strong opinions."

## Fits and tensions

- **Fits:** snacks and drinks, toys and kids media, consumer apps and games, education for beginners, entertainment, telecom challengers, pet brands, greeting cards and events.
- **Common blends:** Jester + Everyperson (friendly mainstream brands); Jester + Innocent (family and kids); Jester + Outlaw (irreverent challengers); Jester + Creator (maker platforms and design tools). Treat the first as the primary archetype that sets color and type, and the second as the secondary that sets the joke style.
- **Tensions:** Jester vs trust and seriousness: finance, health, legal and security require a calm layer; jokes must stop at the money, the diagnosis and the contract. Jester vs luxury: humor can work in premium (wit), rarely in ultra-luxury.
- **Accessibility tension:** bounces, wiggles and confetti can trigger vestibular issues and distraction; provide `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives and avoid flashing above 3 per second.
- **Cultural tension:** humor does not travel; test jokes with local reviewers per market.

## Clichés to avoid

- Memphis squiggles, confetti and emoji applied as a coat of paint to a serious product: derive the joke from the product truth.
- Corporate "quirky" copy ("Oops! Something went wrong!") for failures that cost users money.
- Mascot with no personality system; define voice, poses and rules.
- Rainbow gradients plus comic fonts (Comic Sans, Chalkboard) as a shortcut for fun.

## Reference anchors

- Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001); Carl G. Jung's trickster figure in *The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious*
- Skittles, M&M's, Ben & Jerry's, Old Spice, Oatly, Duolingo: commonly cited Jester or Jester-leaning brands
- Memphis Group (Ettore Sotsass) and Saul Bass for graphic wit
- Paula Scher and Stefan Sagmeister for expressive, funny typography
- Pixar and Aardman for character-driven timing and squash-and-stretch
