# Lover

## Essence

- **Core desire:** intimacy and experience; to be in a relationship with people, work and surroundings they love.
- **Fear:** being alone, unwanted or unloved.
- **Promise:** "You are desirable, and this will make the moment unforgettable." Attraction through beauty and attention.
- **Mark and Pearson group:** belonging and enjoyment (with Everyperson and Jester). Strategy: become more physically and emotionally attractive; connect through sensory detail.
- **Shadow:** objectification, obsession, vanity, manufactured insecurity.

Archetypes are a positioning shorthand (Mark and Pearson, 2001; after Jung), not science. "Lover" is not only romance: it covers any brand sold on appreciation and sensory pleasure, from chocolate to hotels to a well-made chair.

## Voice and tone

- Sensory, warm, unhurried; second person; implied rather than explicit. Appeals to taste, touch, scent, sound.
- Short lines with space: "Slow mornings. Soft light." Avoid pressure tactics, countdowns and shame.
- Express desire through detail (the fold of a collar, the temper of the chocolate), not adjectives such as "luxurious".
- Respect: no stereotyping or objectifying; consent and care are part of the tone.

## Fits and tensions

- **Fits:** fragrance and cosmetics, fashion, jewelry, chocolate and confectionery, wine, hotels and spas, florists, dating and relationship services, weddings, fine dining.
- **Common blends:** Lover + Creator (artisan perfumery); Lover + Ruler (luxury houses); Lover + Magician (fragrance narrative); Lover + Jester (playful romance and gifting); Lover + Caregiver (self-care brands). Keep one primary archetype in control of type and palette and let the secondary appear in copy or a single motif.
- **Tensions:** Lover vs utility: dense, transactional flows (checkout, account, returns) must stay plain; keep the romance in imagery and copy. Lover vs value tier: indulgence requires material evidence, not just a rose-colored palette.
- **Regulatory notes:** beauty, alcohol and intimate-product advertising face platform and national limits on imagery and claims; confirm with counsel for the market.

## Clichés to avoid

- Red roses, red hearts, couples at sunset, rose-gold gradient everything: choose a specific moment, material or setting.
- Script fonts at 12px or in ALL CAPS; script is for one or two words only.
- Overly retouched skin and one narrow beauty standard.
- Pink-and-gold baby-shower template look on adult brands.

## Reference anchors

- Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001); Carl G. Jung's archetype theory
- Godiva, Chanel, Häagen-Dazs, Valentino, Alfa Romeo: commonly cited Lover brands
- *Vogue* and *Harper's Bazaar* art direction (Alexey Brodovitch) for editorial fashion layout
- Art Nouveau (Alphonse Mucha) and Art Deco posters for curves and ornament
- Wong Kar-wai's films for color, slow motion and intimacy in moving image
