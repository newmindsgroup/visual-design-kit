# Caregiver

## Essence

- **Core desire:** to protect and care for others.
- **Fear:** selfishness and ingratitude; failing to protect someone.
- **Promise:** "We look after you (and the people you look after)." Service delivered with generosity and competence.
- **Mark and Pearson group:** stability and control (with Creator and Ruler). Strategy: do things for others.
- **Shadow:** martyrdom, paternalism, guilt-driven marketing, care that is a sales funnel.

Archetypes are a positioning shorthand (Mark and Pearson, 2001; after Jung), not science. Caregiver brands are judged by what happens at the moment of need; the visual system must hold up under stress, not only in a calm brand film.

## Voice and tone

- Warm, steady, plain. Name the person's situation first, then the help. Reading grade 6-8; no jargon without translation.
- Say what happens next and who they can call; give times and numbers (hours, costs, expected wait).
- Avoid guilt, fear and urgency pressure; do not use "we know how hard this is" as filler. Be specific instead.
- Headline register: "Questions about your claim? Talk to Maria's team today."

## Fits and tensions

- **Fits:** healthcare and clinics, health insurance, pharmacy, mental health and wellness, senior care, parenting and baby care, nonprofits and charities, education support, utilities in hardship programs, pet care.
- **Common blends:** Caregiver + Innocent (baby care); Caregiver + Sage (medical information); Caregiver + Ruler (institutions that protect, such as insurers and public health); Caregiver + Hero (rescue and emergency services); Caregiver + Everyperson (community services). Let the primary archetype set type and palette; the secondary shows in tone or one signature device.
- **Tensions:** Caregiver vs sleek premium: care that looks like a luxury product can feel cold. Caregiver vs fundraising: guilt imagery works short-term but erodes dignity; use agency-based stories. Caregiver vs data privacy: protection promises must match data practices.
- **Regulatory notes:** health, insurance and charity advertising carry claim, consent and image-use rules (for example HIPAA for protected health information in the US, fundraising regulations); confirm requirements with counsel for the jurisdiction.

## Clichés to avoid

- Hands forming a heart, stock doctors with stethoscopes, teal-and-white gradients with a cross: replace with real staff, real spaces, specific services.
- Pastel blob backgrounds and handwriting fonts as shorthand for warmth.
- Pity imagery and "your gift saves a life" templates.
- Cute friendliness that hides how to reach a person.

## Reference anchors

- Margaret Mark and Carol S. Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001); Carl G. Jung on the mother archetype in *The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious*
- UNICEF, International Committee of the Red Cross, Johnson & Johnson, Pampers, Volvo (safety positioning): commonly cited Caregiver brands
- NHS Digital Service Manual and GOV.UK Design System for calm, accessible service design
- Braille Institute's Atkinson Hyperlegible typeface for legibility-led type design
- Mayo Clinic and Cleveland Clinic patient-education content as plain-language health communication models
