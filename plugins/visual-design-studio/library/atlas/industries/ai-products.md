# AI Products

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Knowledge workers:** draft, summarize, search and analyze faster, while still being able to defend the result to a boss or client.
- **Developers and technical users:** generate and review code, call models by API, control cost, latency and context.
- **Creators:** explore ideas and variations, keep authorship and style, understand what a tool may do with their work.
- **Consumers and companions:** ask questions, get help with writing or learning, sometimes seek emotional support; vulnerable users need safeguards.
- **Enterprise buyers:** need data-handling answers, admin controls, auditability and predictable cost before deployment.
- **Skeptics and reviewers:** want to know when AI is involved, what it cannot do and who is accountable.

## Trust signals

- **Disclosure:** make it unmistakable when the user is talking to AI or reading AI-generated content (label, avatar treatment, system message) without apologizing for it.
- **Sources and citations:** inline numbered references that open the exact passage, with source names, dates and a clear difference between quoted and generated text.
- **Uncertainty:** signal confidence honestly (hedged wording, "I could not verify this", refusal states, low-confidence flags); do not decorate guesses with precise-looking percentages.
- **Human control:** edit, regenerate, undo, stop generation, approve before an agent acts, see the plan and tool calls, roll back changes.
- **Data clarity:** plain statement of what is stored, what is used for training, retention period and how to delete or opt out.
- **Visible limits:** model name or version, knowledge cutoff, supported languages, rate limits, known failure modes and a report-a-problem path.

## Category conventions

- Chat as the primary interface: a message list, a composer with attachments and mode toggles, streaming text, suggested prompts.
- Side panels for artifacts, canvases, code, sources and history; model pickers; "thinking" or tool-use indicators.
- Neutral off-white or near-black surfaces, one accent, a clean sans for UI and often a serif for responses.
- Marketing pages with a demo video or interactive prompt box, benchmark charts, safety and research pages, and enterprise logos.
- Pricing by seat plus usage or credits, with free tier limits shown in the UI.
- Copy buttons, thumbs up/down feedback, share links and conversation search.

## Where to differentiate

- **A human point of view:** a distinct voice, typographic personality and color world replaces the generic "AI look"; warm paper tones, serif response text and real illustration all read as deliberate.
- **Show the work:** surface sources, steps and tool calls as first-class UI, not a hidden accordion; make verification fast.
- **Controls that match risk:** low-stakes drafts auto-apply; high-stakes actions (send, delete, pay, deploy) require explicit review with a diff.
- **Designed failure states:** refusals, timeouts, rate limits, hallucination reports and "I don't know" are brand moments; write them with care.
- **Context made visible:** show which files, memory or web results the answer used and let the user remove them.
- **Speed that feels right:** stream tokens at a readable pace, keep layout stable while text arrives, give long tasks progress and a way to leave and return.

## Category clichés

- Sparkle and four-point star icons used for every AI feature.
- Purple-to-blue or purple-to-pink gradients, aurora meshes and glowing orbs or "pulse" blobs.
- Brains, circuit-board heads, neural-network node graphs and humanoid robots.
- Floating glass panels, particle fields and animated waveforms behind every hero.
- "Supercharge", "unlock", "10x" and "your AI copilot" headlines; the product named "Genie", "Magic" or "Assistant" with no distinct idea.
- Typing animation that implies a human is on the other end.
- Fix: choose a metaphor that fits the job (a desk, a library, a workshop, a ledger), a restrained palette with one non-purple accent, and an icon for AI features that describes the action (summarize, draft, search), not the technology.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; AI regulation is moving, so check dates before launch.

- **EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689), transparency duties at a high level:** people must be told when they interact with an AI system unless it is obvious from context; providers of generative systems must mark synthetic audio, image, video and text outputs in a machine-readable way; deployers must disclose deepfakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest (with an exception for human editorial review); users of emotion recognition or biometric categorization must inform the people exposed. Article 50 duties began applying in August 2026, with transition periods for some marking duties; verify current dates and scope. Some education, employment, credit and similar uses are classed high-risk with further duties.
- **Provenance:** adopt content credentials (C2PA) and visible labels where outputs could be mistaken for authentic media.
- **US consumer protection:** the FTC treats misleading AI capability claims ("AI-powered", accuracy, human-level) as deceptive advertising; substantiate claims and avoid implying human review that does not exist. Fake AI-generated reviews and testimonials are banned by the 2024 FTC rule.
- **Privacy:** GDPR, UK GDPR and CCPA/CPRA govern chat logs, uploads, voice and training use; show consent and retention controls in the interface, not only in the policy. COPPA, GDPR Article 8 and age-appropriate design codes apply when minors may use the product.
- **Sector rules:** health (HIPAA, FDA software rules), finance (fair lending, model risk), employment (bias-audit laws such as New York City Local Law 144) and insurance add disclosures and human-review requirements.
- **Accessibility:** ADA and the European Accessibility Act apply; streaming text must work with screen readers (polite live regions, no focus theft), and voice or canvas modes need non-visual alternatives.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Marketing site: home with live or recorded demo, product pages by use case, safety/trust and research pages, pricing, enterprise and security, docs and API.
- Core app: empty state with starter prompts, chat thread, composer with attachments and mode controls, sources panel, artifact/canvas view, history and search, settings, memory and data controls.
- Agent UI: task plan, step log with tool calls, approval prompts with diffs, interruptible progress, result review, audit trail.
- Trust surfaces: AI disclosure labels, citation hover cards, feedback and report flows, refusal and error states, privacy dashboard.
- Brand: logo, voice and persona rules, motion for "thinking" and streaming, icon set for AI actions, onboarding.

## Voice and content

- Plain, calm, first-person only when a persona is intended; avoid pretending to feelings or human history.
- State capability and limit together: "I can summarize this document; I cannot see attachments from earlier chats."
- Cite specifically ("Source 3, p. 12") and distinguish quotation from paraphrase from inference.
- Refusals explain what cannot be done and offer an alternative; no moralizing.
- Avoid words that overclaim ("understands", "knows", "guaranteed accurate") and anthropomorphic filler ("thinking...", "I'm excited to help!") unless the product persona demands it.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.quiet-minimal` + `archetype.sage` + `tier.premium`: warm neutral surfaces, a refined sans plus serif response text, one non-purple accent, generous space around the composer. Familiar and trustworthy; differentiate through voice, citations UI and motion restraint.
- **Adjacent:** `style.editorial-magazine` + `archetype.creator`, borrowed from publishing and research tools: footnotes, pull quotes, serif display, annotation-style citations. Makes sources and authorship visible and suits research, writing and analysis products.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.handcrafted-organic` + `archetype.caregiver` (for `audience.older-adults` or wellness and learning assistants): hand-drawn marks, textured paper, warm color, human-scale illustration against the synthetic look. Memorable and disarming. Risk: can undercut perceived technical capability and precision for enterprise or high-stakes use, and generated "hand-drawn" assets feel false; commission real illustration and keep citations and controls crisp.

## Metrics that matter

- Activation: first successful task completed, time to first useful answer.
- Return rate and tasks per week; retention by cohort; share of sessions with an accepted output.
- Trust measures: citation click-through, edit rate before accept, regeneration rate, thumbs down with reason, reports of harmful or wrong output.
- Safety and quality: refusal appropriateness, hallucination rate on a fixed evaluation set, escalation to humans.
- Cost and latency: time to first token, p95 completion time, cost per task.
- Enterprise: admin adoption, audit-log use, security review time.
