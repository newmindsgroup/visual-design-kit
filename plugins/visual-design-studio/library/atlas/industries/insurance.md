# Insurance

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Shoppers:** compare quotes quickly, understand what is and is not covered, find a fair price without a phone call.
- **Policyholders:** find documents and ID cards, change coverage, pay bills, add drivers or property, understand renewal increases.
- **Claimants in distress:** report a loss, upload evidence, know what happens next and when money arrives. This is the moment the brand is judged.
- **Brokers and agents:** quote across carriers, bind, service accounts and explain coverage to clients.
- **Employers and benefits administrators:** enroll people, compare plans, manage changes on deadlines.
- **Health plan members:** find in-network care, understand deductibles and out-of-pocket costs, see what a visit will cost.

## Trust signals

- Financial strength ratings (AM Best, S&P) and licensing details where required, shown without hiding the product.
- Clear coverage summaries in plain language, with examples ("If a pipe bursts, we pay for the repair and the damaged floor").
- Transparent pricing factors, discount logic and renewal-change explanations.
- Claims transparency: status tracker, expected timelines, named adjuster, average payout speed backed by data.
- Real people: phone, chat, local agent; accessible 24/7 emergency claims line.
- Complaint and regulator information, plus customer reviews with source and date.

## Category conventions

- Quote engines: multi-step forms with progress indicators, address autocomplete, prefilled data, a comparison of coverage levels.
- Plan comparison tables (good / better / best), coverage sliders and deductible selectors.
- Digital ID cards, policy documents library, billing, claims center, roadside or emergency assistance buttons.
- Blue, teal and green palettes; shields, umbrellas, houses, cars, hands cradling a family; friendly flat illustration or lifestyle photography.
- Character mascots and memorable ad campaigns in US auto and home markets.
- Long legal disclosures in footers, PDFs of policy language and state-specific notices.

## Where to differentiate

- **Make the policy readable:** a designed coverage summary (what is covered, what is not, what you pay, how to claim) as a first-class page and document, not just a PDF.
- **Design the claim:** a calm, step-by-step flow with photo capture guidance, status tracking and honest timelines; this is where loyalty is won.
- **Explain the price:** show how each input moves the premium and why a renewal changed.
- **Numbers and money typography:** clear premium, deductible and limit presentation with tabular figures and consistent units.
- **Tone:** empathetic and direct without cartoon cheer; humor only in acquisition, never in claims.
- **Specific verticals:** pet, renters, travel and micro-duration cover can carry a lighter personality than life or commercial lines.

## Category clichés

- Umbrellas, shields, padlocks, hands cradling houses, cars or families under a protective dome.
- Stock photos of smiling families, couples with a baby and handshakes with an agent.
- "We've got you covered", "Peace of mind" and "Insurance made simple" headlines.
- Blue gradient banners with a "Get a free quote in minutes" button over a stock photo.
- Fine-print footers that hide exclusions, waiting periods and limits.
- Fix: show an actual coverage example, a real claim timeline and a real person; put exclusions where the decision is made.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; insurance is regulated by state in the US and by national supervisors elsewhere, so rules vary by line and place.

- **State insurance regulation (US):** each state's department of insurance regulates advertising, forms, rates and licensing; the NAIC publishes model advertising and unfair trade practice rules that states adopt in varying forms. Ads and websites must not mislead about coverage, savings or availability; claims like "save up to X%" need substantiation and often qualifying text.
- **Disclosures and licensing:** agency and producer license numbers, company names, state availability and "not available in all states" statements often must appear on quote pages and ads; keep them legible (size, contrast) and placed near the claim they qualify.
- **Standardized documents:** the EU/UK Insurance Product Information Document (IPID) is a standardized short summary of cover; US health plans use the Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC) and uniform glossary. Design around these formats rather than replacing them.
- **Medicare and health marketing (US):** CMS rules govern Medicare Advantage and Part D marketing, including required disclaimers and approval of materials; ACA marketplace plans have plan-display and plain-language requirements. Verify the current CMS marketing rules before any Medicare campaign.
- **UK and EU conduct rules:** the FCA Consumer Duty requires firms to deliver good outcomes, including understandable communications; the EU Insurance Distribution Directive sets pre-contract information requirements.
- **Fairness and AI:** state laws and NAIC guidance on algorithmic underwriting and unfair discrimination affect how automated decisions and price factors are explained; Colorado has a specific statute on external data and algorithms.
- **Privacy, consent and e-signature:** GLBA privacy notices, state insurance privacy rules, GDPR and CCPA; ESIGN/UETA consent flows for electronic delivery of policies and notices.
- **Accessibility:** ADA claims target insurer sites and portals; the European Accessibility Act covers parts of financial services in the EU. Build to WCAG 2.2 AA.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Marketing site: product pages by line, how it works, coverage explainers, claims process, financial strength and about, help center, agent/broker locator.
- Quote and bind: eligibility start, personal and asset details, coverage selection, price breakdown, payment, policy documents, welcome.
- Member portal and app: dashboard of policies, ID cards, documents, billing, claims center, change requests, roadside or emergency help, notifications.
- Claims flow: report loss, guided evidence capture, status timeline, messaging, settlement and payment.
- Brand: logo and mascot or illustration system, policy-document templates, email and SMS notifications, broker materials, ads.

## Voice and content

- Plain language at reading grade 6-8; define terms (deductible, premium, limit, exclusion) at first use and link a glossary.
- Use concrete examples and numbers; avoid "may", "subject to" chains without saying to what.
- Claims copy is warm and procedural: "We're sorry this happened. Here is what happens next."
- Renewal-change emails state the old price, new price, the reasons and what to do to lower it.
- Never use fear-based imagery or countdown tactics; regulators and customers react badly to them.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.flat-material` + `archetype.caregiver` + `tier.mid-market`: friendly flat illustration, one saturated brand color on warm white, large readable type, simple shapes. Familiar to shoppers; differentiate through claims UX and coverage clarity.
- **Adjacent:** `style.illustrative-narrative` + `archetype.everyperson` + `tier.value`, borrowed from consumer apps and children's publishing: a character and scene system that explains coverage with stories. Lightens a heavy category and works for renters, pet and travel cover.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.memphis-postmodern` + `archetype.jester` (for `audience.teens-gen-z` buying renters, gadget or pet cover): bold pattern, loud color and joyful geometry in a category of blue gradients. Strong recall for low-stakes lines. Risk: tonal mismatch at claim time and pattern noise that buries required disclosures; switch to a calm mode in claims, billing and legal screens.

## Metrics that matter

- Quote start to quote completion, quote-to-bind conversion, cost per bound policy.
- Retention and renewal rate; share of renewal changes with a clear explanation viewed.
- Claims: time to first contact, time to settle, digital claim completion, claim satisfaction.
- Support contacts per policy and containment rate in self-service.
- Disclosure and complaint indicators: regulator complaints, accessibility issues, advertising reviews passed first time.
