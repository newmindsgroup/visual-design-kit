# Pharma and Biotech

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Patients and caregivers:** understand a diagnosis, what a treatment does, how to take it, what side effects to expect, how to afford it and where to get support.
- **Healthcare professionals (HCPs):** find prescribing information, clinical data, dosing and safety quickly; compare options; claim samples and resources.
- **Payers and formulary decision makers:** evaluate evidence, cost-effectiveness and access programs.
- **Clinical trial participants and sites:** learn whether a study fits, what participation involves and how to enroll.
- **Investors and analysts:** read pipeline, milestones, financials and science without hype.
- **Scientists, partners and recruits:** judge platform credibility, publications and culture.

## Trust signals

- Approved labeling visible and linked: prescribing information, patient information, medication guide, boxed warnings where they exist.
- Data presented with sample size, endpoints, confidence intervals, comparator and limitations, with citations to published studies.
- Clear safety information shown with prominence similar to benefit claims: Important Safety Information (ISI), indications and contraindications.
- Real patient stories with disclosure of compensation and typicality; named medical reviewers and dates on disease education.
- Transparent access information: copay programs, patient assistance eligibility, hotlines.
- Scientific credibility for biotech: publications, advisory board, preclinical and clinical stage labeled honestly, investigational status stated.

## Category conventions

- Disease-state education hubs, symptom checkers and "ask your doctor" discussion guides.
- Branded drug sites with a persistent ISI bar or panel, a link to full prescribing information, indication statement and a patient-vs-HCP split.
- Calm blue, teal or brand-color palettes with a bright accent; aspirational lifestyle photography of patients enjoying life.
- Mechanism-of-action animations, molecule graphics and clinical-data charts.
- Biotech and platform companies: dark or white science-lab aesthetics, molecular imagery, pipeline tables, investor pages.
- Heavy footers: terms, privacy, adverse-event reporting contact, copyright, job codes (internal approval numbers).

## Where to differentiate

- **Make safety information readable:** a well-set ISI (large enough, high contrast, scannable headings, sensible hierarchy) shows respect and supports compliance.
- **Disease education that helps:** tools, checklists and doctor-visit guides that are useful independent of the brand.
- **Representation with care:** patients with real conditions, varied ages and backgrounds, photographed candidly, with consent and non-stereotyped depictions.
- **Science made visible:** illustrations and data visualization that explain mechanism and results honestly, with consistent encodings and clear axes.
- **A modern identity for biotech:** typography-driven or generative visuals from real data rather than helixes and pipettes.
- **Patient-support design:** forms and enrollment flows that are short, accessible and respectful of privacy.

## Category clichés

- DNA helixes, floating molecules, pills in a heart shape, glowing neural networks and hands holding a glowing cell.
- Sunlit seniors walking dogs or cycling on a beach and couples laughing in a kitchen.
- Blue gradient backdrops with a white overlay of "Science. Care. Hope."
- Fast-scrolling ISI text under a video that obscures it; side-effect lists in tiny gray type.
- "Innovating for life", "Breakthrough science for patients" headlines and a stock scientist in goggles.
- Fix: lead with the patient need and the specific data point; set safety text at body size; use illustration or photography that reflects the actual condition and treatment.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; pharma communication is among the most controlled, and rules differ sharply by country.

- **FDA prescription drug promotion (US):** promotional claims must be consistent with approved labeling, truthful and not misleading, and presented with fair balance of risk and benefit (21 CFR 202.1; FDCA). Branded promotion usually carries the indication, the most important risk information (the ISI) and a path to full prescribing information; broadcast ads rely on a "major statement" plus "adequate provision". FDA has signaled rulemaking on broadcast direct-to-consumer ads and stepped up enforcement; verify current status. Materials are submitted to FDA at first use (Form FDA 2253) and are subject to untitled and warning letters.
- **Design consequences of fair balance:** risk information needs comparable prominence to benefit claims (size, contrast, placement, pacing, audio); persistent or sticky ISI panels must be keyboard-accessible and readable, not obscured by overlays, autoplaying media or tiny type.
- **Digital and social media:** character-limited platforms, influencer posts and paid search ads still need risk information or a compliant link; FDA draft guidances address these cases. Unbranded disease awareness and branded content follow different rules; do not mix them casually.
- **OTC medicines and supplements:** OTC drugs carry a Drug Facts label format; dietary supplements follow structure/function claim rules with a required disclaimer and FTC substantiation.
- **EU and UK:** advertising prescription-only medicines to the public is prohibited in the EU (Directive 2001/83/EC), so patient sites focus on disease education and HCP-gated content; the UK ABPI Code and PMCPA, and the EFPIA Code, govern promotion; HCP areas require professional verification.
- **Medical devices:** EU MDR (Regulation 2017/745) restricts claims for devices and software; the FDA regulates device labeling and promotion. Intended-use statements must match clearance or approval.
- **Clinical trials:** recruitment ads need ethics committee or IRB review, must not claim safety or efficacy of an investigational product and must reflect registry information.
- **Investor and corporate communications:** SEC Regulation FD and forward-looking-statement cautions apply to investor pages and press releases.
- **Privacy and transparency:** HIPAA for patient support data, GDPR for EU patients, adverse-event reporting contacts, and transparency or sunshine rules for payments to HCPs.
- **Accessibility:** ADA and the European Accessibility Act apply to patient sites; ISI panels, carousels and videos need WCAG 2.2 AA behavior.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Branded product site: home with indication and ISI, how it works, efficacy and safety data, dosing, support and savings, doctor discussion guide, full prescribing information.
- HCP portal: gating or verification, clinical data, dosing calculators, resources, sample and medical information requests.
- Disease awareness site: symptom explainer, self-assessment, doctor finder, patient stories.
- Corporate and investor site: pipeline, science, publications, news, ESG, careers, investor presentation.
- Clinical trial pages: eligibility, locations, what to expect, contact and consent information.
- Brand and materials: logo and product mark, patient brochures, detail aids, congress booth graphics, packaging and labels, email and CRM templates.

## Voice and content

- Plain, respectful and medically accurate; no cure language unless approved; use "may" only where the label does.
- Define terms (efficacy, contraindication) on first use; target reading grade 6-8 for patient content.
- Present benefit and risk in comparable formats (numbers and absolute rates rather than only relative risk).
- Tone for HCPs: concise, data-first, with citations; tone for patients: empathetic, practical, without false cheer.
- Avoid overpromising ("transform your life"); avoid fear of missing out.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.corporate-modern` + `archetype.caregiver` + `tier.premium`: clean layouts, one brand color with a restrained accent, humane photography, a clearly set ISI. Credible and reviewable by regulatory teams; differentiate through typography, data visuals and patient tools.
- **Adjacent:** `style.swiss-international` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from scientific publishing and institutions: rigorous grid, tabular data design and numbered references. Suits HCP sites, clinical data hubs and investor materials.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.kinetic-experimental-type` + `archetype.magician` (for biotech platform companies): expressive, animated type and generative visuals derived from real data instead of helixes. Strong differentiation for recruiting and partnerships. Risk: motion and expressive type conflict with fair-balance readability, accessibility and reviewer comfort; keep any product claim, ISI and patient-facing content static and conventional.

## Metrics that matter

- Patient site: visit to program enrollment, doctor-discussion-guide downloads, savings-card activations.
- HCP site: verification completion, time to find prescribing information, resource downloads, medical information requests.
- Readability and accessibility: ISI engagement, scroll depth to safety information, assistive-technology test results.
- Trial recruitment: eligibility screener completion, qualified referrals, cost per enrolled participant.
- Investor and corporate: time on pipeline pages, press pickup, careers applications.
- Compliance: first-pass approval rate in medical-legal-regulatory review, corrections after launch.

## Reference anchors

- FDA Office of Prescription Drug Promotion (OPDP) guidance and untitled/warning letters as the practical standard for fair balance
- Moderna and Pfizer for large-company brand systems with bold typography and scientific storytelling
- Novo Nordisk and Genentech for patient education and diabetes or oncology content ecosystems
- Ginkgo Bioworks and Recursion for biotech platform identities driven by data and generative imagery
- NHS and MedlinePlus for plain-language patient information design
- ClinicalTrials.gov for trial information structure
