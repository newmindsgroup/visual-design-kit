# Events and Conferences

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Prospective attendees:** decide if it is worth the money and time; see who is speaking, what they will learn, who they will meet, the price and dates, and how to convince a manager.
- **Registered attendees:** build a personal agenda, find rooms, meet people, get updates, and never feel lost or excluded.
- **Speakers and artists:** submit, get briefed, know stage and AV details, promote their session.
- **Sponsors and exhibitors:** package tiers, booth specs, lead capture, brand placement, measurable return.
- **Organizers and volunteers:** run registration, scheduling, communications, signage, check-in and incident handling.
- **Remote participants (hybrid):** watch live, interact, access recordings, feel included.
- **Press and communities:** accreditation, assets, embargoes, hashtags, social sharing.
- **Neighbors and venues:** logistics, noise, safety, crowd management.

## Trust signals

- Clear dates, location, venue address and travel information; an up-to-date programme with named, confirmed speakers.
- All-in pricing: ticket types, taxes and fees shown before checkout; refund and transfer policy in plain words.
- Past editions: photos, attendance numbers, testimonials with names and companies, session recordings.
- Accessibility information published before purchase (step-free routes, quiet room, captioning, sign language, assistance animals, food and diet).
- Code of conduct and safety statement, with a contact route for incidents.
- Official partners and sponsors with logos used correctly; no fake "sold out" urgency.

## Category conventions

- Big date-and-place lockup in the hero, countdown or "Early bird ends" notices, an Add to calendar action.
- Speaker grid with circular or square portraits, session list with times and tracks, venue map, sponsor logo wall.
- Ticket tiers in three columns, with a recommended middle tier; group and student discounts.
- Bold typography and a graphic device (a shape, pattern or gradient) that repeats across site, badge, slides and stage.
- Email sequences: save the date, speakers announced, early bird, final call, logistics, thank you and recordings.
- Event app: agenda, networking, maps, notifications, QR check-in, polls and Q&A.

## Where to differentiate

- **Identity as a kit of parts:** a flexible system of pattern, color, type and motion that works on a lanyard, a stage LED wall, a 6-second social clip and a wayfinding sign.
- **Programming as brand:** curation voice, session formats, unusual venues and commissioned art rather than a generic stage-and-keynote look.
- **Wayfinding and flow:** signage with large numerals, color-coded zones, clear sightlines, and consistent room names.
- **Attendee experience design:** personalized agenda, easy introductions, quiet spaces, mentors, clear food and transport info.
- **Accessibility and inclusion as selling points:** live captions on all stages, sign interpretation, sensory-friendly hours, bursary and childcare options.
- **Post-event value:** edited video within a week, slide decks, a community platform that keeps the brand alive 12 months.
- **Sustainability reporting:** travel, waste and energy numbers shared honestly.

## Category clichés

- Purple-to-blue or sunset gradient with a floating 3D shape; stock crowd silhouettes with light flares.
- "Join the revolution", "Where innovation meets impact", "Connect, collaborate, create".
- Speaker grids of circle-cropped headshots with inconsistent lighting; 40 sponsor logos at equal weight.
- Countdown timers resetting every visit; "Only 3 tickets left" on every tier.
- Tiny gray fine print for service fees; unreadable session times on mobile.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Price transparency:** the US FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees (effective 12 May 2025) requires the total price, including mandatory fees, to be shown up front for live-event tickets and short-term lodging; check whether the event type falls within scope, and align conference ticket pages anyway. The UK DMCC Act 2024 bans "drip pricing" (from April 2025), and the EU Consumer Rights Directive requires full price disclosure.
- **Ticket resale and bots:** the US BOTS Act bans circumventing ticket purchase limits with software; the UK has similar offences. Resale marketplaces have disclosure duties in some countries.
- **Accessibility:** ADA Title III covers venues and events in the US (effective communication, accessible seating, and aids such as captioning or interpreters on request); the UK Equality Act 2010 requires reasonable adjustments; the EAA covers ticketing and e-commerce services in the EU from June 2025. Registration forms and event apps need WCAG 2.2 AA, and forms should ask about access needs.
- **Privacy and lead retrieval:** attendee data, badge scanning and photo or video recording need lawful basis and notice (GDPR/ePrivacy, state privacy laws); badges and apps should show what scanning shares. CAN-SPAM, CASL and TCPA/PECR rules govern marketing email and SMS.
- **Fire and safety signage:** exit signs and routes must stay visible; banners and fabrics in venues often need flame-retardant treatment (NFPA 701 in the US) and fire-marshal approval; occupancy limits affect queue and layout design. Local codes apply; confirm with the venue.
- **Sponsorship and endorsements:** FTC Endorsement Guides and platform rules require disclosure when influencers or partners are paid; sponsor logo usage follows each sponsor's brand rules.
- **Refunds and cancellation:** consumer law and event-cancellation insurance determine policy wording; display the rule before payment.
- **Intellectual property:** event names and slogans need trademark clearance; commissioned artwork, fonts and music need event-scale licenses.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Identity: logo and lockups (horizontal, stacked, date-and-place), color, type, pattern, motion toolkit, templates for slides, lower-thirds and social.
- Website: home, programme and speakers, tickets and checkout, venue and travel, accessibility, sponsors, code of conduct, FAQs, press.
- Registration and ticketing flow: ticket selection, attendee details, group purchase, invoice, confirmation with calendar file and wallet pass.
- Event app: agenda, search, maps, networking, notifications, check-in, feedback.
- Physical: badges and lanyards (name at 36pt+, readable from 2 m), signage, stage design, LED content, wayfinding, merchandise, printed programme.
- Sponsor materials: prospectus, booth specs, activation guidelines, lead-retrieval tools.

## Voice and content

- Lead with what people will learn or do, not adjectives: "Two days of 40 sessions on payments infrastructure, 600 practitioners".
- Dates and times with time zones and 24-hour option; locations with maps and transit.
- Speaker bios 40-60 words with a concrete credential and topic.
- Plain calls to action: "Get tickets", "See the programme", "Book a stand".
- Accessibility and conduct copy is practical: "Quiet room on level 2, open all day".

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.vibrant-gradient-saas` + `archetype.magician` + `tier.mid-market` for tech and business conferences: bold type, a signature gradient or shape, high-energy speaker photography. Familiar to audiences; differentiate with curation and wayfinding.
- **Adjacent:** `style.collage-mixed-media` + `archetype.creator`, borrowed from music festivals and art fairs: cut-out portraits, layered posters, hand-made marks that suit creative, cultural and community events. Strong for social and print; check text contrast over busy layers.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.memphis-postmodern` + `archetype.jester`: clashing patterns, oversize shapes and playful type to break the corporate-conference sameness. Memorable on stage and in photos. Risk: pattern clutter hurts readability on schedules and signage, and it can undermine seriousness for regulated, enterprise or government-facing events; keep programme, ticket and safety pages calm and use pattern as an accent.

## Metrics that matter

- Ticket sales curve versus target, share by tier, conversion rate from site visit to purchase, abandoned-checkout rate.
- Attendee experience: net promoter score, session ratings, queue times at check-in, app adoption.
- Sponsor and exhibitor outcomes: leads per booth, renewal rate, satisfaction.
- Accessibility: requests fulfilled, complaints, share of sessions captioned.
- Post-event: recording views, community sign-ups, repeat attendance, cost per attendee.
