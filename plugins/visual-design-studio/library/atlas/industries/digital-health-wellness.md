# Digital Health and Wellness

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Self-improvers:** build sleep, movement, meditation or nutrition habits and see progress without guilt.
- **People managing a condition:** track symptoms, glucose, blood pressure or medication; share data with a clinician; avoid alarm fatigue.
- **Patients seeking convenient care:** get a consultation, prescription or lab test online with transparency on cost, clinician and next steps.
- **People in distress:** reach support for anxiety, depression, grief or crisis quickly and privately.
- **Athletes and quantified-self users:** compare recovery, strain and trends; want depth and export.
- **Employers and payers buying for members:** want engagement evidence, privacy assurances and outcomes reporting.

## Trust signals

- Named, credentialed clinicians and advisors with licenses; visible medical review dates on health content.
- Evidence: peer-reviewed studies or clinical validation with plain summaries and limits, not a footnote of unnamed "research".
- Privacy by design: clear statement of what is collected, never sold, how long it is kept, and easy delete/export; no hidden ad trackers.
- Honest scope: what the app does and does not do, when to see a doctor, crisis resources one tap away.
- Transparent pricing and subscription terms: trial length, renewal date, how to cancel.
- Regulatory status where relevant (cleared or authorized device features versus general wellness) stated accurately.

## Category conventions

- Soft rounded UI, gentle gradients and pastel or nature-inspired palettes; friendly illustration or calm photography.
- Onboarding quizzes that personalize a plan, daily check-ins, streaks, rings, scores and progress charts.
- Notifications that nudge, celebrate and remind; community or coach messaging.
- Wearables: dark data dashboards with sleep stages, heart rate variability, readiness or recovery scores.
- Telehealth: discreet packaging, subscription refills, before/after galleries, testimonial carousels, "see if you qualify" flows.
- Minimal text, large primary buttons, bottom-sheet modals and haptic feedback in native apps.

## Where to differentiate

- **Interpret, do not just display:** turn data into one clear insight and next action; explain what a score means and how it is computed.
- **Tone that respects the person:** kind, non-judgmental language; avoid streak shame, weight-loss moralizing and red failure states.
- **Distinct visual world:** move beyond pastel blobs with a specific illustration language, typography, photography style or data-visualization system.
- **Calm interaction design:** reduce notification volume, let users set quiet hours, make sensitive screens private by default.
- **Trust in the details:** show data provenance and last sync time, flag low-confidence readings, avoid false precision.
- **Care continuity:** connect app data to human clinicians, export to doctors and clear escalation paths.

## Category clichés

- Pastel gradient blobs, abstract breathing circles and lotus or zen-stone imagery.
- Sunrise-on-mountain stock photos, yoga silhouettes and people laughing alone with salad.
- Hearts with ECG lines, DNA helixes, brains with leaf growth and glowing human outlines.
- Stock "diverse smiling patients" in clinic lighting and doctors on tablets.
- "Your journey to a better you", "Take control of your health" headlines.
- Gamification that shames missed days; before/after photos with unrealistic claims.
- Fix: choose one specific visual idea tied to the product's function, show real people and real data, and write copy about outcomes the product can substantiate.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; whether HIPAA, FDA or consumer-privacy law applies depends on who provides the service and what it claims.

- **HIPAA:** applies to covered entities (providers, plans) and their business associates, not to every health app; telehealth companies that deliver care usually are covered. Do not assume that a wellness app is outside privacy law.
- **FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and Section 5:** the FTC amended the rule (effective July 2024) to cover health apps and similar technologies, treating unauthorized disclosure, such as sharing health data with advertising platforms, as a breach. FTC cases against GoodRx, BetterHelp and Premom turned on sharing sensitive data contrary to promises. Audit SDKs, pixels and analytics before launch.
- **State consumer health data laws:** Washington's My Health My Data Act and similar state rules require specific consent and privacy-policy placement for consumer health data; California's CMIA and CCPA add duties. Consent UI should be separate, specific and not bundled.
- **FDA:** software that diagnoses, treats or monitors disease may be a regulated medical device; the general wellness policy covers low-risk claims. Wording in the UI and marketing ("detects", "treats", "prevents") can move a product into device territory; keep claims aligned with clearance status and cite it accurately. EU MDR classifies much medical software as a device and the EU AI Act may add duties.
- **Advertising and endorsements:** the FTC requires substantiation for health claims; testimonials and before/after images must reflect typical results or clearly disclose what is typical. FDA has pursued telehealth marketers over claims for compounded drugs. Prescription drug promotion on telehealth sites must follow FDA advertising rules (see the pharma entry).
- **Subscriptions:** ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws require clear terms and simple cancellation (the FTC's separate click-to-cancel rule was vacated in 2025, but these laws still apply); UK and EU consumer rules add withdrawal rights.
- **Telehealth practice rules:** state licensure and prescribing rules affect where services appear and how clinicians are named; show location restrictions early.
- **Crisis and safety:** for mental-health content, display local crisis lines (for example 988 in the US) and follow safe-messaging guidance.
- **Accessibility and children:** ADA and the European Accessibility Act apply; COPPA and age-appropriate design codes apply if minors can use the app.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Native app: onboarding with goals and consent, home dashboard, daily check-in, trends and insights, content library, reminders, coach or clinician messaging, settings with data controls.
- Telehealth flow: eligibility quiz, medical intake, clinician review status, treatment plan, checkout, shipping and refills, support.
- Wearable dashboards: daily summary, sleep, activity, recovery and long-term trends; sync and device status.
- Marketing site: home, how it works, science and clinicians, pricing, safety, FAQ, blog; app store assets.
- Brand: logo and app icon, illustration or photography system, data-visualization palette, notification copy, motion for breathing and progress.

## Voice and content

- Warm, steady and specific: "You slept 6 h 40 min. That is 40 minutes under your goal. Try an earlier wind-down tonight."
- Explain scores and algorithms in one sentence and link to detail; avoid mystical or pseudo-scientific words.
- Separate information from medical advice and state when to contact a clinician.
- Positive framing without guilt; offer recovery after a missed day, not a reset to zero.
- Keep sensitive notification text neutral on the lock screen (no diagnoses or medications in the preview).

## Metrics that matter

- Activation (first completed check-in or session) and 7-, 30- and 90-day retention.
- Weekly active use tied to the health goal, not just opens; adherence to plan or medication.
- Telehealth: intake completion, time to clinician review, refill continuity, cost per active patient.
- Subscription health: trial-to-paid, involuntary churn, cancellation reasons, refund and chargeback rates.
- Safety: crisis-resource usage, adverse-event reports, content corrections.
- Privacy: opt-in rate for data sharing, delete/export requests handled on time.

## Reference anchors

- Headspace and Calm for illustration-led, gentle meditation and sleep experiences
- Oura and Whoop for wearable data presentation and scoring systems
- Apple Health and Fitness (Human Interface Guidelines for health data) for platform-native patterns and privacy defaults
- Hims & Hers and Ro for consumer telehealth branding and intake flows
- Clue and Flo for cycle tracking, with public attention to data privacy
- Omada Health for condition-management programs with coach and device integration
