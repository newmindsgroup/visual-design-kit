# B2B SaaS

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Economic buyers (VP, CFO, founder):** justify the spend, see risk and payback, skim pricing, security and customer proof in under two minutes.
- **Champions and daily users:** try the product before talking to sales, reach a first useful result fast, and never feel slowed down by the interface.
- **Admins, IT and security reviewers:** check SSO and SCIM, roles and audit logs, data residency, sub-processors and uptime history.
- **Procurement and legal:** collect the security questionnaire, DPA, accessibility conformance report and contract terms without a sales call.
- **Developers and integrators:** read API docs, test webhooks, check rate limits and versioning policy.
- **Switchers:** compare against the incumbent, estimate migration effort, ask "can I leave later?" (export, API, open formats).

## Trust signals

- Named customers with permission to show them: logos in the customer's own brand colors or one neutral tone, plus case studies with a measurable outcome and a named person.
- A public trust center: security overview, SOC 2 report availability, ISO 27001 scope, DPA, sub-processor list, status page with real incident history.
- Real product UI: actual screens with believable data, not abstract dashboards of rising lines.
- Pricing that can be read: tiers, limits, seat logic, what "Contact sales" means and roughly what it costs.
- Integration directory, changelog with recent dates, public roadmap or release notes; proof the product is alive.
- Humans: named support, solutions team and CSM presence; response-time commitments.

## Category conventions

- Marketing site: hero with promise, product shot and two CTAs ("Start free" and "Book a demo"), logo wall, feature sections, tabbed use cases, testimonial block, three-tier pricing, footer with security badges.
- App shell: left sidebar or top nav, global search or command palette (Cmd/Ctrl+K), light and dark themes, dense tables with filters, empty states with a primary action, onboarding checklist.
- Rounded cards, subtle shadows, a blue or indigo accent, Inter or a close neutral grotesque, line icons.
- Gated assets (whitepapers, calculators) and a demo-request form with 6-10 fields.
- Documentation, help center and community as part of the product surface.

## Where to differentiate

- **Show the product doing the job:** crop real UI to the one interaction that proves the claim; animate the actual state change rather than a generic loop.
- **Point of view in the copy and layout:** an opinionated claim ("Meetings are a bug report on your docs") reads as expertise; a feature list reads as everyone else.
- **Own a type and color pairing:** a licensed or custom display face plus one unexpected hue (ochre, forest, signal orange, deep teal) beats the default indigo gradient.
- **Craft in small places:** loading states, keyboard shortcuts, empty states, error messages, release notes and the pricing calculator.
- **Honest comparison and migration pages:** "Switch from X" with import tools shows confidence.
- **A system, not a campaign:** one illustration, photography or diagram language that scales from website to app to slides.

## Category clichés

- Purple-to-blue gradient mesh hero with floating UI cards and a glowing orb.
- "The all-in-one platform to supercharge your workflow" and "Built for modern teams" headlines.
- Three icon cards under the hero, each with a rounded square and a 14-word blurb.
- Stock photos of a diverse team around a laptop or a high-five.
- Isometric spot illustrations and abstract 3D blobs unrelated to the product.
- Unverified "Trusted by 10,000+ teams" counters and logo walls of companies that are free-plan users.
- Fix: lead with a specific outcome, a real screen and a named customer; cut any decoration that does not carry information.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; the regimes below are the usual ones.

- **Privacy and consent (GDPR, ePrivacy, CCPA/CPRA):** cookie and tracking banners must offer reject as easily as accept; opt-out links for sale or sharing of data in California; keep consent UI neutral in color weight and wording. Marketing forms need clear purpose text and a link to the privacy notice.
- **Security claims:** SOC 2 is an attestation report by an auditor, not a certification; ISO 27001 certificates have a defined scope. Badges and copy must match what the audit covers. Misstated claims invite deceptive-practice enforcement (FTC Act Section 5 in the US) and contract disputes.
- **Accessibility:** ADA claims target inaccessible websites in the US; the European Accessibility Act (applying since 28 June 2025) covers consumer-facing services; business buyers ask for an accessibility conformance report (VPAT/ACR) and may require EN 301 549 or Section 508 conformance. Build to WCAG 2.2 AA and keep the report current.
- **Testimonials and reviews:** FTC Endorsement Guides and the 2024 FTC rule on fake or incentivized reviews apply to customer quotes, review-site badges and incentives for G2-style reviews. Get written permission for logos and quotes and disclose material connections.
- **Subscriptions and renewals:** self-serve plans fall under ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws in the US (the FTC's separate click-to-cancel rule was vacated in 2025, but these laws still apply) and EU/UK consumer rules for individual buyers; show renewal terms before payment and make cancellation as easy as sign-up.
- **AI features:** disclose AI-generated output and chatbots; see the AI Products entry for EU AI Act transparency duties.
- **Sector overlays:** HIPAA business associate terms for health customers, FedRAMP for US federal, data residency promises for EU and public-sector buyers.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Marketing site: home, product and use-case pages, pricing, customers, security/trust, integrations, comparison pages, changelog, blog, docs, careers.
- Conversion flows: trial sign-up, demo request, pricing calculator, quote request, procurement packet download.
- Product: onboarding and activation checklist, home/dashboard, list and detail views, filters and saved views, bulk actions, settings, roles and permissions, billing and invoices, admin audit log, notifications, empty and error states.
- Brand and system: logo, type and color system, illustration or photo system, UI kit and tokens, motion guidelines, icon set.
- Sales and success: pitch deck, one-pagers, case study template, onboarding emails, in-app announcements, QBR template.

## Voice and content

- Name the user, the job and the result; use verbs and numbers ("Close your books in 3 days, not 9").
- Sentence case for UI; button labels say what happens ("Invite teammates", not "Submit").
- Explain limits and prices plainly; avoid "leverage", "synergy", "next-generation" and "solutions".
- Error messages say what happened, why, and how to recover; never blame the user.
- Release notes and empty states are brand voice opportunities; keep humor out of billing, permissions and security screens.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.corporate-modern` + `archetype.sage` + `tier.mid-market` (for `audience.b2b-buying-committee`): neutral grotesque, one confident accent, real product screens, clear information architecture. Safe and credible; differentiate through copy specificity and craft.
- **Adjacent:** `style.swiss-international` + `archetype.creator` from consulting and design-tool brands: asymmetric grid, oversized type, numbered sections, monochrome UI with one signal color. Reads as precise and opinionated; suits infrastructure, analytics and design-adjacent tools.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.neo-brutalist` + `archetype.outlaw` (for challenger tools aimed at small teams and creators): hard offset shadows, flat saturated color, blunt copy, visible grid. High recall against a sea of gradients. Risk: enterprise security and procurement reviewers may read it as unserious, and heavy borders strain dense data tables; keep the logged-in product, trust center and legal pages conventional.

## Metrics that matter

- Visitor-to-trial and visitor-to-demo conversion; pricing-page-to-checkout rate.
- Activation: share of new accounts reaching the first valuable action (and time to get there).
- Trial-to-paid conversion, net revenue retention and seat expansion.
- Pipeline sourced by the website; demo-to-opportunity rate.
- Support contacts per active account; help-center search success.
- Accessibility defects found in audits; security questionnaire turnaround time.
