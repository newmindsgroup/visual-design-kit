# Legal and Professional Services

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Corporate buyers (general counsel, CFOs, procurement):** shortlist firms for a matter, check relevant experience and named individuals, compare teams, rates and capabilities, and defend the choice internally.
- **Individuals in a life event:** divorce, injury, arrest, immigration, wills, a dispute with an employer. They are anxious, short of time and unsure which type of lawyer they need.
- **Founders and small businesses:** incorporation, contracts, tax, hiring; want a fixed price, a clear scope and fast replies.
- **Referrers:** other lawyers, accountants and bankers who recommend firms based on trust and speed of response.
- **Recruits:** graduates and lateral partners judging culture, mentoring, work mix and values.
- **Existing clients:** secure portals, matter status, invoices, document exchange.

## Trust signals

- Named partners with real photographs, bios, bar admissions and specialties; a team page that matches the practice.
- Matter experience stated precisely (type, sector, jurisdiction) and permitted by confidentiality rules; client logos only with consent.
- Credentials and directories (Chambers, Legal 500, Best Lawyers, professional-body memberships) shown with year and category, and only if current.
- Insights and thought leadership with real authors, dates and corrections; proof that the firm thinks before it sells.
- Plain-language fee information: fixed fees, rate ranges or scoping process; in the UK, mandatory price and service information for specified services.
- Secure, professional digital presence: working HTTPS, secure client portal, clear confidentiality statement, no stock-photo "legal" props.

## Category conventions

- Navy, charcoal, burgundy or forest green with a serif logotype (often a name list or a monogram) and a clean sans for UI.
- Photography of the city skyline, glass offices, books or hands on documents; individual headshots against a neutral background.
- Navigation: People, Services/Practices, Industries, Insights, About, Careers, Contact; practice-area landing pages with a team list.
- Footer carrying the legal disclaimer, regulatory status, office addresses and privacy policy.
- Pitch decks and proposals as dense, text-heavy documents; Word and PDF as native formats.

## Where to differentiate

- **Specificity over adjectives:** "We act for founders raising a seed round" beats "full-service law firm". Name the client type and the problem.
- **Legible pricing and process:** a three-step "what happens next", a fee estimator, a response-time promise.
- **Editorial rigor:** a real publication with strong typography, authors with faces and a distinct point of view, which doubles as the SEO engine.
- **People-first presentation:** warm, well-lit portraits and short first-person bios beat cropped stock-model photography.
- **Systemized documents:** branded engagement letters, reports and deal templates that clients see more than the website.
- **Client experience as brand:** a matter dashboard, plain-English summaries beside the legal text, and invoices that are readable.

## Category clichés

- Scales of justice, gavels, columns, courthouse steps, handshake close-ups, blurred skylines.
- Navy gradient hero with "Experienced. Trusted. Results." and a phone number in a button.
- Lorem-ipsum-grade copy: "committed to excellence", "tailored solutions", "client-centric".
- Founding-partner surnames set in a generic Times-like serif with an ampersand flourish.
- Dark-pattern "free consultation" pop-ups that obscure content on mobile.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Lawyer advertising (US):** ABA Model Rule 7.1 prohibits false or misleading communications about a lawyer or services; Rules 7.2 and 7.3 govern advertising, payment for referrals and solicitation. Each state bar adopts its own version, and several require labels, disclaimers (for example prior-results language) or firm filing of ads; rules differ on testimonials, specialist or "certified" claims, superlatives and firm names. State rules are being amended; verify the current rule text for each state in which the firm advertises.
- **Design effects:** results and testimonials need context and disclaimers placed near the claim, not buried in the footer; avoid "best" and "top" unless substantiated; keep ratings badges accurate; show jurisdictional admission statements on lawyer bios.
- **Confidentiality and intake:** contact forms and chat can create duties to prospective clients (ABA Model Rule 1.18). Add a pre-submit notice that sending a message does not create an attorney-client relationship, and avoid asking for confidential facts in the first form.
- **UK (SRA-regulated firms):** the SRA Transparency Rules require specified price and service information for defined services (for example residential conveyancing and probate), complaints information that includes the Legal Ombudsman, and display of the SRA digital badge and regulated status. Verify the current list of covered services.
- **Accounting, audit and consulting:** professional-body rules (for example the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct, ICAEW and other chartered bodies) bar false or misleading promotion; regulated titles (CPA, Chartered Accountant) are protected. Audit independence rules restrict some non-audit services for audit clients, which affects what a firm can promote to them.
- **Legal tech and self-help products:** unauthorized-practice-of-law rules and FTC action against "robot lawyer" claims (DoNotPay, 2024-25; verify outcome) make "AI lawyer" copy risky; separate legal information from legal advice in UI.
- **Privacy and security:** GDPR and state privacy laws, client-confidentiality duties, and client security questionnaires (ISO 27001 or SOC 2) affect portal design and data-room UX.
- **Accessibility:** firm sites are litigation targets under the ADA; aim for WCAG 2.2 AA, accessible PDFs and forms.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Identity: wordmark or logotype, color and type system, letterhead, business cards, email signature, presentation and report templates.
- Website: home, practice and industry pages, people directory with filters, bio template, insights hub and article template, careers, offices, contact.
- Pitch and credentials tools: matter database, tailored credentials deck builder, team CV generator.
- Client portal and intake: onboarding form, secure messaging, document upload, matter timeline, invoices.
- Recruitment: graduate site, event invitations, offer pack.
- Legal tech: guided self-service flows (forms, checklists), clear separation of information and advice.

## Voice and content

- Plain English first, legalese only where required; define terms, short paragraphs, active voice. Reading grade 9-11 for corporate readers, 6-8 for consumers.
- Lead with the client's problem and the outcome, then credentials.
- First-person bios in short sentences; avoid "renowned" and "leading" without evidence.
- Fees and timelines stated as ranges with assumptions; never "affordable" without a number.
- Insight headlines state the finding: "New rules on non-competes: what employers must change by June".

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.swiss-international` + `archetype.ruler` + `tier.premium`: grotesk or humanist sans with a refined serif for headings, strict grid, generous white space, restrained navy or ink with one accent. Reads as confident and orderly; differentiate through content.
- **Adjacent:** `style.classic-luxury-serif` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from private banking and wealth management, for private client, trusts and family-office practices serving `audience.affluent-hnwi`: high-contrast serif, ivory paper, quiet motion. Keep body text at 17-18px and avoid thin hairlines that fail contrast.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.scandinavian-nordic` + `archetype.caregiver` for family, employment and immigration law: daylight palette, soft natural photography, large readable type and a human tone. Risk: can undercut authority for contested or high-stakes corporate work, and approachable styling must still respect advertising rules on results and tone.

## Metrics that matter

- Qualified inquiries per month by practice; inquiry-to-instruction conversion rate; response time to first reply.
- Share of traffic and leads from insights content; newsletter growth and repeat readers.
- Pitch win rate for shortlisted opportunities; referral volume from partners.
- Client portal adoption and time to complete onboarding; invoice query rate.
- Recruitment: applications per vacancy, offer acceptance rate.
