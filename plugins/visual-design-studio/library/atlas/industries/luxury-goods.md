# Luxury Goods

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Established clients (HNWI):** receive recognition, personal service, access to limited pieces, discretion, and confidence in provenance and resale.
- **Aspirational buyers:** enter the brand through an accessible product (fragrance, small leather goods, entry watch) and be treated like a client, not a transaction.
- **Gift buyers:** find the right piece, wrapped and delivered flawlessly, with an understated way to check size or taste.
- **Collectors and investors:** references, serial numbers, auctions, authenticity, limited editions, condition reports.
- **Clients in a boutique or by appointment:** a continuous handoff between online, clienteling app and store.
- **Press, stylists and partners:** archive imagery, collections, craftsmanship stories.
- **Artisans and employees:** pride in craft; recruiting talent for ateliers and boutiques.

## Trust signals

- Heritage with proof: founding date, workshop location, named master craftspeople, archive images.
- Material and process transparency: what is used (gold purity, leather tannage, movement caliber), where and how it is made.
- Authentication: serial numbers, certificates, NFC or digital passports, official resale or care programs.
- White-glove service: personal advisors, complimentary repairs, gift wrapping, delivery by courier, flexible returns.
- Selective distribution: official boutiques and authorized dealers only; clear note on counterfeits.
- Quiet confidence: no discount banners, consistent pricing across channels, restrained copy.

## Category conventions

- White, black, ivory, or a signature house color (Hermès orange, Tiffany robin's-egg blue, Cartier red) on generous negative space.
- Serif or high-contrast Didone logotypes in uppercase, widely tracked; a refined sans for UI.
- Slow, large product photography on neutral backgrounds; campaign imagery by named photographers with a fashion-editorial feel.
- Minimal navigation (collections, world of the house, services, boutiques), no popups, chat concierge instead of a chatbot widget.
- "Book an appointment", "Contact an advisor", "Discover the savoir-faire"; price shown or "price on request" at the top end.
- Packaging as an experience: boxes, ribbons, tissue, hang tags, care cards, signature unboxing.

## Where to differentiate

- **Material truth over adjectives:** show the stitch, patina, tolerances; macro photography and process films replace "luxury" copy.
- **Service as interface:** clienteling app, WhatsApp or WeChat advisor, appointment booking, bespoke orders, repair tracking.
- **Digital craftsmanship:** page performance, typography, subtle motion and sound that feel like the product; speed is luxury.
- **Cultural patronage:** foundations, artist collaborations, exhibitions and publishing that extend the brand beyond product.
- **Pre-owned and repair programs:** authenticated resale and restoration reinforce longevity (verify claims).
- **Distinctive codes:** a pattern, a color, a shape, a sound, a gesture that is owned and consistent.
- **Selective irreverence:** playful campaigns or architecture-led stores, without sacrificing quality.

## Category clichés

- Black-and-gold everything; gold foil gradients as a quality signal.
- Script fonts with swirls; crests and laurels on new brands; "Since 20XX" fake heritage.
- Models staring away from camera on a marble backdrop; slow-motion perfume splash.
- Overused words: "timeless", "exquisite", "curated", "bespoke", "iconic" without substance.
- Cookie banners and chat bubbles that obscure hero imagery; low-contrast hairline serif text.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Material and composition claims:** the FTC Jewelry Guides (16 CFR Part 23) govern terms like "gold", "karat", "platinum", "diamond" and "lab-grown" (the 2018 revision removed "natural" from the definition of "diamond", so lab-grown stones are diamonds but must be disclosed as such); the Textile, Wool and Fur labeling laws, and rules on leather and "genuine" claims, apply to apparel and accessories.
- **Origin claims:** "Made in USA" is regulated by the FTC's Made in USA Labeling Rule (unqualified claims need "all or virtually all" US content); Swiss Made watches must meet Swiss legislation (the "Swissness" rules, including a 60% manufacturing-cost threshold and a Swiss movement; verify details); other countries have their own rules. Check before using origin in campaigns or on dials.
- **Exotic materials and fur:** CITES permits for crocodile, python and similar skins affect cross-border shipping and which markets can see the imagery; some jurisdictions ban fur sales (e.g., California). Many houses have dropped fur and exotics, so editorial imagery should match current policy.
- **Environmental claims:** EU Directive (EU) 2024/825 applies from 27 September 2026 and restricts generic claims such as "sustainable" and offset-based "carbon neutral" claims; the EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation bans destroying unsold apparel and footwear for large companies from 19 July 2026 (disclosure rules follow; verify). The FTC Green Guides and UK CMA Green Claims Code apply elsewhere.
- **Pricing and tax display:** EU and UK consumer prices include VAT; the Omnibus Directive's prior-price rules affect "was/now" displays. US prices exclude sales tax; duty and shipping must be shown before payment where required.
- **High-value transaction checks:** anti-money-laundering rules cover dealers in precious metals and stones and traders accepting large cash payments (EU AMLD; US Bank Secrecy Act for covered dealers). This shapes online high-value checkout, identity checks and "contact an advisor" flows.
- **Privacy:** client data is the core asset; GDPR, CCPA and China's PIPL govern clienteling, consent for personalized marketing and cross-border transfer. Keep consent screens quiet but unambiguous.
- **Advertising superlatives (China):** the Advertising Law restricts absolute terms like "best" and "highest"; localize copy rather than translate it (verify).
- **Accessibility:** the EAA covers EU e-commerce from June 2025; thin type, low contrast and heavy motion often fail WCAG 2.2 AA, so design elegant, accessible alternatives.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Identity: logotype, monogram or emblem, color codes (including signature color), typography, pattern, sonic and gesture codes.
- Packaging: boxes, bags, ribbons, tissue, hang tags, certificates and care guides; unboxing script.
- Campaign and editorial: seasonal campaign imagery, films, lookbooks, press kits, magazines.
- Digital: homepage, collection and product pages, appointment booking, clienteling app, private-sales portal, aftercare and repair tracking.
- Retail and events: boutique design, windows, signage, invitation suites, show sets.
- Corporate: annual report, heritage archive site, sustainability and craft reports.

## Voice and content

- Quiet, precise and sensory; describe materials, making and use before story. Short sentences, no exclamation marks.
- Third-person or a discreet "we"; avoid hype and urgency tactics ("only 2 left").
- Product copy uses specific facts: dimensions, materials, craftsmanship hours, origin.
- Service messages are personal and calm ("Your advisor will contact you within one working day").
- Localize with craftsmen of language, not machine translation; check honorifics and formality.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.classic-luxury-serif` + `archetype.ruler` + `tier.luxury`: ivory and black, high-contrast serif, wide tracking, slow imagery. Credible and legible to the category; differentiate through material truth and service.
- **Adjacent:** `style.quiet-minimal` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from Japanese craft and "quiet luxury" fashion (The Row, Loro Piana): muted palettes, tactile photography, no logo shouting. Works for `audience.affluent-hnwi` who avoid conspicuous branding; keep contrast at 4.5:1.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.neo-brutalist` + `archetype.outlaw`: raw grids, default-looking type, ironic copy (the Balenciaga and Vetements anti-luxury posture). Strong recall with younger fashion audiences. Risk: reads as cheap or ironic to traditional clients, depends on genuine product and cultural capital, and can turn controversial; protect service quality and keep checkout and care flows calm and conventional.

## Metrics that matter

- Appointment requests, store visits attributed to digital, advisor response time.
- Conversion at high order values; average order value; repeat purchase rate by client tier.
- Client lifetime value and retention; waitlist length for core pieces.
- Brand: unaided awareness among target clients, search for brand name, press and social share-of-voice.
- Operations: return rate, authenticity-dispute rate, repair turnaround time.

## Reference anchors

- Hermès for craft storytelling, window displays and a distinctive color code
- Cartier and Tiffany & Co. for signature color and jewelry packaging systems
- Chanel, Dior and Louis Vuitton for editorial campaigns and monograms
- Bottega Veneta and Loewe for art-led, restrained brand direction
- Loro Piana and The Row for quiet luxury
- Rolex and Patek Philippe for watch-industry restraint and archive culture
- Business of Fashion and Vogue Business for category analysis
