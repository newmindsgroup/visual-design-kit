# Energy and Climate Tech

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Households:** understand and lower the bill, switch supplier or tariff, install solar, a heat pump or a charger, and trust the installer and the savings number.
- **Small businesses and landlords:** predictable costs, tax and incentive eligibility, financing, maintenance.
- **Utilities and grid operators:** balance supply and demand, communicate outages, manage demand-response programs, meet regulators.
- **Developers and project finance:** assess sites, yields, permitting, interconnection and returns; need data rooms and credible models.
- **Corporate buyers (sustainability and procurement leads):** buy renewable power, offsets or removal credits and report emissions without greenwashing exposure.
- **Investors, regulators, communities:** evidence of impact, safety, land use and local benefit.
- **Field technicians and installers:** apps for commissioning, diagnostics and safety under real conditions.

## Trust signals

- Claims backed by method: lifecycle assessment basis, third-party verification, certification names (Energy Star, EPD, B Corp, Gold Standard or Verra for credits), each with date and scope.
- A real bill example and a "how we calculate your savings" page with assumptions and ranges, not a single optimistic number.
- Clear fuel-mix and source disclosure, tariff price per kWh with standing charges, exit fees and contract length before purchase.
- Installer credentials (MCS in the UK, NABCEP in the US), warranty terms, financing terms and customer references.
- Live system data: grid status, production, outages, charger availability and uptime.
- Safety and community: incident handling, land restoration, local jobs, noise and wildlife information.

## Category conventions

- Greens, sky blues and sun yellows; leaf, sun, drop, wind turbine and plug icons; rounded geometric sans.
- Hero photography of rooftops, turbines, fields and families under blue skies.
- Savings calculators, tariff comparison tables, usage graphs and monthly bill breakdowns.
- Installer quote funnels and postcode/address lookups at the top of the home page.
- Energy-use dashboards in apps: kWh by day, cost, comparison to similar homes, carbon avoided.
- Utilities: outage maps, payment and meter reading flows, long legal footers, emergency contacts.

## Where to differentiate

- **Evidence design:** charts with sources, uncertainty ranges, methodology drawers and downloadable data make claims credible.
- **Bill clarity:** a redesigned bill that explains charges in one glance is both service design and brand (Octopus Energy's bill and app design show the value).
- **Tone:** specific and calm rather than apocalyptic or smug; humor in low-stakes moments.
- **Color beyond green:** warm oranges, deep blues, black-and-white with a signal color; many leaders avoid the leaf.
- **Devices as brand:** meters, inverters, chargers and apps designed as consumer products with intuitive status lights.
- **Show the invisible:** real-time flows between solar, battery, home and grid; carbon intensity of the grid by hour; load-shifting prompts.
- **Place-based storytelling:** named communities, projects and workers rather than generic stock landscapes.

## Category clichés

- Green leaf wrapped around a lightbulb, globe in cupped hands, earth with a plug.
- Wind turbines on rolling green hills at sunset; solar panels with lens flare.
- "Powering a sustainable future" and "net zero by 2050" with no interim targets.
- Unqualified "eco-friendly", "green", "clean" and "carbon neutral" badges.
- Gradient-glow futuristic dashboards that hide the units and the baseline.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **FTC Green Guides (US, 16 CFR Part 260):** the 2012 guides require substantiation and qualification of environmental claims, discourage unqualified "eco-friendly" or "green", and set rules for carbon offset, recyclable and renewable-energy claims. The FTC opened a review in December 2022 and, no final revision had been confirmed when this entry was written (October 2026); verify on ftc.gov.
- **EU Directive (EU) 2024/825 (Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition):** member states had to transpose by 27 March 2026 and the rules apply from 27 September 2026. It restricts generic environmental claims (such as "climate friendly" or "eco") without recognized excellent environmental performance, bans claims that a product is climate neutral or has reduced impact based on offsetting, and limits sustainability labels to those based on certification schemes or set by public authorities.
- **EU Green Claims Directive:** a 2023 Commission proposal on substantiating explicit environmental claims. Its adoption was uncertain through 2025-2026; verify current status before relying on it.
- **UK:** the CMA Green Claims Code and the DMCC Act 2024 (direct fining powers from April 2025), plus ASA and CAP Code rules on environmental claims.
- **California AB 1305:** requires disclosures for voluntary carbon offset and net-zero claims made in the state (verify scope and any amendments).
- **Energy labelling and marks:** EU energy label (A to G), FTC EnergyGuide labels for appliances, and ENERGY STAR (a licensed mark). Use official artwork and rules only.
- **Utility and retail energy disclosure:** price per kWh, standing charges, contract terms, exit fees and fuel mix are disclosed in prescribed formats by state public utility commissions (US) and Ofgem (UK); verify per market.
- **Incentive and savings claims:** federal and local incentives changed in 2025 legislation in the US; verify eligibility and end dates before publishing "save X%" or "30% credit" claims.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 AA for bill-pay, outage and comparison tools; the EAA covers e-commerce in the EU from June 2025.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Brand: identity with a non-leaf symbol, color system, iconography for energy flows, photography direction, installer livery.
- Website: home with address/postcode entry, product pages (solar, battery, heat pump, charger, tariff), savings calculator, how-it-works, evidence and methodology, help center.
- App: onboarding and meter connection, live energy flow, usage history, bills and payments, tariff controls, outage reporting, charger map and session start.
- Quote and design tool for installers; commissioning app for field teams.
- Reports: impact and sustainability report, disclosure tables, data portal, carbon accounting dashboards.
- Hardware: label and indicator light design, quick start guides, packaging.

## Voice and content

- Quantify with units and baselines: "Cuts grid use by about 60% in a typical 3-bed home (assumptions here)".
- Explain jargon once (kW vs kWh, standing charge, PPA, interconnection); use the same names in bills, app and support.
- Say what is known and what is estimated; give ranges and date the data.
- Replace moral pressure with practical action: "Run the dishwasher after 9 pm to use cheaper power".
- Avoid absolutes ("zero emissions", "100% green") unless the claim is defined and certified.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.eco-natural-sustainable` + `archetype.caregiver` + `tier.mid-market`: natural palette, rounded sans, calm photography of homes and communities. Familiar and safe; stand out with evidence and a clear bill.
- **Adjacent:** `style.data-dense-utilitarian` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from trading, transport and open-data tools (Electricity Maps, grid dashboards): grid-based charts, mono numerals, restrained color. Credible for commercial, industrial and developer audiences.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.seventies-psychedelic-groovy` + `archetype.explorer`: warm sunburst palette, rounded retro type and optimistic imagery to make energy feel human and fun (compare Octopus Energy's irreverent tone). Risk: retro playfulness can seem unserious beside large purchases like solar loans, and it draws scrutiny to claims; pair it with sober, sourced evidence pages and plain contract terms.

## Metrics that matter

- Quote-to-install conversion, time from enquiry to commissioning, install defects.
- Bill comprehension (support contacts about bills per 1,000 customers), payment-failure recovery.
- App engagement with load-shifting prompts and measured energy or cost reduction per household.
- Charger uptime and session success rate; outage-communication accuracy.
- Claim integrity: number of claims with a published method; regulator complaints (target zero).
