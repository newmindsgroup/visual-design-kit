# Restaurants and Food Service

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Diners deciding where to eat:** check menu, prices, photos, hours, location, dietary options and availability within a minute, usually on a phone.
- **Reservation seekers:** find a table for a date and party size, understand deposits and cancellation terms, get reminders.
- **Takeout and delivery customers:** order quickly, customize items, see real wait times, pay, track and reorder.
- **Regulars and loyalty members:** collect rewards, repeat favorites, get offers without spam.
- **People with allergies or dietary needs:** find reliable allergen information and know the kitchen can handle requests.
- **Staff and operators:** run service from POS and kitchen displays, update menus, 86 items and read signage in a busy room.
- **Event and catering clients:** compare packages, get quotes and manage logistics.

## Trust signals

- Accurate, current menu and prices in text; photos of real dishes; opening hours and holiday exceptions.
- Food-safety visibility: inspection grade where posted, clean and recognizable premises, allergen guidance on menu and site.
- Social proof: reviews, press mentions, chef or owner story, awards used with date and source.
- Clear fees and policies: service charge, delivery fees, deposit and cancellation rules, tipping prompts that are not manipulative.
- Reliable ordering: confirmation with ready time, live status and fast resolution for mistakes.
- Provenance: farms, suppliers, house-made claims that are true.

## Category conventions

- Website: hero photo, menu, reservations or order button, location and hours, about, gallery, gift cards, catering and events.
- Menus: sections by course, short descriptions, prices without currency symbols in fine dining and with them in casual, icons for dietary tags (V, VG, GF).
- Booking widgets from third parties (OpenTable, Resy, Tock) embedded; ordering via native or third-party delivery apps.
- Signage and menu boards with large type, high contrast and limited items; table QR codes.
- Loyalty apps, digital punch cards and personalized offers; social media with food photography and short video.
- Interiors, uniforms, packaging, cups and bags that echo the logo, color and typography.

## Where to differentiate

- **A point of view in the menu:** typography, layout, voice and descriptions that reflect the kitchen; menu design is the most-read brand asset.
- **Photography that tastes real:** natural light, honest portions and consistent styling; avoid overstyled stock.
- **Atmosphere online:** sound, texture and motion that evoke the room, without delaying the "menu and book" path.
- **Ordering that feels like hospitality:** friendly microcopy, accurate wait estimates and a no-dark-pattern tip step.
- **Physical-digital consistency:** cup, bag, receipt, signage, uniform and app speak the same language.
- **Community and local identity:** neighborhood references, collaborations and events that are not generic.

## Category clichés

- Chalkboard lettering, Edison bulbs, reclaimed wood and "farm to table" claims for every kind of restaurant.
- Overhead flat-lay photos of burgers, avocado toast and cocktails on marble.
- PDF menus that require pinching and zooming, or image-only menus.
- Stock photos of chefs in kitchens and generic cutlery icons.
- "Fresh, local, handcrafted" copy repeated without specifics.
- Fix: put the actual menu in accessible HTML, name suppliers and dishes, and design a recognizable look from type, color and plating style.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; rules for restaurants vary by city and state as well as country.

- **Menu labeling (US):** FDA rules require chain restaurants with 20 or more locations to show calories on menus, menu boards and online ordering points of selection, with other nutrition on request; states and cities add rules, such as New York City's sodium warning icon for certain chain items. Place these without crowding the dish name, and keep type legible.
- **Allergen information:** US federal allergen labeling applies to packaged food, not restaurant meals, but states and cities add rules and liability follows from mistakes; the UK and EU require information on 14 allergens for non-prepacked food (written or signposted to staff) and UK Natasha's Law applies to food prepacked for direct sale. Design allergen matrices, icons and "ask your server" notices to be clear and consistent.
- **Calorie labeling (UK):** large out-of-home businesses must display calories on menus; design menu layouts to fit them.
- **Pricing and fees:** California's Honest Pricing Law requires all-in advertised prices in many sectors and has specific provisions for restaurant service charges (verify the current text); UK drip-pricing rules under the DMCC Act push the same way. Show service charges, delivery fees and gratuity policies before payment.
- **Alcohol:** state alcohol control rules, license display requirements and age verification apply to bars and delivery; promotions are restricted in many places, and responsible-service messaging is standard.
- **Delivery platforms and orders:** commission caps, fee transparency and data-sharing rules exist in some cities; online ordering terms must state refund and substitution policies.
- **Deposits and cancellations:** reservation deposits and no-show fees must be disclosed clearly before payment under consumer protection law.
- **Messaging and loyalty:** TCPA for SMS waitlist and offers, CAN-SPAM, GDPR/CCPA for loyalty and reservation data; PCI DSS for card payments (use the provider's hosted fields).
- **Accessibility:** ADA Title III suits target restaurant websites, PDF menus and ordering apps; the European Accessibility Act covers e-commerce ordering for EU consumers. Provide menus as real text, with alt text, keyboard-operable forms and large-print or accessible alternatives in the room.
- **Health inspection display:** some cities require posting inspection grades or certificates; leave space in the entrance signage system.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Website or landing page: menu, reservations, order online, locations and hours, private events, gift cards, press, jobs.
- Ordering and app: menu browse, item customizer, cart, pickup or delivery scheduling, payment, order status, reorder, loyalty and account.
- Reservations and waitlist: availability picker, party size, special requests, deposit, confirmation and reminders.
- Menu and print: dining menu, bar list, takeaway menu, specials card, kids menu, allergen guide, large-print version.
- Signage and environment: exterior sign, menu boards, table cards, wayfinding, restroom signage, staff uniforms, packaging (cups, boxes, bags, labels).
- Brand: logo and secondary marks, type and color, pattern or illustration, photography style, voice, social templates.

## Voice and content

- Appetite words and plain description: ingredients, method, portion; no overwriting.
- Friendly, host-like tone; the voice should sound like the room.
- Prices consistent and unambiguous; dietary and allergen flags simple and standardized.
- Order and booking confirmations state time, location, contact and what happens next.
- Error messages for sold-out items or fully booked nights offer alternatives (nearby times, waitlist).

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.vintage-heritage-americana` + `archetype.everyperson` + `tier.mid-market`: slab or script wordmarks, warm palette, enamel-sign and paper-bag detailing, honest photography. Familiar and friendly; differentiate through a specific location story and menu typography.
- **Adjacent:** `style.art-nouveau-ornamental` + `archetype.lover`, borrowed from Parisian brasseries, patisserie and cocktail bars: flowing lines, botanical ornament, rich color, ornamented menus. Signals romance and craft for bistros, bakeries and cocktail bars.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.neo-brutalist` + `archetype.jester` (for fast-casual, delivery-first and ghost-kitchen brands): flat saturated color, thick outlines, oversized type and blunt jokes, ordering as the hero. Very distinctive in a delivery-app grid. Risk: legibility and mood mismatch for sit-down or premium dining; keep menus, allergen information and checkout simple and high contrast.

## Metrics that matter

- Online order conversion, average order value, repeat order rate and loyalty enrollment.
- Reservation fill rate, no-show rate and deposit acceptance.
- Menu page views to orders or bookings; time to find the menu on mobile.
- Delivery: order accuracy, delivery time, rating and refund rate.
- Table turn time and kitchen display speed where design affects service.
- Review ratings and sentiment; accessibility audit results.

## Reference anchors

- Sweetgreen and Shake Shack for brand-led digital ordering and consistent in-store/app identities
- Starbucks for app, loyalty and packaging systems at scale
- Pret a Manger and Wagamama (UK) for consistent casual-dining brand systems and menu design
- Noma and Eleven Madison Park for menu and identity design in fine dining
- OpenTable, Resy and Toast for reservations and POS interface conventions
- *Menu Design in America* (Steven Heller and Jim Heimann, Taschen) for historical menu typography and illustration
