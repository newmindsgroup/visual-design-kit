# Kids and Family

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Ages 0-3:** no reading, no fine motor precision. A caregiver operates the product with the child on their lap. Design for the adult's hands and the child's eyes and ears.
- **Ages 4-6:** pre-readers. They navigate by icon, color, sound and voice prompts, tap targets of 64px or more, and tolerate few steps (2-3 taps to any activity).
- **Ages 7-9:** early readers who collect, build, finish and show off. Progress and ownership matter more than rewards.
- **Ages 10-12:** reject anything that looks "babyish". They want autonomy, identity, friends and tools that feel real; design closer to teen products, with parent controls kept visible.
- **Parents and caregivers (the buyer and gatekeeper):** is it safe, is it worth the screen time or money, will it frustrate or embarrass me, can I control it in two taps, will my child still like it next week.
- **Gift givers and educators:** age fit at a glance, no setup, clear learning or play value.
- **Hard moments:** bedtime, car rides, waiting rooms, meltdowns. A parent holding a toddler needs a one-handed, interruptible product.

## Trust signals

- Age range with the reason ("Ages 3+: small parts") on pack, product page and app store listing; honest developmental claims.
- Safety conformity stated plainly for physical goods: US ASTM F963 and CPSIA, EU EN 71 with CE marking, UK UKCA/CE. Show marks only if the product is certified.
- Privacy in one screen: what is collected, "no ads, no third-party trackers" when true, a parent dashboard to view and delete data.
- A parental gate in front of purchases, external links, settings and social features; Apple's Kids Category rules require this for links out and purchases.
- Independent validation used with permission: kidSAFE or PRIVO seals, Common Sense Media ratings, educator or pediatric advisor names with credentials.
- Real photography of the product at true scale with hands for size reference; battery, material and cleaning information.

## Category conventions

- Saturated primaries and secondaries, rounded letterforms (Fredoka, Baloo 2, Nunito; all OFL), 20px+ body text for anything a child reads, 28px+ for headings.
- Characters and mascots carry the brand; packaging is a character-led window box with an age burst top-right.
- Child-facing and parent-facing layers are separated: a playful front, a plainer "grown-ups" area behind a gate.
- Sound, narration and animated feedback on every tap; stars, stickers and badges as rewards.
- Retail: shelf-blocking color, character faces at child eye level; online: lifestyle photo of a child playing plus a "what's in the box" flat lay.

## Where to differentiate

- **Design the two audiences deliberately.** A calm, premium parent layer (restrained palette, clear type) wrapped around an expressive child layer reads as more trustworthy than loud-everywhere.
- **Open-ended play over reward loops.** No streak shaming, no countdown timers, and designed stopping points ("All done. See you tomorrow.").
- **Sound and touch as brand.** Voice talent, a signature chime, material feel and weight of the object (Tonies, Yoto, Toca Boca show the range).
- **Original worlds rather than licensed IP** with illustrated or handmade qualities that survive across book, toy, app and film.
- **Representation that is specific:** skin tones, abilities, hair, family structures (single parents, two dads, grandparent carers), shown doing ordinary things.
- **Child-made output on show:** drawings, songs and builds saved and displayed back to the child.

## Category clichés

- Confetti, rainbow and starburst on every surface; gendered pink/blue splits.
- Comic Sans-like faces and wobbly "kiddie" fonts that are hard to read for early readers.
- Stock photos of a child laughing at a tablet; googly-eyed mascots with no personality or story.
- "Learning through play" claims with no mechanism; sparkles-and-stars gamification.
- The beige "premium baby" palette (oatmeal, sage, terracotta) now repeated across the category.
- AI-generated children's illustrations with malformed hands and inconsistent characters.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **COPPA (US):** services directed to children under 13, or with actual knowledge of under-13 users, need verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information, including persistent identifiers. The FTC's amended rule was published 22 April 2025, took effect 23 June 2025 and has a 22 April 2026 compliance date for most provisions (verify the carve-outs); it adds separate consent for disclosing data to third parties, written retention limits and security programs. Design effects: neutral age screens that do not hint at the "right" answer, consent flows, no behavioral ad SDKs in child mode.
- **UK Age Appropriate Design Code (ICO Children's Code):** 15 standards, including best interests of the child, high-privacy defaults, no nudging children to weaken privacy, geolocation off by default, profiling off by default, and child-readable privacy information.
- **GDPR Article 8 and EU DSA Article 28:** parental consent below the member-state digital-consent age (13-16); platforms accessible to minors must provide a high level of privacy and safety and cannot show profiling-based ads to known minors.
- **US state laws:** California's Age-Appropriate Design Code is enacted but largely enjoined in litigation (NetChoice v. Bonta); other states are passing age-assurance and minors' privacy laws. Verify current status per state.
- **CARU guidelines (BBB National Programs):** ads directed to children must be clearly separated from content, avoid pressuring or "ask your parents" tactics, and disclose sponsorship in games, videos and influencer content. Design effects: labeled ad breaks, no in-content character selling.
- **Store policies:** Apple Kids Category (parental gates, restricted analytics and advertising) and Google Play Families policy are stricter than law in some respects and decide whether the app ships.
- **Physical goods:** CPSIA lead, phthalate and tracking-label rules, small-parts and choking-hazard cautionary statements on packaging (US Child Safety Protection Act), EU toy safety rules (verify the current instrument). Warning text has minimum legibility and placement rules that constrain pack layout.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 AA is the baseline for parent-facing surfaces; EAA covers e-commerce in the EU since June 2025.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Character and world bible: model sheets, expressions, voice, sound palette, do and don't poses.
- Child app: first run (parent-led), home world, activity, save/show, stop screen, parental gate, grown-up dashboard, subscription screens behind the gate.
- Parent site: product and age finder, safety and privacy page, how it supports learning, retailers, press kit.
- Packaging system: front window and age burst, back-of-pack play value, safety cautions, QR to parent resources (no child data collection).
- Retail and e-commerce: shelf display, product page with scale shots, gift guides by age.

## Voice and content

- Child-facing: one instruction per screen, verb first, 1-6 words, reading grade 1-3, narrated for pre-readers; "Try again" never "Wrong".
- Parent-facing: plain, specific, grade 6-8; say what the child will do and learn, in minutes, without fear or guilt marketing.
- Names pronounceable by a four-year-old; avoid adult puns as the product name.
- Safety and privacy copy is concrete ("We do not show ads or sell data"), never "your privacy is important to us".

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.playful-bubbly` + `archetype.innocent` + `tier.mid-market` for `audience.children-under-13`: rounded type, saturated palette, big targets, mascot-led. Credible fast; differentiate on sound, characters and a calm parent layer.
- **Adjacent:** `style.handcrafted-organic` + `archetype.caregiver`, borrowed from wooden-toy, Montessori and children's publishing: natural materials, hand-drawn texture, muted-but-warm color. Parents read it as safe and slow; keep contrast above 4.5:1 for text.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.risograph-print-texture` + `archetype.creator`: two- or three-ink overprint, grainy shapes, art-school energy aimed at design-literate parents and kids who make things. Risk: low-contrast pastel inks and texture hurt early-reader legibility, and it can read as "for adults"; keep child-facing text on solid fields at 7:1.

## Metrics that matter

- Activation: first completed activity within 24 hours; parent setup completion rate.
- Healthy engagement: sessions ending at the designed stop point, not time-in-app maximization.
- Parent trust: dashboard opt-in rate, privacy-page views, accidental-purchase refunds and support tickets.
- Retail and e-commerce: sell-through, return rate with reason "wrong age", review score from parents.
- Safety and quality: incident reports, complaints about content or ads, store-policy rejections.
