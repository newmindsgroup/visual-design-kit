# Food, Beverage and CPG

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Shoppers at the shelf:** decide in two or three seconds among dozens of products; look for familiar brands, price, flavor, dietary fit and cues of quality.
- **Online grocery and marketplace buyers:** judge from a thumbnail and a short title; need accurate images, size, ingredients and allergens in text.
- **Label readers:** parents, people with allergies or conditions, athletes and ethical shoppers who read ingredients, nutrition and claims.
- **Gift and occasion buyers:** want packaging that signals taste, care and suitability.
- **Retail buyers and category managers:** judge shelf presence, velocity potential, pack efficiency and sustainability.
- **Fans and communities:** engage via social, recipes, limited editions and merch.

## Trust signals

- Clear, legible ingredient list and nutrition panel; allergen statements in the required form and place.
- Recognized certifications used correctly (organic, Fairtrade, non-GMO verification, kosher, halal, vegan) with the official mark and scope.
- Provenance and process: where ingredients come from, how it is made, expiry and storage guidance.
- Honest portrait of the product: the photographed food or drink matches what is inside; serving suggestions labeled as such.
- Brand heritage or founder story that is true, plus consistent quality and recall transparency.
- Retail presence and reviews; price consistency across channels.

## Category conventions

- Front of pack: brand mark, product name, variant color-coding, flavor or hero ingredient image, net quantity, claims bursts ("Non-GMO", "High protein").
- Back or side: ingredients, nutrition facts, allergen statement, barcode, storage, company address, recycling marks.
- Color coding by flavor or variant; a consistent brand block for shelf impact; window or die-cut reveals; appetite photography with steam, droplets and condensation.
- Direct-to-consumer sites, retailer pages and subscription boxes alongside retail distribution.
- Seasonal and limited editions, collaborations, multipacks and promotional flashes.
- Alcohol and spirits: heritage cues, serif labels, embossing, foil, bottle shapes and age statements; low- and no-alcohol lines mimic spirits or soda.

## Where to differentiate

- **Shelf-first design:** test in a real shelf mock-up and at thumbnail size; strong silhouette, a distinctive color block, and one clear hero element beat many small details.
- **Voice on pack:** copy that has a personality and a point of view turns the pack into media, if mandatory text still reads cleanly.
- **Structural design:** shape, closures, texture and sustainability (refill, mono-material) as part of the brand.
- **A system across variants:** a modular layout that scales to new flavors while remaining collectible.
- **Honest sensory cues:** typography, color and illustration that communicate taste and texture without photographic clichés.
- **Digital continuity:** QR and web experiences that deliver recipes, sourcing and traceability, not only promotions.

## Category clichés

- Kraft-paper-and-twine "artisan" look on factory products; hand-drawn script for everything natural.
- Fake farm scenes, wooden barns and sunbeam rays; leafy green badges with no evidence.
- Splash photography: liquid crowns, flying ingredients and floating fruit on gradient backgrounds.
- "Natural", "wholesome", "real" claims with no definition, and green coloring for sustainability by default.
- Starburst "NEW!" and "50% more!" flashes on every pack; cartoon mascots without a story.
- Fix: be specific about ingredients and process, keep one distinctive visual idea, and treat mandatory labeling as part of the layout.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; food labeling is tightly regulated and differs by market.

- **US FDA food labeling:** the Nutrition Facts panel format, ingredient order by weight, net quantity statement on the principal display panel (Fair Packaging and Labeling Act), and allergen declaration for the major allergens (milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy, plus sesame since 2023) affect type size, placement and contrast. The FDA's updated "healthy" claim rule has a 2028 compliance date and a front-of-package Nutrition Info box has been proposed; verify current status. "Natural" has no formal FDA definition, but claims must not mislead.
- **USDA and other US rules:** meat and poultry labels are reviewed by USDA FSIS; the National Organic Program controls use of the organic seal; the Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard governs disclosure (text, symbol or QR). California Prop 65 may require warnings.
- **EU and UK:** Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 requires emphasis of 14 allergens in the ingredient list, minimum character height for mandatory information, nutrition per 100 g or 100 ml and origin labeling for some foods; Regulation (EC) 1924/2006 limits nutrition and health claims to an authorized list; organic products use the EU organic logo. UK Natasha's Law requires full allergen labeling on food pre-packed for direct sale. The UK restricts advertising of less-healthy food and drink: a 9pm TV watershed and a ban on paid online ads, in force since January 2026.
- **Alcohol:** in the US, TTB label approval (COLA), the government health warning and strict class and alcohol-content statements apply; trade codes and state rules limit imagery that appeals to minors and audience composition for ads. UK Portman Group and ASA/CAP codes, and France's Loi Evin, restrict content, tone and associations; age-gating and responsible-drinking messages are standard.
- **Advertising to children:** self-regulatory programs (CARU and the Children's Food and Beverage Advertising Initiative in the US) and national rules limit mascots, premiums and claims aimed at children; COPPA applies to apps and promotions.
- **Sustainability and packaging claims:** FTC Green Guides and state laws (for example California's rules on recyclability and the chasing-arrows symbol); EU Directive 2024/825 restricts generic environmental claims from September 2026; the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation brings labeling and recyclability rules from 2026 (verify dates).
- **Online selling:** accurate ingredient, allergen and nutrition text on product pages; ROSCA for subscriptions; privacy law for loyalty programs.
- **Accessibility:** ADA and the European Accessibility Act apply to shop sites; pack accessibility benefits from minimum type sizes, strong contrast and tactile or high-contrast cues.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Pack system: primary label or carton, variant architecture, size and multipack formats, regulatory panel layout, barcode and recycling marks, dielines and print specifications.
- Shelf and retail: shelf-ready displays, point-of-sale, secondary packaging, planogram thumbnails, retailer listing images (front, back, lifestyle, nutrition).
- Digital: brand site or DTC shop, recipe and product pages, QR landing pages, email, social content system, retailer media assets.
- Campaign: hero film or photography, packaging reveal, seasonal and limited-edition kits, influencer and sampling materials.
- Brand: logo and brand block, type, color by variant, illustration or photography direction, tone of voice, naming system.

## Voice and content

- Short, flavorful and specific; name the ingredient and the taste ("roasted hazelnuts, dark cocoa, pinch of sea salt").
- Humor and irreverence work on pack when mandatory information remains clear.
- Claims are literal and substantiated; avoid health claims not permitted for the market.
- Allergen and safety text is plain and never styled to hide.
- For alcohol, pair brand voice with responsible-drinking language and avoid implying performance or social success.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.handcrafted-organic` + `archetype.everyperson` + `tier.value` (or mid-market): warm natural palette, friendly hand lettering, honest photography, simple structure. Reads as approachable and wholesome; differentiate through a specific illustration or color idea.
- **Adjacent:** `style.art-deco` + `archetype.lover`, borrowed from spirits and cocktail culture for premium non-alcoholic drinks, chocolate and mixers: metallic accents, geometric symmetry, deep colors. Signals occasion and indulgence.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.grunge-punk-zine` + `archetype.outlaw` (for beverages or snacks targeting `audience.teens-gen-z`): rebellious typography, heavy contrast and irreverent copy that treats water, snacks or oat drinks like a metal band. Very high recall. Risk: alienates some retailers and parents, ages quickly, and mandatory panels still need legible, calm treatment; test with shoppers and retail buyers.

## Metrics that matter

- Shelf standout and findability in eye-tracking or shelf tests; time to find the product.
- Velocity and rate of sale per store; trial and repeat rates.
- Online: listing image click-through, conversion on retailer pages, rating and review volume.
- Pack comprehension: correct identification of flavor, allergens and key claims in testing.
- Compliance: label rework, delisting or complaint events.
- Sustainability and cost: pack weight, recyclability rate, material cost per unit.
