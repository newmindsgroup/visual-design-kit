# Crypto and Web3

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **First-time buyers:** buy a small amount safely, understand fees and risk, not get scammed or locked out.
- **Active traders:** fast order entry, deep order books, charts, margin and derivatives controls, accurate fills and tax exports.
- **Self-custody users:** create and back up a wallet, sign transactions, approve contracts and revoke permissions with confidence.
- **DeFi and protocol users:** swap, lend, stake and bridge while comparing yield, risk and gas costs across chains.
- **Builders and DAOs:** read docs, deploy contracts, govern treasuries, coordinate contributors and votes.
- **Collectors and communities:** mint, trade or display digital items and belong to a culture around them.
- **Institutions:** custody, settlement, reporting and compliance with auditability.

## Trust signals

- Clear regulatory status and licenses for each jurisdiction served, plainly stated, not implied.
- Proof of reserves or audited statements for custodians; smart-contract audit reports with scope, date and unresolved findings.
- Self-custody clarity: who holds the keys, what recovery means, what the company can and cannot do.
- Transaction transparency: fees, slippage, network costs, price impact and final amount shown before signing.
- Security UX: hardware wallet support, address allow-listing, anti-phishing codes, clear transaction decoding instead of hex.
- Open-source code, public governance records and verifiable on-chain addresses; named team and investors.
- Honest risk language: loss of value, irreversible transfers and scam warnings in the flow where they matter.

## Category conventions

- Dark UI with neon accents and gradients; candlestick charts, tickers, token icons and green/red price changes.
- Wallet-connect buttons, network switchers, approval dialogs and gas fee selectors.
- Landing pages with floating 3D coins, chrome objects, glowing grids and "to the moon" energy.
- Roadmaps, tokenomics pie charts and community links (Discord, X, Telegram) in the header.
- Abbreviated addresses (0x1234...abcd) with copy buttons and block-explorer links.
- Exchange and wallet apps modeled on fintech patterns: balance first, buy/sell/convert actions, activity feed.

## Where to differentiate

- **Say less, show more:** replace hype with a clear explanation of what the product does, who it is for and what can go wrong.
- **Human-readable transactions:** translate signing requests into plain sentences ("You are allowing this app to spend up to 500 USDC"), with a warning for unlimited approvals.
- **Safety as brand:** invest in recovery flows, scam warnings, address checks and test transactions.
- **Craft of numbers:** precise decimals, unit handling, tabular figures and consistent rounding across charts, tables and receipts.
- **Culture with discipline:** community-led brands can be playful, but keep trading, custody and legal screens calm and conventional.
- **Beyond the coin motif:** build identity from type, pattern, color and motion rather than coin and chain imagery.

## Category clichés

- Bitcoin, Ethereum and coin symbols as decoration; gold coins raining, rockets, moons and laser eyes.
- Neon purple-blue-teal gradients with glowing grid floors and chrome 3D shapes.
- Hooded hackers, padlocks, chain-link icons and abstract blockchain cubes.
- Cartoon apes, pixelated avatars and generic "metaverse" 3D worlds without a product behind them.
- "Own your future", "Join the revolution", "Financial freedom" headlines; APY numbers without risk context.
- Fix: lead with the user task and the real cost; use gradients only where they encode data, and show risk where it applies.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; crypto rules differ widely by country and asset type and change often.

- **US securities and commodities law:** whether a token is a security (the SEC applies the Howey test) affects how it can be marketed and to whom; SEC and CFTC enforcement actions treat misleading performance claims, undisclosed paid promotion and unregistered offerings seriously. Avoid yield, returns or "guaranteed" language in UI copy unless counsel approves it, and treat endorsements by influencers under the FTC Endorsement Guides and securities antifraud rules.
- **Money transmission and AML:** FinCEN registration, state money-transmitter licenses (and New York's BitLicense) and the travel rule drive KYC and onboarding flows, address screening prompts and transaction holds; design them as understandable steps with status, not dead ends.
- **Stablecoin and "insured" claims:** US stablecoin legislation (the GENIUS Act, 2025) and bank-advertising rules restrict implying that digital assets are government-backed or FDIC-insured; keep "not insured, may lose value" statements prominent and verify the implementing rules.
- **EU MiCA (Regulation 2023/1114):** crypto-asset service providers need authorization, issuers publish white papers, and marketing communications must be identifiable, fair, clear and not misleading; design disclaimers and white-paper links accordingly.
- **UK FCA financial promotions regime (since October 2023):** promotions need FCA authorization or approval, carry prescribed risk warnings, include a cooling-off period for first-time investors and ban referral incentives; these shape sign-up screens, ads and bonus mechanics.
- **Other regimes:** Singapore restricts public advertising of crypto services; several markets limit promotion to retail audiences. Check the target country before any campaign.
- **Privacy:** GDPR, CCPA and the visibility of on-chain data; avoid dark patterns for consent and notifications.
- **Accessibility:** ADA and the European Accessibility Act apply to exchanges, wallets and checkout flows; do not rely on red/green alone for gains and losses.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Marketing site: home, product pages, security and proof of reserves, fees, learn center, legal and licenses, status, careers.
- Onboarding: account creation, KYC and identity verification, funding, first buy with fee preview, recovery phrase or passkey setup and test.
- Trading and portfolio: balance, assets, price charts, order tickets, open orders, history, tax reports, alerts.
- Wallet: send/receive with address verification, token approvals, network switching, NFT gallery, activity decoded in plain language, connected apps and revocation.
- DeFi and DAO: swap, pool and staking screens with APY and risk, bridge flows, proposal list and voting, treasury dashboards.
- Brand: logo that works as token icon at 16-32px, color system for light and dark, motion for transaction states, community assets.

## Voice and content

- Plain, direct and unhyped; explain terms (gas, slippage, seed phrase) on first use.
- Warnings are specific and actionable: "Transfers cannot be reversed. Check the first and last 6 characters of the address."
- Show both token and fiat amounts; state time to finality and fees before confirmation.
- Avoid financial advice language unless licensed; replace "buy now" urgency with neutral action labels.
- Community voice may be irreverent in social channels; keep product and support copy sober.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.y2k-cyber` + `archetype.outlaw` + `tier.premium` (for `audience.teens-gen-z` and retail traders): dark surfaces, chrome and neon accents, bold type. Instantly legible as crypto; keep trading screens clean and risk language prominent.
- **Adjacent:** `style.quiet-minimal` + `archetype.ruler`, borrowed from private banking and custody firms: restrained palette, serif or refined grotesque, calm motion. Signals institutional safety for custody, treasury and exchange products.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.civic-plain` + `archetype.sage`: public-service plainness, high contrast, short sentences, no hype imagery, risk and fee tables as the main content. Stands out in a noisy category and fits the post-MiCA era. Risk: loses community energy and may feel dull to speculative users; add cultural expression through community channels and limited editorial moments.

## Metrics that matter

- KYC completion rate and time to first funded account; first purchase success.
- Failed or abandoned transactions, support contacts about stuck or wrong-network transfers.
- Phishing and scam reports, account takeover rate, share of users with 2FA or passkeys and verified backups.
- Retention beyond the first month; share of users who understand fees (survey or quiz proxy).
- Regulatory and advertising review outcomes; app store review scores.
