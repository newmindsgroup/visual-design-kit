# Gaming and Esports

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Core players:** want mastery, fast feedback, fair competition, customization and a game that respects their time and skill.
- **Casual and mobile players:** pick up in short sessions, understand the game in under a minute, progress without frustration.
- **Competitors and teams:** track stats, schedules, rankings and brackets; communicate clearly in match; grow a personal brand.
- **Viewers and community:** watch live, follow teams, join Discord, collect cosmetics, share clips.
- **Creators and streamers:** overlays, assets, alerts, sponsorship deliverables, brand-safe usage rights.
- **Parents of young players:** age ratings, spend controls, chat settings, screen time.
- **Developers and modders:** documentation, tooling, store pages, patch notes and community feedback loops.
- **Sponsors and publishers:** brand fit, audience data, tournament packages.

## Trust signals

- Official age rating and content descriptors (ESRB, PEGI, USK, IARC-issued ratings) shown on store pages, trailers and packaging.
- Honest monetization: price in real currency, odds for random items before purchase, no pay-to-win in competitive modes.
- Fair-play infrastructure: anti-cheat statement, ban transparency, moderation and reporting tools that work.
- Regular, plain-language patch notes and a public roadmap; studio credits and team presence.
- Performance honesty: minimum and recommended specs, frame-rate targets, Steam Deck or console verification status.
- Community health: visible code of conduct, moderators, accessible options and parental controls.

## Category conventions

- Dark UI, neon or high-chroma accents, angular or condensed display type, texture, glow and chromatic effects.
- Key art is an illustrated or rendered hero scene with a logo lockup and "Wishlist now" / "Play free" call to action.
- Store page pattern (Steam, Epic, console): trailer first, capsule art, tags, system requirements, reviews, DLC.
- In-game UI: HUD, inventory, skill trees, battle pass tracks, loadout screens, minimap, scoreboards; each game builds a diegetic style (sci-fi terminals, fantasy parchment, military stencil).
- Esports identities: mascot or abstract emblem, team colors, jersey, overlays, lower-thirds; broadcast package with transitions and stingers.
- Live-service rhythm: seasons, events, limited-time offers, notifications and daily-login rewards.

## Where to differentiate

- **A diegetic UI language** that fits the fiction yet stays readable: consistent iconography, contrast and clear states. Games like Hades, Persona 5 and Destiny show how UI is brand.
- **Accessibility as feature:** remappable controls, scalable subtitles, colorblind modes, difficulty and assist options; marketing it widely is a differentiator, not just compliance.
- **Honest storefront presence:** accurate trailers with gameplay footage, short clips, a clear loop explanation.
- **Sound and motion identity:** stingers, UI sounds, loading animations and logo reveals recognized in two seconds.
- **Community-first systems:** cosmetic creation, mod support, tournament tools, clip sharing designed in.
- **Esports identity beyond the mascot:** wordmark-led systems, apparel as fashion, documentary content.
- **Quiet-premium hardware and peripherals** against the RGB default (Teenage Engineering-like restraint, Playdate-style personality).

## Category clichés

- Neon-green-on-black "gamer" aesthetic with angular edges and glitch effects on everything.
- Hooded silhouette with glowing eyes; sword, skull or dragon logos with chrome gradients.
- RGB rainbow loops on products and websites; screaming caps with exclamation marks.
- "Conquer", "dominate", "level up" copy; fake pro-gamer headset-and-hoodie stock photos.
- Battle-pass grids with red notification dots on everything; countdown timers on every offer.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Age ratings:** ESRB (US/Canada), PEGI (Europe, with legal force in several countries), USK (Germany), CERO (Japan), GRAC (Korea) and the Australian Classification Board; IARC issues storefront ratings for stores such as Google Play and Nintendo eShop. Rating badges and descriptors must appear on store pages and packaging per each body's rules.
- **Random-item purchases (loot boxes):** ESRB added the "In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)" notice in 2020 and PEGI "Includes Paid Random Items"; Apple's App Store and Google Play require odds disclosure before purchase. Belgium treats paid loot boxes as gambling; since 22 September 2024 Australia rates games with simulated gambling R18+ and paid-chance mechanics at M or above. Design effects: odds in the purchase flow, real-currency price shown, spending limits and receipts.
- **Minors and dark patterns:** COPPA applies to games directed to under-13s; the FTC's 2022 settlements with Epic Games combined a COPPA penalty with refunds for dark-pattern purchase flows. The UK Age Appropriate Design Code applies to games, and China's rules limit minors' playtime and require real-name registration. Confirm per market.
- **Influencer and esports sponsorship:** FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) require clear disclosure of paid or gifted promotion by streamers and teams; platform tools (Twitch branded-content labels) do not replace it. Betting sponsors trigger gambling-advertising and age-gating rules (21+ in many US states; UK Gambling Commission and ASA rules against appeal to under-18s).
- **Accessibility:** the CVAA requires accessible in-game voice and text chat in the US (an FCC waiver for games has been extended several times; verify status). For photosensitivity, follow WCAG 2.3.1 (no more than 3 flashes per second) and ITU-R BT.1702; add warnings. Xbox Accessibility Guidelines and gameaccessibilityguidelines.com are widely used references.
- **Platform and IP rules:** Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo and Valve publish brand and button-glyph rules for partners; using console symbols or logos without a license is infringement. Game IP and trade dress also limit fan-made merch.
- **Privacy:** GDPR and state privacy laws govern telemetry, voice chat and player IDs; do not collect voice or location from minors without consent.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Brand and key art: logo lockups (horizontal, stacked, icon), key art with safe areas for platform capsules, color and typography system, character and world references.
- Store and marketing: Steam/Epic/console capsule sets, trailers, press kit, landing site, social templates, launch and season campaigns.
- In-game UI kit: HUD, menus, inventory, shops, matchmaking, settings with accessibility options, tutorials; scalable for 1080p to 4K and handheld sizes.
- Esports: team identity, jersey, broadcast overlays, lower-thirds, bracket and stats screens, tournament site.
- Community and live ops: launcher, news feed, patch-note template, event pages, battle-pass screens, moderation tools.
- Hardware and packaging: peripheral industrial design, unboxing, retail displays.

## Voice and content

- Direct and fluent in the game's idiom without trying too hard; avoid forced slang. Test with real players.
- Say what you do in the game in one line ("Build a base, defend it, raid rivals") before the lore.
- Patch notes: changes grouped by system, numbers in a table, bug fixes separated, tone honest about regressions.
- Monetization copy is exact: price, currency, what is included, odds, refund policy.
- Reading grade 5-8 for UI; localized strings sized for 30-40% expansion in German, French and Russian.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.y2k-cyber` + `archetype.hero` for `audience.teens-gen-z`: dark canvas, high-chroma accent, sharp geometry, motion-heavy trailers. Fast recognition; differentiate with a distinctive UI language and sound.
- **Adjacent:** `style.illustrative-narrative` + `archetype.explorer`, borrowed from children's-book and animation art direction (Hades, Gris, Studio Ghibli references): painterly worlds, hand-drawn UI details, warm color. Suits narrative, indie and cozy games and travels well into merch.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.skeuomorphic-tactile` + `archetype.creator`: physical hardware references, textured plastics and metal, mechanical sounds, restrained color, for gaming hardware, retro handhelds and design-led indie studios. Risk: niche appeal, heavier assets, and competitive-esports audiences may read it as not for them; keep competitive HUDs high-contrast and flat.

## Metrics that matter

- Wishlist-to-purchase conversion, trailer view-through, store page conversion; install-to-first-session rate on mobile.
- Retention: D1, D7, D30; session length distribution; completion of the tutorial.
- Monetization health: payer conversion, refund and chargeback rate, complaints about odds or pricing.
- Community: Discord activity, report resolution time, toxicity rate, creator content volume.
- Esports: viewership, peak concurrent viewers, sponsor activation delivery.
