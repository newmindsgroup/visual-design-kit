# Sports and Fitness

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Athletes and serious trainers:** track progress, plan training and recovery, compare data, find gear that performs, belong to a tribe.
- **Beginners and returners:** feel welcome, know where to start, avoid injury and intimidation, build a habit.
- **Gym and studio members:** book classes, enter the building, manage billing and freezes, and cancel without a fight.
- **Fans of teams and leagues:** scores, schedules, tickets, highlights, fantasy, merchandise, community.
- **Parents of youth athletes:** schedules, carpool, fees, safety, permissions, communication with coaches.
- **Coaches and clubs:** rosters, availability, drills, payments, video review.
- **Outdoor and endurance users:** routes, safety, weather, offline maps, live tracking.
- **Wellness-oriented members:** stress relief, sleep, mobility and community more than performance.

## Trust signals

- Real athletes and coaches with credentials and results, shown with consent and context (typical outcomes, not best case).
- Product performance claims with test data: materials, weights, certifications, independent reviews.
- Science and safety: qualified trainers, injury-prevention guidance, medical disclaimers in the right place without scaring users.
- Accurate data: GPS, heart rate and sleep metrics with stated accuracy and sources; clear unit settings.
- Fair, readable membership terms: price, contract length, freeze rules, cancel flow in the app.
- Community integrity: inclusive code of conduct, safeguarding for minors, moderation.

## Category conventions

- High-energy photography of motion, sweat and effort, shot low and tight with strong flash or backlight; black, white and one neon or primary accent.
- Condensed, uppercase, italic or heavy geometric sans; diagonal cuts, big numerals, speed lines.
- Product pages with a hero image, key specs, fit guide, sustainability notes and reviews; drop culture for limited editions.
- Training apps: today screen, activity ring or score, streaks, leaderboards, badges, social feed, Apple Health and Google Fit integration.
- Team and league sites: scoreboard, schedule, standings, ticketing, shop, video, fan zones.
- Gym marketing: transformation imagery, membership offers, class timetable, club locator.

## Where to differentiate

- **A point of view on the athlete:** older, plus-size, adaptive, recreational and everyday movers shown as athletes, not as a diversity afterthought.
- **Data craft:** a readable training-load chart, clear recovery guidance, and big, tabular numerals (Whoop, Oura and Strava lead).
- **Community as product:** run clubs, local groups, segment leaderboards, shared challenges that need no ranking to feel rewarding.
- **Tone beyond hype:** coaching language that is calm, specific and encouraging; "rest day" treated as a feature.
- **Heritage and place:** local club identity, stitched crests, retro kit reissues (see Tracksmith and club football shirts).
- **Physical and digital continuity:** gear, packaging, store, app and event in one system.
- **Sustainable performance:** materials, repair and resale (Patagonia, Arc'teryx ReBird).

## Category clichés

- Silhouette of a runner at sunrise; "Just do it"-style imperatives without a point of view.
- Before-and-after transformation photos; sculpted torsos as the only body type.
- Neon gradients with lightning bolts; flexed biceps icons; motivational quotes in all caps.
- Streak anxiety and loss-aversion nudges ("Don't break your streak!") as the engagement strategy.
- Countdown "limited time" membership offers that renew indefinitely.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Health data:** the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, amended with effect from 29 July 2024, covers non-HIPAA health apps and wearables and treats unauthorized sharing as a breach. Washington's My Health My Data Act and similar state laws require specific consent for consumer health data; GDPR treats health data as a special category requiring explicit consent. Design effects: granular, unbundled consent screens, no pre-ticked boxes, sharing defaults that protect location (Strava's privacy zones are the model).
- **Subscriptions and memberships:** ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws apply, and many US states have health-club statutes with cancellation and cooling-off rules. The FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025, but enforcement under ROSCA continues. Show price, term, renewal date and cancellation route before payment; make cancel as easy as sign-up.
- **Advertising claims:** the FTC Act requires substantiation of health, performance and weight-loss claims; the Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) require disclosure of material connections and that testimonials reflect typical results or state them. "Results not typical" alone is not an adequate disclaimer. Supplement claims also fall under FDA rules (structure/function claims need the required statement).
- **Sports betting and fantasy:** state rules in the US (age 21+ in most states, responsible-gambling messaging), and UK Gambling Commission and ASA rules against ads with strong appeal to under-18s. Design betting integrations as clearly separate from fan content.
- **Marks and events:** Olympic and Paralympic terms and symbols are protected by law (in the US, the Ted Stevens Olympic and Amateur Sports Act); the Olympic Charter's Rule 40 restricts non-sponsor use of athletes in advertising around the Games. League, team and tournament marks are licensed. Do not reference them in campaigns without rights.
- **Youth sports:** COPPA, state age-appropriate design laws and SafeSport rules limiting one-to-one electronic communication between adults and minor athletes; team messaging apps need group threads and guardian visibility.
- **Accessibility:** ADA Title III applies to gyms and venues, and the EAA covers e-commerce in the EU; apps need WCAG 2.2 AA, large tap targets for use mid-workout (48px or more), and voice or audio cues.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Identity: logo and crest/wordmark system, color and type, kit or apparel graphics, number set, iconography.
- Website and store: home, product category and detail, size and fit, programs, class schedule, membership join, stories.
- Training app: onboarding and goal setting, today, workout player, tracking, progress, social, plan builder, settings and data sharing.
- Gym operations: access (QR or NFC), booking, billing, signage and wayfinding, class screens.
- Team and league: matchday graphics, scoreboard and broadcast overlays, ticketing, merchandise, fan app.
- Campaigns: product launch film, athlete content, social templates, OOH, event identity (race, tournament).

## Voice and content

- Active, specific and physical: "Warm up for 8 minutes", "Run 5 km at conversational pace".
- Coach, do not shout. Replace guilt ("You missed") with invitation ("Ready to pick up where you left off?").
- Use plain units and let people choose metric or imperial; label data with sources and ranges.
- Medical or injury messages are calm and give the next step ("Stop and consult a clinician if pain persists").
- Inclusive language for ability and body size; avoid weight-loss framing unless the product is clinical.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.bold-geometric-sport` + `archetype.hero` + `tier.mid-market` for `audience.teens-gen-z`: heavy geometric type, high-contrast photography, diagonal energy, one electric accent. Instantly legible; differentiate with a real point of view and honest data.
- **Adjacent:** `style.vintage-heritage-americana` + `archetype.explorer`, borrowed from heritage outdoor and running brands (Tracksmith-style club culture): varsity lettering, crests, muted palettes, craft photography. Builds belonging and works well for clubs, races and premium running gear.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.fashion-editorial-didone` + `archetype.lover`: high-contrast serif headlines, fashion-grade photography and a sensual tone for boutique studios and lifestyle fitness (Equinox-style fashion-led fitness). Risk: body-image pressure and exclusion, and low-contrast thin type on photography; use diverse bodies, give inclusive options and keep booking and billing screens plain.

## Metrics that matter

- Activation: first workout completed within 7 days; weekly active users; workouts per week.
- Retention: 30/90-day retention, membership churn, freeze and cancel reasons.
- Commerce: conversion, return rate by fit reason, repeat purchase, sell-through of drops.
- Data and trust: opt-in rate to data sharing, privacy complaints, accuracy-related support tickets.
- Community: club or group participation, event attendance, referral rate.
