# Healthcare Providers

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Patients seeking care:** find the right doctor or location, confirm insurance, book fast, know what to bring and what it will cost.
- **Patients in treatment:** read results, message the care team, refill prescriptions, manage multiple conditions and family members.
- **Anxious and unwell users:** low energy and attention, sometimes in pain or on a phone in a waiting room; need large type, short steps and reassurance.
- **Caregivers and family:** manage appointments and records for a parent or child with proxy access.
- **Referring clinicians and staff:** see referral status, schedules, forms and clinical documents without friction.
- **Community and funders:** understand the organization's mission, quality and outcomes; donors and board members read annual reports.

## Trust signals

- Real clinician profiles: photo, credentials, specialties, languages, board certification, patient-facing bio, availability.
- Accreditations, awards and quality ratings with the source and year, shown without implying guaranteed outcomes.
- Clear price and insurance information: accepted plans, estimates, financial assistance, how to dispute a bill.
- Privacy that is visible: secure login, plain-language privacy notice, who can see what in the portal.
- Facility reality: honest photography of the actual building, parking, entrances and rooms; accessibility information.
- Responsive human support: phone, chat hours, urgent-care and emergency guidance on every page.

## Category conventions

- Blue, teal and green palettes on white; rounded sans-serif type; photography of smiling patients and clinicians.
- Header with "Find a doctor", "Locations", "Book online", "Patient portal", "Pay a bill", and a prominent emergency or phone number.
- Doctor and location finders with filters; service-line pages (cardiology, oncology, pediatrics) with condition guides.
- Patient portal: appointments, messages, test results, medications, billing, visit summaries.
- Wayfinding and printed forms that reuse the web brand; large-format signage in hospitals.
- Long legal footers: notice of privacy practices, nondiscrimination notice, language assistance, price transparency files.

## Where to differentiate

- **Findability and booking:** make "get seen" the product: real availability, two-tap rebooking, reminders with prep instructions.
- **Plain-language medicine:** condition and procedure pages written at grade 6-8, with visuals, and a glossary; test results with context and next steps.
- **Warmth with competence:** photography of real staff and patients with consent, local detail, human voice instead of generic stock.
- **Service design across touchpoints:** website, portal, signage, appointment letters, bills and phone scripts that feel like one system.
- **Language and access:** multilingual content, interpreter request, captioned video, large-print and screen-reader-first journeys.
- **A calm, distinctive identity:** restrained color beyond hospital blue, with a type pairing and illustration language that suit the community served.

## Category clichés

- Blue gradient banners with a stethoscope, DNA helix, heartbeat line and hands holding a heart.
- Stock images of white-coat doctors with arms crossed, smiling seniors on benches, glowing medical hologram interfaces.
- "Compassionate care", "Your health is our priority", "Expect more from your healthcare" headlines.
- Red crosses and look-alike emblems; the Red Cross emblem is legally protected in many countries, so use plus signs and crosses with care.
- Fix: lead with specific services, real wait times or availability, and real people; avoid protected emblems.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; healthcare is among the most regulated categories.

- **HIPAA (US):** the Privacy and Security Rules govern protected health information. Patient portals, forms, chat, scheduling and email must protect PHI; marketing uses of PHI and patient testimonials, photos and stories need written authorization. Third-party tracking pixels, session replay and analytics on authenticated or appointment pages can disclose PHI; HHS guidance on tracking technologies was partly vacated by a federal court in 2024, so verify its current status.
- **FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and Section 5:** applies to health apps and sites not covered by HIPAA; unauthorized disclosure of health data to advertisers can count as a breach.
- **Section 1557 (ACA) and Section 504:** nondiscrimination notices, language-assistance availability and accessible electronic information; HHS's 2024 rule adopts WCAG 2.1 AA for web content and mobile apps for covered recipients, with compliance dates since extended (verify the current dates). Treat WCAG 2.2 AA as the target.
- **Price transparency:** hospital price transparency rules require machine-readable standard charges and a consumer-friendly display of shoppable services; the No Surprises Act adds good-faith estimates and notices. Design these as findable pages and plain-language estimates.
- **Medical advertising rules:** states regulate claims such as "board-certified", "best", outcomes and before/after photos; the FTC requires substantiation. Telehealth marketing must reflect state licensure.
- **Messaging consent:** TCPA governs appointment reminder texts and calls; collect and record consent and offer opt-out.
- **Interoperability:** the 21st Century Cures Act and information-blocking rules push easy patient access to records; design export and sharing flows.
- **EU/UK:** GDPR Article 9 treats health data as special category data; NHS digital service standards and the NHS design system guide public-sector patterns.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Website: home with search and booking, find a doctor/location, service lines, conditions and treatments, visitor information, billing and financial help, price transparency, careers, news.
- Booking and intake: provider search, availability picker, insurance verification, digital intake forms, pre-visit instructions, reminders.
- Patient portal and app: sign-in with recovery, results with context, messaging, prescriptions, bills and payments, proxy access, telehealth room, after-visit summary.
- Clinician and staff views: schedules, referrals, secure messaging, forms.
- Physical and print: wayfinding system, appointment cards, discharge instructions, signage, patient education sheets, annual report.
- Brand: logo, color and type system, photography guidelines, tone of voice, forms and letter templates.

## Voice and content

- Warm, direct, plain: "Your results are ready. Your doctor will explain them at your visit on Thursday."
- Reading grade 6-8 for patient content; avoid acronyms or define them; offer translations.
- Use the patient's terms for conditions alongside clinical names; avoid fear-based language and blame.
- Give next steps and timelines with every status, error and result.
- Emergency guidance ("If this is an emergency call 911") on forms and chat; humor is rarely appropriate.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.clinical-calm` + `archetype.caregiver` + `tier.mid-market` (for `audience.older-adults`): cool neutrals with a calm teal or green, large readable sans, spacious layouts, real photography. Credible and accessible; differentiate through booking speed and plain language.
- **Adjacent:** `style.scandinavian-nordic` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from hospitality and wellness design: warm woods, soft daylight palette, restrained type. Feels humane and modern, suits specialty and private practices.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.collage-mixed-media` + `archetype.everyperson` (for community health centers serving `audience.low-literacy-plain-language`): hand-cut shapes, bright local photography, illustration by community artists, bilingual signage. Distinctive and belonging-first. Risk: can weaken perceived clinical rigor and complicates accessibility; keep clinical information, forms and results screens plain and high contrast.

## Metrics that matter

- Online booking rate, time to book, no-show and late-cancel rate.
- Portal activation and monthly active patients; message response time.
- Task success for find-a-doctor, pay-a-bill and view-results (usability testing).
- Call-center volume per 1,000 patients and top call reasons.
- Patient satisfaction scores and complaint themes; accessibility audit defects.
- Price estimate accuracy and billing disputes.

## Reference anchors

- NHS Design System and service manual (service-manual.nhs.uk) for plain-language, accessible health patterns
- CMS Design System and the US Web Design System (USWDS) for public-sector health forms and content
- Mayo Clinic and Cleveland Clinic for patient education content at scale
- One Medical and Zocdoc for consumer-grade booking and membership experiences
- Epic MyChart as the dominant patient portal and its lessons on inbox and results design
- Kaiser Permanente for integrated insurer-provider communication across channels
