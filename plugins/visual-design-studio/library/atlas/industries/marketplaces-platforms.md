# Marketplaces and Platforms

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Buyers, guests and clients:** search, compare, judge quality and risk quickly, pay safely, get what was promised, and resolve problems.
- **Sellers, hosts and providers:** list fast, price well, get discovered, communicate, get paid on time, and understand fees and rankings.
- **Power sellers and businesses:** bulk tools, inventory sync, analytics, advertising, tax and shipping.
- **New entrants:** first listing, first sale, first review; the cold-start problem for both sides.
- **Trust and safety teams, moderators:** triage reports, spot fraud, enforce policies, explain decisions.
- **Regulators and rights holders:** notice-and-action routes, seller identity data, takedown processes.
- **Developers and partners:** APIs, widgets, integrations, affiliate programs.
- **Advertisers:** sponsored placements with clear labeling and measurable return.

## Trust signals

- Verified identity, with clear levels (email, phone, ID, business) and what each means.
- Reviews with context: verified purchase or stay, date, response from the seller, balance of positive and negative; clear display of rating counts.
- Transparent seller information: name or business, location, response rate, member since; for high-volume sellers, contact details as required.
- Protection: escrow, payment holds, guarantees, dispute resolution, refund timelines stated before payment.
- Honest ranking and sponsored labeling; consistent photo and listing standards (size, background, required fields).
- Visible moderation: report buttons, safety tips, policy pages written in plain language, status after reporting.

## Category conventions

- Search-led home: a prominent search bar with location, dates or category, and filters (price, rating, distance, availability).
- Listing grid or list with photo, price, rating, seller or host, location, quick-save heart; map-and-list split view.
- Listing detail: gallery, key facts, description, seller card, reviews, price breakdown, a sticky Buy / Book / Contact panel.
- Messaging inbox between parties; offers and counteroffers; order or booking status timeline.
- Seller dashboard: listings, orders, earnings, payouts, performance metrics, policy warnings.
- Trust-and-safety: verified badges, report link, safety center, community standards.

## Where to differentiate

- **Supply quality:** photo guidelines, listing assistants, required fields, and visible curation make the marketplace look better than its competitors (Airbnb photography program, Etsy Picks).
- **Density where it helps:** compact result lists for professional buyers; spacious visual cards for inspiration categories.
- **Price clarity:** total price including fees and taxes in results, not only at checkout; shipping and delivery dates in the card.
- **Search that understands intent:** facets that match how people shop, natural-language search, saved searches, alerts.
- **Seller success tools:** onboarding checklist, pricing suggestions, fee calculators, and plain explanations of ranking.
- **Brand personality that is not generic:** a distinct voice, illustration or photography system, a mascot or mark that is memorable (Etsy, Depop, Vinted, Faire).
- **Curation and editorial:** collections, guides and storytelling that give a reason to browse rather than search.
- **Community and repeat use:** seller storefronts, follow, wishlists, rewards, guild-like programs.

## Category clichés

- Pastel gradient hero with a search bar and three icon cards ("Find. Book. Enjoy.").
- Stock photo of a smiling host handing over keys; handshake icon for "trust".
- Fake scarcity ("3 people are looking at this") and countdown timers; "Only 1 left" without inventory logic.
- Reviews with only five-star ratings and no distribution; verified badges that mean nothing.
- Heart, star, shield and checkmark icons used interchangeably as trust symbols.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **EU Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065):** online marketplaces must trace and verify traders (Article 30), design interfaces that let traders provide required pre-contractual information (Article 31), provide a notice-and-action mechanism (Article 16) and statements of reasons for moderation (Article 17). Dark patterns that distort users' choices are prohibited on platform interfaces (Article 25), ads must be labeled with who paid (Article 26), recommender-system main parameters must be explained (Article 27), and platforms accessible to minors need high privacy and safety (Article 28). Rules apply to all platforms from 17 February 2024; verify obligations by size.
- **EU consumer rules:** the Omnibus Directive requires marketplaces to say whether the seller is a trader (and whether consumer law applies), disclose main ranking parameters and paid placements, and take reasonable steps to ensure reviews are genuine. The P2B Regulation (2019/1150) requires transparent terms and ranking for business users.
- **US INFORM Consumers Act (effective 27 June 2023):** marketplaces must collect and verify bank, tax and contact information for high-volume third-party sellers (200+ sales and $5,000+ in a 12-month period), disclose seller name and contact details to consumers for sellers with $20,000+ in annual gross revenue, and provide a clear way to report suspicious activity.
- **FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, effective 21 October 2024):** prohibits fake or bought reviews, undisclosed insider reviews and review suppression. Design review display, moderation and incentives accordingly.
- **Payments and seller onboarding:** KYC/KYB, money-transmitter licensing or use of a regulated payment facilitator (such as Stripe Connect), PSD2 in Europe, and tax reporting (US 1099-K, EU DAC7) shape seller onboarding forms and payout screens.
- **Content liability and safety:** UK Online Safety Act duties for user-to-user services; prohibited-item policies; illegal-content takedown processes. US Section 230 offers protection but does not remove the need for transparent, consistent moderation.
- **Price display:** all-in pricing laws for tickets and lodging (US FTC fees rule, UK DMCC Act "drip pricing") affect booking and ticket marketplaces.
- **Accessibility:** the EAA covers e-commerce services from 28 June 2025 and ADA claims target marketplaces; seller tools and buyer flows should meet WCAG 2.2 AA.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Brand and design system with listing-card, price, rating and badge components; photography and illustration guidelines for supply.
- Buyer app/web: home, search results (list, grid, map), listing detail, cart or booking, checkout, order tracking, messages, reviews, help.
- Seller app/web: onboarding and verification, listing creator with photo guidance, pricing tools, calendar or inventory, orders, payouts, analytics, policy center.
- Trust and safety: report flow, case status, appeals, safety center, verified-ID flow.
- Admin and ops tools: moderation queue, dispute resolution, fraud signals.
- Marketing: seller acquisition landing pages, buyer campaigns, email and push, brand film.

## Voice and content

- Neutral, clear and fair to both sides; explain ranking, fees and rules in plain terms.
- Listings need structured fields first, free text second; microcopy tells sellers what buyers want to know.
- Money language is exact: "Total includes service fee and taxes", "Funds are released 3 days after delivery".
- Safety and dispute copy is calm and procedural; never blames a party before review.
- Localize categories, units, currency, formats and cultural norms.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.flat-material` + `archetype.everyperson` + `tier.value` (or `tier.mid-market`): clean white canvas, a single brand color, rounded cards, photography-led results, a familiar app-store design language. Reliable; compete on supply quality and search.
- **Adjacent:** `style.data-dense-utilitarian` + `archetype.creator`, borrowed from trading, auction and B2B procurement tools (eBay seller hub, Alibaba, Faire): compact tables, filters, bulk actions, status chips. Best for professional sellers and wholesale; give buyers a calmer card view.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.editorial-magazine` + `archetype.explorer`: a curated publication where stories, guides and creators lead and the listing grid is secondary (Etsy editorial, Kinfolk-style shops). Distinct and memorable. Risk: slower search and lower conversion for intent-driven shoppers and added editorial cost; keep search, filters and price visible on every page.

## Metrics that matter

- Liquidity: search-to-contact or search-to-purchase rate, time to first sale, fill rate, share of listings that sell.
- Supply health: new seller activation, listing quality score, seller retention and earnings.
- Demand health: repeat purchase rate, buyer retention, average order value, net promoter score.
- Trust: dispute rate, fraud loss, review authenticity audits, report-to-action time.
- Regulatory: verification completion, takedown response time, appeals overturned.
