# Industrial, Manufacturing and Construction

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Engineers and technical specifiers:** find a part or system, verify dimensions, ratings and certifications, download CAD and datasheets, and justify the specification.
- **Buyers and procurement:** compare suppliers, lead times, minimum order quantities, price breaks and total cost of ownership; need quotes, not marketing.
- **Plant, site and fleet managers:** keep machines running; need spare parts, service booking, uptime data, safety compliance and training.
- **Operators and tradespeople:** use tools and interfaces with gloves, noise, glare and limited time; read labels from a distance.
- **Contractors and developers:** bid, schedule, document progress, manage subcontractors and handover.
- **Executives and investors:** capacity, safety record, sustainability, geography, backlog.
- **Recruits:** apprentices and skilled-trade workers who respond to pay, safety culture, training and pride in work.

## Trust signals

- Safety record shown honestly (TRIR or LTIR, days since last incident) and a visible safety culture, not a stock hard hat.
- Certifications and conformity: ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO 45001, CE, UL or NRTL listing, relevant industry marks; each linked to a certificate.
- Real facilities, real equipment, real people in correct PPE; project case studies with scale, schedule, location and outcome.
- Technical depth: datasheets, CAD files, tolerance and rating tables, installation guides, SDS documents.
- Service and parts network: dealer locator, response times, warranty terms in plain words.
- Longevity and scale: years in operation, installed base, named reference customers with permission.

## Category conventions

- Safety yellow, orange, steel gray, black and one corporate blue or red; heavy, condensed or geometric sans in uppercase for product names.
- Dark, high-contrast product photography on site or in the field; wide hero shots of machines at work.
- Mega-menus by product family and industry; filterable catalogs with parametric attributes; "request a quote" as the main conversion.
- Spec tables and downloads as first-class content; part numbers visible and searchable.
- Print remains important: catalogs, trade-show graphics, equipment decals, safety signage and manuals.
- Operator interfaces: large buttons, status colors, stripped-down screens on rugged tablets and HMIs.

## Where to differentiate

- **Findability of the right part:** search by part number, dimension, application and compatibility; McMaster-Carr's fast, no-nonsense catalog sets the standard.
- **Instruction and onboarding design:** exploded diagrams, step-numbered illustrations, short videos shot at the point of use, multilingual pictograms.
- **Honest, human recruiting:** wages, schedules, training paths and real crew photos draw skilled labor in a shortage.
- **Digital twin and data experience:** dashboards for uptime, energy and maintenance, designed for control rooms and phones.
- **Sustainability proof:** EPDs, recycled content and energy data in a standardized, downloadable form.
- **Craft pride in the physical product:** industrial design, finish, color and decals considered across machine, packaging and apparel.
- **Bold identity where competitors are all gray:** one confident color, a strong geometric mark and consistent decals.

## Category clichés

- Hard hat and hi-vis vest stock photos with arms crossed; blueprint-blue backgrounds with glowing gears.
- Globe-with-network lines and "innovative solutions" claims; sparks flying from a weld for every industry.
- Drone shots of a site at golden hour with no project information.
- Robot-arm 3D renders on a gradient background; AI-generated machinery with impossible parts.
- Hazard stripes used decoratively on unrelated pages.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Safety signs and labels:** ANSI Z535 (US) and ISO 3864 / ISO 7010 define safety colors, symbols, signal words (DANGER, WARNING, CAUTION) and layout. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.145 sets specifications for accident-prevention signs and tags. Do not use signal words or safety colors decoratively on products or brand materials, and follow prescribed formats on equipment decals.
- **Hazard communication:** OSHA HazCom (aligned with GHS) and the EU CLP Regulation (EC) 1272/2008 prescribe pictograms (red-bordered diamonds), signal words and the 16-section Safety Data Sheet. Packaging and label layouts must allow the required elements.
- **Conformity marks:** the CE mark has fixed proportions and a minimum height of 5 mm unless the applicable legislation says otherwise; UL, ETL and UKCA marks are licensed. Do not restyle or place them without certification. The EU Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 replaces the Machinery Directive from January 2027 and allows digital instructions; verify transition details.
- **Industrial HMIs:** ISA-101 (human-machine interfaces for process automation) and alarm-management standards ISA-18.2 and EEMUA 191 shape color use (reserve red and yellow for alarms), screen hierarchy and alarm priorities. These standards are guidance, but audits and customers expect them.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 AA for portals, catalogs and configurators; federal buyers request an Accessibility Conformance Report (VPAT) under Section 508. Permanent site signage follows ADA Standards (tactile characters mounted 48-60 inches above the floor).
- **Environmental claims:** EPDs follow ISO 14025 / EN 15804; "low-carbon", "green" or "recycled" product claims fall under FTC Green Guides and EU rules (see Energy and Climate).
- **Export and trade:** country-of-origin marking, HS codes and dual-use controls affect data sheets and product pages; confirm with trade counsel.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Brand: logo and equipment decal system, color standards (including paint codes), photography guide, uniform and PPE branding.
- Website: product-family pages, filterable catalog, product detail with specs and downloads, industry and application pages, dealer locator, quote request, careers.
- Technical content: datasheet and manual templates, exploded-view and wiring diagram style, CAD library, configurator.
- Operator and field apps: machine dashboard, checklists, service request, parts lookup by serial number.
- Print and environmental: trade-show stand, catalogs, safety signage, vehicle livery, site hoardings.
- Corporate: annual and sustainability report, investor deck, case-study template.

## Voice and content

- Plain engineering language: measurable claims with units ("Lifts 4,500 kg to 12 m", "IP67", "Lead time 3-5 days").
- Specific over superlative: say what it does, in which conditions, and how it was tested.
- Instructions are numbered, one action per step, and begin with a verb; warnings come before the step they apply to.
- Use metric and imperial where the market requires, with correct unit symbols.
- Recruiting copy states pay, shift and training; avoid "family" claims that cannot be backed up.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.corporate-modern` + `archetype.hero` + `tier.mid-market` for `audience.b2b-buying-committee`: strong industrial photography, safety-yellow or deep-blue accent, uppercase sans, spec-first pages. Predictable; compete on catalog UX and proof.
- **Adjacent:** `style.vintage-heritage-americana` + `archetype.everyperson`, borrowed from workwear and tool brands (Carhartt, Stanley, Snap-on): stamped badges, durable-goods tone, tradesperson photography. Strong for tools, parts and trades; keep spec data in a clean utilitarian layer.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.bauhaus-constructivist` + `archetype.creator`: primary geometric forms, diagonal compositions, poster-like photography celebrating industrial work. Distinctive in a gray sea. Risk: can read as political or retro to conservative procurement teams; keep safety information and specs in the standard formats and test with real buyers.

## Metrics that matter

- Quote requests and qualified leads by product family; request-to-quote conversion and time to quote.
- Catalog search success rate, zero-result searches, datasheet and CAD downloads per visit.
- Parts and service: first-time fix rate, order accuracy, online parts-order share.
- Safety and brand: incident rate, decal and signage compliance audits, training completion.
- Recruiting: applications per trade vacancy, apprentice conversion.
