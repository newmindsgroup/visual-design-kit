# Logistics and Supply Chain

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Shippers and merchants:** get a quote, book, print labels, track, handle exceptions and returns, and reconcile invoices.
- **Consignees and parcel recipients:** know when it arrives, change delivery, pick a locker or safe place, prove receipt, and get help when something goes wrong.
- **Freight forwarders and brokers:** manage bookings across modes, documents, customs filings, and carrier rates.
- **Supply-chain and operations managers:** visibility across suppliers and ports, ETA accuracy, inventory and demand planning, risk alerts.
- **Warehouse teams:** receive, pick, pack, and ship with handhelds, voice and wearables; safety and speed matter.
- **Drivers and couriers:** routes, tasks, proof of delivery, hours-of-service, with limited attention and variable connectivity.
- **Customs and compliance teams:** correct classification, documents, duties and certificates.
- **Executives and procurement:** cost, service level, sustainability and carrier performance.

## Trust signals

- Real-time, accurate tracking with timestamps, location, and a plain-language status ("Held at customs: documents requested").
- Service-level proof: on-time performance, claims ratio, transit times by lane, published as data, not slogans.
- Certifications and compliance: ISO 9001/14001/45001, C-TPAT or AEO status, GDP for pharma, dangerous-goods training, insurance.
- Network and assets: warehouse and port map, fleet size, offices, partners; credible scale.
- Transparent pricing: quote breakdown with surcharges, fuel, duties and accessorials; dimensional-weight rules explained.
- Human support: dedicated account contact, escalation path, 24/7 operations for exceptions.

## Category conventions

- Bold corporate color (UPS brown, FedEx purple/orange, DHL yellow/red, Maersk blue) and condensed or humanist sans; trucks, containers and planes as hero imagery.
- Tracking input on the home page; quote or rate calculator; service tiers (express, standard, freight, ocean, air).
- Dashboards with maps, shipment tables, status chips (on time, delayed, at risk), alerts and documents.
- Labels, barcodes, QR and signage are first-class design objects; 4 x 6 inch (102 x 152 mm) thermal labels are the common parcel size in the US.
- Driver and warehouse apps with big buttons, high contrast, scan-first interaction and offline mode.
- Sustainability reporting with emissions per shipment and green-service options.

## Where to differentiate

- **Exception experience:** when a shipment is late or damaged, say so first, explain why, show the new ETA and the fix. This is the moment brand is built.
- **Visibility design:** one map, one timeline and one status vocabulary across modes (ocean, air, road, rail), so customers do not decode carrier codes.
- **Operator ergonomics:** designing for gloves, glare, noise and 10-hour shifts; scan feedback by sound, vibration and color.
- **Documents without friction:** upload, extract, validate, and share bills of lading, invoices, certificates and customs data in one flow (Flexport-style digital freight).
- **Consumer delivery as brand:** delivery windows, photo proof, locker choice, carbon-neutral options, friendly driver messaging.
- **Physical identity:** liveries, uniforms, packaging, crates and warehouse environments carry the brand more than ads.
- **Honesty about complexity:** plain explanation of customs, surcharges and Incoterms.

## Category clichés

- Container ship at sunset; globe with glowing arcs; arrows circling a planet.
- Handshake in front of a truck; high-vis worker pointing at a tablet.
- "Delivering the future", "Connecting the world", "End-to-end solutions", "Resilient supply chain" copy.
- Blue-gradient dashboards with unlabeled map dots and no empty or error states.
- Chatbots that ask for a tracking number already on the page.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Dangerous goods marking and labeling:** US Hazardous Materials Regulations (49 CFR Parts 171-180), the UN Model Regulations, IATA DGR (air), IMDG Code (sea) and ADR (European road) fix label and placard shapes, colors, symbols, dimensions and wording. Do not restyle them; layouts must leave room for required marks.
- **Handling and packaging marks:** ISO 780 pictorial handling marks (fragile, this way up, keep dry), ISPM 15 stamps for wood packaging, and GS1 identifiers (GTIN, GS1-128, SSCC pallet labels) define what labels must carry and how barcodes are placed; GS1's Sunrise 2027 initiative promotes 2D barcodes at retail (verify timelines). Labels need quiet zones and print-quality contrast.
- **Customs and trade documents:** HS codes, commercial invoices, country of origin and Incoterms (ICC Incoterms 2020) vocabulary shape forms; EU ICS2 and US ACE filing add required data fields. Validation and error messages should be specific.
- **Driver apps and in-cab devices:** FMCSA ELD rules (49 CFR Part 395) define hours-of-service displays, and FMCSA bans hand-held phone use and texting by commercial drivers (49 CFR 392.80, 392.82). Design glanceable, voice-first, locked-while-moving interfaces.
- **Food and pharma traceability:** the US FDA Food Traceability Rule (FSMA 204) has an extended compliance date of 20 July 2028 (verify); DSCSA (US pharma) and EU FMD rules require serialization and verification; cold-chain data and alerts are part of the UX.
- **Delivery promises:** the FTC Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule requires a reasonable basis for shipping-time statements and delay notices with a cancellation option. Estimates should reflect real performance.
- **Worker monitoring and biometrics:** GDPR and state laws (for example Illinois BIPA) constrain driver cameras, biometrics and tracking; consent screens and notices are required.
- **Environmental claims:** "carbon neutral shipping" and similar claims fall under green-claims rules (FTC Green Guides, EU Directive 2024/825 from 27 September 2026); ISO 14083 and the GLEC Framework provide calculation methods.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 AA for customer portals; the EAA covers e-commerce and passenger transport but not freight directly. Federal customers may require Section 508 conformance reports.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Brand: logo, livery, uniform, label and packaging system, signage for depots and warehouses, vehicle decals.
- Customer web and app: quote and book, tracking page, delivery preferences, returns, invoices, claims.
- Control tower / visibility platform: shipment list, map, exceptions queue, document center, analytics, alerts.
- Warehouse UI: receiving, putaway, pick, pack, ship, cycle count, labeling; handheld and voice workflows.
- Driver app: route, stops, scan and proof-of-delivery, issues, hours-of-service.
- Labels and documents: shipping label, packing slip, bill of lading, customs forms, pallet label, carton marks.
- Reports: sustainability, service-level scorecards, annual report.

## Voice and content

- Direct, factual and time-stamped: "Departed Rotterdam 14:20 CET. Expected at Chicago on 12 May."
- Define terms once (bill of lading, dwell time, demurrage) and be consistent across modes.
- Error messages name the field and the fix: "HS code must have at least 6 digits."
- Delay and damage messages lead with the update and the next step, not an apology paragraph.
- Use 24-hour times with time zones for international moves; show locale-appropriate dates and units.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.corporate-modern` + `archetype.hero` + `tier.mid-market`: a strong brand color, clean sans, photography of vehicles and facilities, an operator-grade dashboard. Reliable; compete on visibility and exception handling.
- **Adjacent:** `style.swiss-international` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from transport signage and wayfinding (Swiss and Dutch rail systems): clear grids, tabular numerals, restrained color, explanatory diagrams (Flexport-style product marketing). Suits freight forwarders and supply-chain software.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.playful-bubbly` + `archetype.jester` for consumer parcel delivery, lockers and returns: friendly rounded forms, illustration, humorous status messages. Strong recall. Risk: B2B shippers need reliability signals, and playfulness during late or damaged deliveries can feel dismissive; switch to a plain tone for exceptions and claims.

## Metrics that matter

- On-time-in-full (OTIF), ETA accuracy, transit time variance, dwell time.
- Exception rate, time to resolution, claims and damage rate, first-attempt delivery rate.
- Digital adoption: share of bookings and documents handled online, tracking page contact deflection.
- Warehouse: pick accuracy, lines per hour, scan error rate, safety incidents.
- Customer: net promoter score, cost-to-serve, rate quotes converted to bookings.
