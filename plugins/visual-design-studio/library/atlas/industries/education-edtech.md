# Education and EdTech

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Learners (children, teens, adults):** understand a topic, practice, get feedback, see progress and stay motivated without feeling stupid or overloaded.
- **Teachers and instructors:** plan and assign work, monitor progress, give feedback, save time and keep classroom control.
- **Parents and guardians:** see how a child is doing, trust the safety of the product, understand fees and screen-time.
- **Prospective students:** compare programs, understand cost, aid, outcomes and admission steps.
- **Administrators and IT:** buy, deploy, integrate with student information systems, manage accounts and ensure privacy and accessibility.
- **Employers and L&D teams:** assign, track and certify training; prove completion and ROI.
- **Self-directed adult learners:** upskill around jobs and families in short sessions on mobile.

## Trust signals

- Learning evidence: efficacy studies, alignment with standards or curricula, transparent assessment methodology, instructor credentials.
- Honest outcomes: completion rates, job placement or salary data with definitions, sample size and date.
- Safety and privacy: clear data use, no ads for children, moderation of social features, parent controls, student-data agreements.
- Institutional recognition: accreditation shown with the accrediting body's name and scope; university and employer partners.
- Teacher voices: educator testimonials, classroom case studies, professional development.
- Transparent pricing: course fees, subscription terms, financial aid options and refund policy.

## Category conventions

- Friendly, rounded UI with bright color, mascots or characters, progress bars, streaks, badges and XP.
- Course catalogs with ratings, instructors, duration and level; syllabus pages and previews.
- Lesson player with video, transcript, quiz, notes and discussion; assignment and gradebook views for teachers.
- Dashboards for progress and goals; leaderboards and certificates; push reminders.
- University sites: program finders, apply/visit/give calls to action, campus photography, student stories.
- Heavy use of illustration or stock photos of students at laptops; blue, green and orange palettes.

## Where to differentiate

- **Design for learning, not engagement:** reduce extraneous cognitive load, chunk content, give immediate and specific feedback, and let learners see mastery over time.
- **Serve the whole triangle:** student, teacher and parent each need views that fit their tasks and time; most products serve one well.
- **Humane motivation:** celebrate effort, allow recovery from missed days, avoid shame-based streak pressure and manipulative notifications.
- **Accessibility as pedagogy:** captions, transcripts, dyslexia-friendly typography, adjustable pace and Universal Design for Learning principles that benefit every learner.
- **Distinct world:** a coherent character, illustration, sound and motion system that makes practice pleasant for months.
- **Credible adult and higher-ed identity:** a tone that is respectful and outcome-focused, not infantilizing.

## Category clichés

- Graduation caps, lightbulbs, open books, pencils and apples as logo shorthand.
- Stock photos of diverse students pointing at a screen or raising hands; a floating laptop with a glowing hologram.
- "Unlock your potential", "Learn anything, anytime", "Future-ready skills" headlines.
- Confetti and badge barrages for trivial actions; leaderboard pressure and loss-aversion streak flames.
- Campus photos with blue skies and brick buildings in every university brochure.
- Fix: show real learning moments and outcomes, set a clear tone for each audience, and use reward mechanics that support mastery.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; student and children's data rules are the strictest part of the category.

- **COPPA (US):** verifiable parental consent is required before collecting personal information from children under 13; the FTC's amended rule (compliance date April 2026) tightens retention, third-party sharing and persistent-identifier rules. In some cases a school can consent on behalf of parents for educational use only. Avoid behavioral advertising and cross-site trackers in children's experiences and design consent screens that parents can understand.
- **FERPA (US):** governs education records at institutions receiving federal funds; vendors typically act as "school officials" under contract and may use data only for the school's purposes. Student-record screens, parent portals and data exports need role-based access, audit logs and clear consent for directory information.
- **State student privacy laws:** California's SOPIPA, New York Education Law 2-d and similar statutes limit targeted advertising and secondary use of student data and require contractual terms; many districts require signed data-privacy agreements.
- **GDPR and children's codes:** GDPR Article 8 sets a digital age of consent between 13 and 16 by member state; the UK Age-Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code) requires high-privacy defaults, no nudge techniques and age-appropriate transparency for services likely to be used by children.
- **Accessibility:** US ADA Title II web rules for public schools and universities (WCAG 2.1 AA, with compliance dates extended in 2026; verify), Section 504 and Section 508, the EU Web Accessibility Directive for public bodies, and ADA suits against private providers. Design to WCAG 2.2 AA, include captions and transcripts, keyboard and screen-reader support for assessments and equations.
- **AI in education (EU AI Act):** systems for admission, assessing learning outcomes or monitoring exams are high-risk, and emotion recognition in education settings is prohibited; AI tutors and chatbots must disclose that they are AI (see the AI Products entry).
- **Advertising and enrollment claims:** the FTC Act, state consumer laws and, for institutions receiving federal aid, US Department of Education misrepresentation rules require accurate claims about accreditation, costs, graduation, job placement and earnings; state licensing and bootcamp disclosure rules may apply. Income share agreements and financing offers carry lending disclosures.
- **Subscriptions and refunds:** ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws for paid plans and free trials; UK and EU withdrawal rights for digital content.
- **E-rate filtering:** schools using E-rate funds must follow the Children's Internet Protection Act; products should work in filtered environments.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Learner app or web: onboarding with placement, home with today's plan, lesson player, practice and quiz, feedback, progress and mastery map, settings and parental controls.
- Teacher tools: class roster, assignment builder, gradebook, analytics, messaging, resource library, school admin and SSO.
- Parent view: child progress digest, safety settings, billing and consent.
- Marketing and admissions: program or course pages, outcomes and cost, application flow, financial aid calculators, open day booking, alumni stories.
- Content and brand: course templates, worksheets, slide decks, certificates, mascot or character, illustration system, sound and notification design.
- Physical and campus: wayfinding, prospectus, event graphics, school uniforms and merchandise.

## Voice and content

- Encouraging and clear: "You got 8 of 10. Let's practice the two you missed."
- Write for the right reading level of each audience; avoid jargon for children, and avoid baby talk for adults.
- Feedback explains why an answer is right or wrong, not only that it is.
- Treat errors and missed days as normal; no guilt or loss-aversion copy for minors.
- For institutional marketing, state outcomes with definitions and dates; avoid superlatives like "world-class" unless backed by rankings with source.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.playful-bubbly` + `archetype.innocent` + `tier.value` (for `audience.children-under-13` and casual language or practice apps): rounded type, bright palette, characters, springy motion, clear progress. Engaging and familiar; keep animation, sound and rewards under control and optional.
- **Adjacent:** `style.civic-plain` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from public services and libraries: very legible type, high contrast, plain-language structure, generous spacing. Suits school districts, adult education, accessibility-first platforms and assessments.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.skeuomorphic-tactile` + `archetype.caregiver` (for `audience.neurodivergent` and craft-based learning): paper textures, desk objects, hand-drawn annotations and physical metaphors that make a digital notebook feel real. Memorable and calming. Risk: heavier assets, slower performance on low-end devices and dated perceptions; offer a reduced-texture mode and test legibility with dyslexic readers.

## Metrics that matter

- Activation: first lesson completed; day-1, day-7 and day-30 retention.
- Learning outcomes: mastery gains on validated assessments, completion of courses, time to proficiency.
- Teacher adoption: weekly active teachers, assignments created, time saved.
- Parent trust: consent completion, support contacts, satisfaction.
- Enrollment funnel: inquiry-to-application, application-to-enrollment, cost per enrolled student.
- Equity and accessibility: usage and outcomes across devices, languages and learner needs; audit scores.
