# Real Estate and PropTech

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Buyers:** find homes that fit budget, commute, schools and lifestyle; understand price history, neighborhood and mortgage cost; schedule viewings.
- **Renters:** search by rent, bedrooms, pets and move-in date; apply quickly, understand total costs and lease terms, and manage payments and maintenance.
- **Sellers and landlords:** price correctly, present the property, choose an agent, track interest and offers.
- **Agents and brokers:** generate leads, manage listings and clients, share documents, close deals; need a personal brand and tools.
- **Developers and new-build sales teams:** present unbuilt homes with renders, floor plans, availability and reservation.
- **Property managers and building residents:** handle maintenance requests, access, packages, amenities and community communication.
- **Investors:** compare yield, risk, comparables and market data.

## Trust signals

- Accurate listings: current status, price history, days on market, correct square footage with measurement standard, disclosed digital edits.
- Agent credentials: license, brokerage, years in the area, recent sales, reviews, response time.
- Local expertise: neighborhood guides with real data (commute times, schools, price trends), maps, transit and amenities.
- Transparent cost: taxes, HOA, utilities, fees and estimated monthly payment with assumptions shown.
- Secure transactions: e-signature, document portal, wire-fraud warnings and clear closing steps.
- Brand legitimacy: license numbers, office addresses and fair-housing statements in the footer.

## Category conventions

- Search-first home page: location input, buy/rent/sell tabs, price and bedroom filters; map plus list with sticky cards; photo carousels.
- Listing cards with price, beds/baths/area, address, status badge and heart-to-save; detail pages with gallery, 3D tour, floor plan, key facts, map and schools.
- Mortgage calculators, affordability tools and "get pre-approved" flows; agent contact forms with prefilled messages.
- Agent profiles with portrait, bio, recent sales and testimonials; brokerage logos with mandatory disclosures.
- Blue, green or navy palettes with trustworthy sans type; luxury brokerages use black, gold and serif type.
- Developer sites: hero render, neighborhood story, floor plan explorer, availability grid, register-interest form.

## Where to differentiate

- **Honest, beautiful property photography:** consistent lighting and lenses, unretouched where it matters, floor plans and measurements that match.
- **Local authenticity:** neighborhood guides written by people, with specifics (the bakery, the park, the commute) instead of generic lifestyle copy.
- **Clarity of money:** a true total cost view (mortgage, taxes, fees) and an explanation of next steps.
- **Search that fits decisions:** commute-based, school-based and lifestyle-based search, saved comparisons, side-by-side tools.
- **Human agents as brand:** warm, credible portraiture and short videos in place of stock agent headshots.
- **Space visualized well:** 3D tours, floor plans and spatial interfaces that preload quickly and work on slow connections.

## Category clichés

- Glowing house icon, keys, roof outlines and location-pin logos.
- Stock photos of a couple handing over keys, a family on a lawn, a handshake in front of a "sold" sign.
- "Find your dream home", "Home is where the heart is" and "Your journey home starts here" headlines.
- Over-processed HDR interiors with blown-out windows and distorted wide-angle lenses.
- Sliders and carousels that bury listings; countdown-style urgency on open houses.
- Fix: lead with search and real listings, use truthful photography and neighborhood data, and treat the agent as a person.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; real estate is regulated at federal, state and local levels and by industry bodies.

- **Fair Housing Act (US):** it prohibits discriminatory advertising and statements in housing sales and rentals based on protected classes. Copy and images must not suggest preferences or limits (for example "perfect for young professionals", "no children", or imagery that consistently excludes groups). HUD advertising guidance recommends using the Equal Housing Opportunity logo or statement, and 2024 HUD guidance addresses digital advertising, ad targeting and algorithmic delivery for housing and credit. Use the official logo, keep it legible, and verify the current HUD guidance.
- **Digital image disclosure:** California requires licensees to disclose digitally altered listing images and point to originals (effective January 2026), and many MLSs have similar rules; virtual staging and AI edits need conspicuous labels next to the image. Verify the rules in each state and MLS.
- **Agent and brokerage advertising:** state license laws typically require the brokerage name and sometimes license numbers in ads, websites and emails; team names have limits; REALTOR is a registered membership mark with usage rules; MLS and IDX rules dictate listing attribution, data refresh and disclaimers. Practice rules for buyer agreements and compensation display have changed since the 2024 NAR settlement; confirm local MLS rules.
- **Mortgage advertising and lending:** TILA (Regulation Z) triggering-term rules and CFPB Regulation N apply to mortgage ads; mortgage loan originators must show their NMLS ID where required; Equal Credit Opportunity Act rules apply to prequalification flows. Monthly-payment calculators should state assumptions and that results are estimates.
- **RESPA and referral arrangements:** restrictions on referral fees and settlement-service marketing affect co-branded lead forms and partner placements.
- **Valuation estimates:** automated valuations and price estimates need accuracy disclaimers and must not be presented as appraisals.
- **Rental and screening:** the Fair Credit Reporting Act and state tenant-screening and fee rules affect application flows; several states require all-in rent pricing and limit application fees. Fair-housing law applies to screening criteria and algorithms.
- **UK and other markets:** property listings must carry material information under consumer protection law (UK Trading Standards guidance, verify); estate agent redress schemes and licensing apply elsewhere.
- **Privacy and security:** CCPA/GDPR for lead data and location data; clear consent on forms; wire-fraud warnings in closing communications.
- **Accessibility:** ADA and the European Accessibility Act apply to portals; provide accessible features information in listings and WCAG 2.2 AA for maps, galleries, 3D tours and calculators.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Portal or brokerage site: home search, results map/list, listing detail, saved searches and alerts, agent directory and profiles, neighborhood pages, sell/home value tool, mortgage calculators.
- Rental platform: search, listing detail, application, screening consent, lease signing, payment, maintenance requests, resident portal.
- Agent tools: CRM dashboard, listing creation with photo and disclosure fields, showing scheduler, offer management, document and e-signature flow.
- Developer marketing: master site, floor plan and availability explorer, renders and virtual tours, brochure, sales gallery, signage and hoarding.
- Property management and smart building: resident app, access, amenity booking, notices, visitor management.
- Brand: logo and agent co-brand system, yard signs and print, social templates, photo and video guidelines, tone of voice.

## Voice and content

- Plain, factual and friendly; lead listings with what is true and verifiable.
- Describe the property and neighborhood, not the buyer; avoid terms that imply protected classes.
- Explain money in steps: price, down payment, monthly payment, closing costs.
- Be clear about status (active, pending, sold) and date of last update.
- Warm, brief agent voice for bios and messages; avoid hype superlatives ("stunning", "must see") without specifics.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.flat-material` + `archetype.everyperson` + `tier.mid-market`: clean cards, strong search, a trustworthy blue or green accent, large photography. Fast and familiar for portals and brokerages; differentiate through data clarity and photography.
- **Adjacent:** `style.glass-spatial` + `archetype.magician`, borrowed from consumer hardware and spatial computing: layered translucent panels over immersive tours and floor plans, soft depth, fluid transitions. Suits 3D tours, new-build exploration and proptech tools.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.vintage-heritage-americana` + `archetype.everyperson` (for local independent agents and neighborhood brokerages): hand-lettered yard signs, printed neighborhood maps, community history, warm paper tones. Counters the sameness of portals with local belonging. Risk: can feel dated or low-tech, and nostalgic imagery must not imply exclusion; keep search and listings modern and fair-housing compliant.

## Metrics that matter

- Search-to-lead conversion, saved-search and alert engagement, listing detail views per session.
- Showing requests and booking completion rate; time to agent response.
- Rental: application start-to-submit rate, time to lease, maintenance request resolution time.
- Agent tools: listings published per agent, time to create a listing, disclosure completion.
- Quality: listing accuracy complaints, fair-housing review flags, accessibility audit results.
- Brand: unaided awareness in target neighborhoods and agent referral rates.

## Reference anchors

- Zillow, Redfin and StreetEasy for US search, map and listing-card conventions
- Rightmove and Zoopla (UK), Idealista (Spain) for national portal patterns and data depth
- Compass and Sotheby's International Realty for brokerage brand systems from tech-forward to luxury
- Opendoor and Airbnb for instant-offer and photography-led trust patterns
- Matterport-style 3D tours for spatial presentation of interiors
- HUD's Equal Housing Opportunity logo and fair-housing advertising guidance as compliance anchors
