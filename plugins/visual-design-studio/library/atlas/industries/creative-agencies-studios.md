# Creative Agencies and Studios

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Prospective clients (marketing directors, founders, procurement):** judge taste and competence in under a minute, find relevant work in their sector, understand process, team, price range and how to start.
- **Existing clients:** review work in progress, approve, access assets, understand invoices, and get timely updates.
- **Talent (designers, developers, producers, interns):** see culture, work quality, values, pay and growth, and apply easily.
- **Press, juries and peers:** award entries, interviews, case-study depth, credits.
- **Partners and collaborators:** freelancers, photographers, illustrators, production houses; clear briefs and onboarding.
- **The agency itself:** pitch decks, proposals, case studies and social content produced efficiently and consistently.

## Trust signals

- Work that is clearly good, with credits that say who did what and what results followed (named client, objective, outcome).
- Client logos and testimonials with permission; long-term relationships called out.
- A visible team with real roles and bios; leadership accessible.
- Process transparency: discovery, concept, design, build, launch, support; timeframes and typical budget ranges.
- Thought leadership with real authors, dates and opinions; talks, open-source tools, side projects.
- Professionalism: contract clarity, security practices, accessibility and privacy competence, references on request.

## Category conventions

- Minimal home page: big type, a reel or a rotating selection of projects, a short positioning statement, a visible Work / About / Contact nav.
- Case-study templates: hero, challenge, approach, outcome with metrics, credits, next project.
- Custom cursor, hover reveals, smooth scroll, marquee text, full-bleed video, grain textures.
- Monochrome base with one accent; oversized display type; unusual grid; handcrafted details.
- Sectors in tags (Brand, Digital, Campaign, Motion); awards badges (Cannes Lions, D&AD, Awwwards) as proof.
- Email signature, pitch deck and PDF credentials with the same visual system.

## Where to differentiate

- **A real point of view:** a stated belief about how work should be done, visible in both copy and design choices.
- **Showing process and thinking:** sketches, failed routes, system diagrams, working-prototype demos rather than only polished mockups.
- **Craft where clients look:** page performance, typography, motion and accessibility that visitors can feel and a pitch meeting can inspect.
- **Specialization:** a sector, discipline or method (healthcare brands, motion, design systems) that makes shortlisting easy.
- **Client collaboration tools:** shared project spaces, versioned reviews and clear approval gates.
- **Culture as proof:** team projects, studio rituals, open-source work, mentoring and hiring practices shown honestly.
- **Pricing clarity:** starting ranges or packaged offers (brand sprint, design-system audit) to reduce friction.
- **Own the structural novelty:** a site that is itself a unique piece of work, with a plain fallback.

## Category clichés

- Full-screen autoplay reel with no pause control; custom cursor that hides the real one.
- "We are a full-service creative agency" and "We craft digital experiences"; "storytelling", "purpose-driven", "bold ideas".
- Unlabeled mockups of poster-on-wall and billboard stock templates used for every project.
- Giant words that overlap images, illegible navigation, mystery-meat menus.
- Dark gradients, 3D glass blobs and generated imagery as a substitute for real project work.
- Awards laurels crowding the home page; a "Our process" circle diagram.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Advertising and endorsements:** the FTC Act and Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) apply to agency campaigns: material connections must be disclosed clearly (a visible "#ad" or paid-partnership label, not buried), and substantiation is needed for claims. UK ASA/CAP Code and EU unfair-commercial-practices rules apply similarly. Agencies share liability for misleading work.
- **AI-generated assets:** US Copyright Office guidance holds that works lacking human authorship are not registrable and that AI-generated material must be disclosed in registration; client contracts need AI-use clauses. EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations (labeling synthetic media and deepfakes) apply from 2 August 2026 (verify transition details). Consider content credentials (C2PA) for provenance.
- **Licenses for fonts, images, music and footage:** commercial licenses differ by use (desktop, web, app, broadcast, ad impressions); deliver license transfers with the project. OFL fonts are safe to bundle; stock and platform assets often are not transferable.
- **Trademark and naming:** clearance searches belong to counsel; agencies should present names as unvetted until cleared. Logos must avoid confusing similarity with existing marks.
- **Privacy and consent in client builds:** GDPR/ePrivacy cookie consent (reject as easy as accept, no dark patterns), CCPA/CPRA and Global Privacy Control signals, children's privacy where relevant; analytics and pixel choices are legal decisions.
- **Accessibility warranties:** clients face ADA and EAA exposure. Many contracts now require WCAG 2.2 AA conformance, an accessibility statement and a conformance report (ACR/VPAT); agree scope and testing in the statement of work.
- **Awards and case studies:** juries such as Cannes Lions expect work to have been genuinely commissioned and run, and check results claims in entries (verify current rules). Publish only results the client has approved.
- **Contracts and IP:** work-for-hire and assignment clauses, moral rights in UK/EU, confidentiality and non-disparagement clauses limit what a portfolio may show; get written permission and embargo dates.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Studio identity: wordmark, type, color, motion rules, templates for decks, proposals, invoices and social.
- Website: home, work index with filters, case-study template, services, team, journal, careers, contact/brief form.
- Case-study kit: result summary, image/video capture standards, credit format, client approval workflow.
- Pitch system: credentials deck, rate card, scope-of-work template, estimate builder.
- Client portal or workspace: review links, approvals, asset library, status updates.
- Recruiting: job pages, portfolio-review instructions, internship program, onboarding packet.

## Voice and content

- Specific and opinionated, written by the people who do the work; avoid pooled "we".
- Case studies state the problem, the decision and the measurable result in plain language; explain jargon.
- Services described by outcomes and deliverables ("Brand identity: logo, type, color, guidelines, in 8-10 weeks").
- Humor is welcome when it comes from character, not from template jokes.
- Contact copy is direct: who to write to, what to include, how long a reply takes.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.swiss-international` + `archetype.creator` + `tier.premium`: strict grid, grotesk type, black-and-white with one accent, large project imagery. The studio default for brand and design practices; differentiate through the work and writing.
- **Adjacent:** `style.dark-tech-developer` + `archetype.magician`, borrowed from developer-product marketing (Vercel, Linear): dark canvas, mono accents, interactive demos and code-level craft. Suits digital product, WebGL and AI-leaning studios; ensure contrast and keyboard access survive the effects.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.web-brutalist` + `archetype.outlaw`: raw HTML aesthetic, system fonts, plain lists of projects, link-heavy text and no hero reel. Distinct and honest; signals confidence. Risk: can look careless or unfinished to conventional procurement, and a plain list gives less proof of craft; compensate with exceptional writing, typographic precision and a fast, accessible build.

## Metrics that matter

- Qualified inbound inquiries per month, brief-form completion rate, inquiry-to-pitch and pitch-to-win rates.
- Average project value, retained-client share, referral rate.
- Case-study engagement: time on page, scroll depth, shares; visits from award or press mentions.
- Talent: applications per role, offer acceptance rate, regretted attrition.
- Delivery: on-time delivery, utilization, project margin, client satisfaction after launch.
