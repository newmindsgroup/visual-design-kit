# Beauty and Cosmetics

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Shade and product finders:** find the right foundation, skin-care routine or fragrance without testing in a store; need accurate color and honest guidance.
- **Routine builders:** understand what each product does, in what order, for how long, and whether ingredients conflict.
- **Skin and hair concern seekers:** acne, sensitivity, aging, texture; look for evidence and dermatologist input, often anxious about ingredients.
- **Self-expression and trend followers:** discover looks via creators, tutorials and drops; want playful experimentation.
- **Gift and ritual buyers:** value sets, packaging, scent notes and unboxing.
- **Professionals:** makeup artists, stylists and salons who buy in volume and need pro pricing and technical detail.

## Trust signals

- Complete ingredient lists (INCI) in a legible format, with plain-language explanations and concentrations only where substantiated.
- Evidence for claims: clinical or consumer-perception study results labeled as such, with sample size and duration.
- Inclusive shade range and honest swatching on many skin tones; model and creator diversity that shows the product on real skin.
- Dermatologist or chemist involvement with names and credentials; allergy and sensitivity guidance, patch-test instructions.
- Clear returns, sampling and shade-exchange policies; verified reviews with photos and skin type filters.
- Certifications used accurately (for example cruelty-free programs, organic standards) with scope stated.

## Category conventions

- Macro texture photography (cream swirls, droplets, powders), glossy close-ups, before/after and swatch grids.
- Soft neutral, pastel or monochrome palettes in clean beauty; black, gold and deep jewel tones in prestige.
- Serif or fine sans wordmarks; packaging as hero; minimal navigation by category, concern and ingredient.
- Quizzes, shade finders and routine builders; AR virtual try-on; subscription and refill programs.
- User-generated content galleries, creator tutorials and live shopping events.
- Product pages with benefits, key ingredients, how to use, full ingredients and reviews.

## Where to differentiate

- **Honest color:** calibrated product photography and a swatch system with consistent lighting across all shades; this is the single most credible detail in the category.
- **Ingredient and routine education that is design-led:** diagrams of layering order, concentration visuals, plain explanations and myths addressed.
- **A distinct sensory identity:** texture, scent language, sound and packaging finish carried through digital, social and print.
- **Representation beyond tokens:** consistent portrayal of age, gender, skin tone and hair texture across campaigns, not a single diverse tile.
- **Packaging and refill as brand:** structural design, tactile finish and refill systems that photograph well and reduce waste.
- **Tools that reduce returns:** shade matching, skin-concern diagnostics with clear limits, sample programs.

## Category clichés

- Pastel pink or beige gradient backdrops with a floating product and a splash of liquid.
- Dewy skin close-ups with airbrushed perfection; leaf-and-dropper imagery for every botanical claim.
- "Clean", "natural", "glow", "radiant" and "self-care" copy without definition; "chemical-free" claims.
- Filters and heavy retouching in ads that overstate results.
- Gold foil serif on black as a signal of luxury regardless of price point.
- Fix: show unretouched skin and real results, define each claim, and build identity from a specific texture, color and type system.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; cosmetics are regulated at the product level and at the claim level.

- **US cosmetics law:** the FDA regulates cosmetics under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act and the Fair Packaging and Labeling Act; the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA, 2022) added facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation, adverse-event reporting and labeling with contact information for adverse events, with fragrance-allergen labeling rules to follow (verify status). Labels must show ingredient lists in descending order, net contents and warnings where needed.
- **Cosmetic versus drug claims:** products that claim to treat or prevent disease or to affect body structure or function (acne treatment, sun protection, anti-dandruff, "regrows hair") can be drugs, requiring a Drug Facts panel and different approval. Keep claim wording aligned with product category in UI, product pages and social copy.
- **EU and UK cosmetics rules:** Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 (and its UK equivalent) requires a Responsible Person, safety assessment, product notification, INCI ingredient lists, the period-after-opening symbol and conformity with claims criteria (Regulation (EU) 655/2013: legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed decision-making).
- **Advertising standards:** the FTC requires substantiation for "clinically proven" and results claims and applies Endorsement Guides to creators; the UK ASA and CAP rules restrict filters and retouching that exaggerate product effect in ads. EU Directive 2024/825 restricts generic environmental claims from September 2026.
- **Animal testing and sourcing claims:** "cruelty-free", "vegan" and "organic" require substantiation and often certification; the claim scope and the market (some markets historically required animal testing) must be accurate.
- **Biometrics and AR try-on:** face-scanning tools can trigger biometric privacy laws such as Illinois BIPA and GDPR special-category rules; consent and retention notices belong in the try-on flow.
- **Subscriptions and sampling:** ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws for replenishment programs; see the e-commerce entry for pricing and returns.
- **Children and teens:** COPPA and age-appropriate design codes apply to youth-oriented skincare content; avoid anti-aging claims aimed at minors.
- **Accessibility:** ADA and the European Accessibility Act apply to beauty retail sites; swatch selectors must not rely on color alone and need text labels and focus states.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Website and shop: home, category and concern pages, product detail with swatches, shade finder, routine builder, ingredient glossary, reviews and Q&A, loyalty and account.
- Mobile and retail: app with try-on, scan-to-learn, in-store tablets, assets for retail partners such as Sephora, Ulta and Boots.
- Packaging: primary, secondary and refill systems, ingredient panels, labels, shippers, samples and gift sets.
- Campaign and social: hero imagery and film, creator briefs, UGC templates, ad variants, email sequences.
- Brand: logo and monogram, type, color and finish palette, photography and texture guidelines, motion, scent and sound language, voice guidelines.

## Voice and content

- Sensory and specific without hyperbole: describe texture, finish and feel before mood.
- Explain active ingredients and how to use them; give timing ("results typically seen in 8 weeks in our consumer study of 120 participants").
- Warm, inclusive and body-neutral; avoid shaming language about age, skin or weight.
- Safety copy is plain and visible: patch test, sun protection, sensitivity.
- Avoid medical claims in cosmetic copy; avoid fear-based "toxic ingredient" framing.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.classic-luxury-serif` + `archetype.lover` + `tier.luxury`: fine serif or didone wordmark, tonal palette, glossy macro photography, packaging-led layouts. Familiar to prestige beauty; differentiate through texture and photography.
- **Adjacent:** `style.clinical-calm` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from healthcare and dermatology: white space, lab-style typography, ingredient concentration tables, minimal imagery. Suits derm-led and results-first skincare.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.seventies-psychedelic-groovy` + `archetype.creator` (for `audience.teens-gen-z`): warm saturated palettes, rounded retro type, flowing patterns, candid portraits. Stands out against clean-beauty neutrals. Risk: strong period flavor can read as costume and warm saturated lighting distorts shade perception; keep swatches and product photography color-neutral and calibrated.

## Metrics that matter

- Conversion by device, shade-finder completion and add-to-cart after finder use.
- Return and exchange rate by shade; reasons for return.
- Repeat purchase and replenishment rate; refill adoption.
- Review volume, rating and photo-review share; creator-driven traffic quality.
- Subscription retention and cancellation reasons.
- Social engagement and share of voice among target segments; accessibility audit scores.

## Reference anchors

- Glossier for community-led, skin-first beauty brand design and a recognizable pink-and-white palette
- Fenty Beauty for a 40-shade launch that reset expectations for foundation range and representation
- Aesop for apothecary-style typography, packaging and retail design in a premium tier
- The Ordinary (DECIEM) for ingredient-led, transparent, clinical pack design
- Chanel and Dior beauty for prestige luxury packaging and campaign photography
- Rhode and Rare Beauty for founder-led modern brands built around social-first design
