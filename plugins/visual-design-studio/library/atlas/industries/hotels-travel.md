# Hotels and Travel

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Dreamers and researchers:** gather inspiration, compare destinations, read guides and save ideas for later, often over weeks and across devices.
- **Bookers:** find availability for dates and party size, compare room types and total prices, understand cancellation terms and pay.
- **Business travelers:** book fast, repeat preferences, need receipts, loyalty recognition and reliable connectivity.
- **Families and groups:** need room configurations, kid-friendly features, shared itineraries and clear costs per person.
- **Travelers with access needs:** require specific information about step-free access, room features and assistance services.
- **In-trip guests:** check in, find directions, order services, change plans and get support from a phone.
- **Hosts, hoteliers and agents:** manage inventory, rates, content and guest communication.

## Trust signals

- Total price including mandatory fees and taxes shown from the first result, with fee breakdown on demand.
- Honest photography: rooms, views and neighborhoods as they are, dated, with guest photos.
- Clear cancellation, deposit and payment timing; transparent change fees.
- Verified reviews with date, trip type and management responses; rating breakdowns by category.
- Scheme logos and licenses (for example ATOL and ABTA in the UK) used accurately; payment security and booking confirmation by email within seconds.
- Real service channels: 24/7 phone, chat or WhatsApp, local support at destination.

## Category conventions

- Search-first hero: destination, dates, guests and a prominent search button; map and list toggles; filters for price, amenities, rating and cancellation.
- Large, full-bleed photography with image carousels; room type tables; amenity icons.
- Urgency cues: "Only 2 rooms left", "5 people viewing", countdowns and "Genius" or member prices.
- Itinerary builders, saved lists and trip timelines; boarding passes and wallet integration.
- Loyalty programs with tiers; upsells for rooms, transfers, insurance and add-ons.
- Destination content: guides, neighborhood pages, seasonal calendars, curated collections.

## Where to differentiate

- **Place-based identity:** typography, color and photography that express the location and the property's character, not a generic beach and pool.
- **Clarity at decision time:** all-in price, calendar price view, map-based context and flexible-date search with plain language on rules.
- **Editorial content with utility:** itineraries, local advice and seasonal guidance written by people who have been there.
- **Service design:** pre-arrival communication, digital key and check-in, in-stay requests and post-stay follow-up that reflect the hospitality voice.
- **Inclusive information:** detailed accessibility data, family and pet information, and transparent sustainability data.
- **Quiet confidence:** fewer pop-ups and fake urgency; calm, helpful booking flows signal trust.

## Category clichés

- Infinity pool at sunset, hammocks, palm silhouettes and couples walking on a beach in white linen.
- Airplane wing windows, suitcases on a bed, compass and globe icons, paper-plane logos.
- "Escape the ordinary", "Your dream getaway awaits", "Wander more" and "Unforgettable experiences".
- Heavy discount banners, red countdowns and pre-ticked add-ons.
- Overlaid blue gradients on every photo to keep the headline legible.
- Fix: choose a regional palette and local typographic cue, show actual rooms and streets, and replace urgency tactics with clear availability information.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; travel mixes consumer protection, transport, accessibility and privacy rules.

- **Total price display (US):** the FTC's Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees (effective May 12, 2025) requires businesses that advertise short-term lodging to show the total price, including mandatory fees, prominently and not hidden behind later steps; US DOT rules require airline advertised fares to include taxes and mandatory charges, and rules on ancillary-fee disclosure and refunds have been litigated (verify current status). UK drip-pricing rules under the DMCC Act and EU transparency rules for air fares (Regulation (EC) 1008/2008) require the final price to be shown at all times, and optional extras to be opt-in, not pre-ticked.
- **Urgency and scarcity claims:** "Only 1 room left" and "popular now" messages are scrutinized under misleading-practice rules; the UK CMA secured commitments from major hotel-booking sites in 2019. Make claims accurate, dated and verifiable.
- **Package and travel protections:** the EU Package Travel Directive (2015/2302) requires standard pre-contract information forms and insolvency protection notices for packages; UK ATOL/ABTA and similar schemes control use of their logos; passenger rights (for example EU Regulation 261/2004) require displayed information in some contexts.
- **Cancellation and refunds:** terms, deposits and change fees must be clear before payment; EU and UK withdrawal rights do not always apply to dated accommodation and transport but notices must say so.
- **Accessibility information (US):** the ADA hotel reservations rule requires identifying and describing accessible features in enough detail for travelers to decide, and allowing accessible rooms to be reserved; Air Carrier Access Act rules apply to airlines. Website accessibility claims target travel sites frequently; the European Accessibility Act covers passenger transport ticketing and e-commerce services.
- **Short-term rentals:** many cities and the EU's short-term rental data rules require displaying registration numbers on listings (EU rules apply from 2026; verify local requirements).
- **Sustainability claims:** "eco", "green" and "carbon neutral" claims fall under FTC Green Guides, the UK CMA Green Claims Code and EU Directive 2024/825 from September 2026; use certifications accurately.
- **Privacy and payments:** GDPR/CCPA for guest and passport data, marketing consent, PCI DSS for card capture, PSD2 SCA for EU/UK payments; tracking on booking pages is a frequent consent issue.
- **Subscriptions and memberships:** ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws for travel clubs and paid loyalty tiers.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Website: home with search, destination and property pages, room details, offers, experiences, gallery, map, reviews, loyalty, help.
- Booking flow: search results with filters and map, property page, room selection, guest details, extras, payment, confirmation, manage booking.
- Apps and in-stay: check-in, digital key, requests and orders, itinerary, boarding passes, maps and offline mode, feedback.
- Campaign and content: destination films, photography library, seasonal campaigns, email journeys (pre-arrival, in-stay, post-stay).
- Physical and environment: signage and wayfinding, in-room collateral, key cards, amenities, stationery, uniforms.
- Brand: logo and sub-brands for properties, type and color, photography direction, illustration or map style, voice.

## Voice and content

- Hospitable, specific and concise; describe the room, the street and the morning, not "luxury experiences".
- Plain price and policy language: "Total for 3 nights, taxes and fees included. Free cancellation until 18 June."
- Accessibility copy is factual (door widths, step-free route) rather than euphemistic.
- Avoid pressure language; give honest status such as "Last booked 3 hours ago" only if true and verified.
- Local words and place names spelled and pronounced correctly; translate with care for each market.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.editorial-magazine` + `archetype.explorer` + `tier.premium`: large photography, serif display with clean sans, generous white space, story-led pages. Reads as inspiring and credible; differentiate through location-specific type and photography.
- **Adjacent:** `style.eco-natural-sustainable` + `archetype.lover`, borrowed from outdoor and wellness brands: earth palette, tactile textures, slow pacing. Suits eco-lodges, retreats and nature travel, with evidence for sustainability claims.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.retro-futurism-synthwave` + `archetype.explorer` (for design-led city hotels and airline-nostalgia brands): space-age forms, saturated dusk gradients, playful signage. Memorable and photogenic. Risk: gimmick that dates quickly, neon-on-dark contrast issues and a mismatch for business or family audiences; keep booking, price and policy screens clear and high contrast.

## Metrics that matter

- Search-to-booking conversion by device, availability search success, abandonment at payment.
- Direct booking share versus OTA commission cost; average daily rate and revenue per available room influenced by design.
- Price clarity: complaints about fees, support contacts about cancellation or charges.
- Content engagement: guide-to-search click-through, saved lists, return visits.
- In-stay: digital check-in adoption, service request time, satisfaction score.
- Accessibility feature search use and audit results; loyalty enrollment and repeat stays.
