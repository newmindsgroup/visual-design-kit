# E-commerce and DTC Retail

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Browsers and gift seekers:** discover products by category, occasion or price; see enough to decide without a store visit.
- **Intent shoppers:** search for a specific item, compare size, color and price, see availability and delivery date.
- **Repeat customers:** reorder, track an order, manage subscriptions, redeem points.
- **Cautious buyers:** need proof (reviews, fit guidance, return policy, warranty) before paying a brand they do not know.
- **Mobile-first and social shoppers:** arrive from ads, Instagram, TikTok or creators and expect a fast product page and wallet payment (Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay).
- **Operators:** merchandisers, support and fulfillment teams who need clean catalogs, correct inventory and consistent policies.

## Trust signals

- Real reviews with photos, verified-purchase labels, rating distribution and sort by fit or skin or use case.
- Clear total price, shipping cost, delivery date, tax and return policy before checkout, plus easy returns and exchanges.
- Secure and familiar checkout: recognized payment methods, address autocomplete, order summary always visible, guest checkout.
- Product truth: dimensions, materials, ingredients, care instructions, country of origin and certifications stated plainly.
- Responsive support: chat, email and phone hours; visible order tracking; honest stock and shipping delays.
- Brand reality: founders or makers shown, physical address, press, community and UGC.

## Category conventions

- Home page: announcement bar (free shipping threshold), hero banner, featured collections, best sellers, social proof, newsletter sign-up.
- Collection pages with grid, filters, sort and quick add; product pages with gallery, swatches, size guide, add to cart, accordions and reviews.
- Slide-in cart, upsell and cross-sell modules, free-shipping progress bar, one-page or three-step checkout with express wallets.
- Sticky add-to-cart on mobile, reviews summary near the title, "frequently bought together".
- Email and SMS flows: welcome discount, abandoned cart, post-purchase, win-back; subscribe-and-save for consumables.
- Clean white or off-white pages, large product photography on neutral backgrounds, one accent color for calls to action.

## Where to differentiate

- **Photography and product storytelling:** consistent lighting, scale and context shots, short video, 360 or on-model galleries; they matter more than layout tricks.
- **Information design on the product page:** make fit, ingredients, materials and care scannable; answer the top five pre-purchase questions above the fold.
- **Signature brand moments:** packaging, unboxing, order emails and 404 pages designed with the same care as the home page.
- **Typographic voice:** a distinctive but fast-loading typeface and a copy tone that survives repeated shipping and returns messages.
- **Merchandising by taste, not only by category:** editorial collections, guides and stories that make the shop worth browsing.
- **Post-purchase experience:** order tracking, care guides, repair and resale programs, customer-owned data.

## Category clichés

- Pastel hero with centered sans headline, a "Shop now" pill button and a floating product on a colored circle.
- "Clean", "minimalist" and "sustainable" copy with no evidence; the nomad-lifestyle-in-linen photo set.
- Pop-ups on page load asking for an email for 10% off before the user sees a product.
- Countdown timers and "Only 2 left!" urgency that is not real.
- Identical Shopify theme layouts with a swapped logo and hero image.
- Fix: invest in a signature photographic system and a content-rich product page; use real stock levels and delayed, relevant prompts.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; consumer protection and privacy law are the main drivers for online retail.

- **FTC Endorsement Guides and the 2024 reviews rule (US):** influencer and affiliate content needs clear disclosure of material connections; the rule bans fake or bought reviews and certain review suppression, and incentivized reviews tied to sentiment. Show review collection and moderation honestly; do not gate or hide negative reviews.
- **Made in USA and origin claims:** the FTC's Made in USA Labeling Rule requires that an unqualified "Made in USA" claim be backed by "all or virtually all" US content; qualified claims need care. Apply the same discipline to "handmade", "organic" and sustainability statements; EU Directive 2024/825 restricts generic environmental claims from September 2026.
- **Pricing and fees:** FTC guides on reference prices and the EU Omnibus rules require truthful "was/now" pricing, with the prior lowest price shown in EU promotions; the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act and several US states regulate drip pricing, so show mandatory fees early.
- **Delivery and returns:** the FTC Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule requires shipping within the stated time or notice of delay; the EU gives a 14-day withdrawal right for most online sales and requires a clear "order with obligation to pay" button.
- **Subscriptions and trials:** ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws require clear terms, consent and simple cancellation (the FTC click-to-cancel rule was vacated in 2025, but these laws remain); the UK is preparing a subscription regime under the DMCC Act (verify the start date).
- **Privacy and marketing consent:** GDPR/ePrivacy cookie consent without dark patterns, CCPA/CPRA opt-out links, CAN-SPAM, TCPA for SMS, CASL in Canada; discount-for-email pop-ups must disclose use.
- **Product safety and labeling:** EU General Product Safety Regulation requires manufacturer details and safety information on online listings; Prop 65 warnings in California; CPSIA for children's products; textile and cosmetics labeling rules (see the fashion and beauty entries).
- **Payments:** PCI DSS scope (use hosted fields), PSD2 strong customer authentication in the EU/UK.
- **Accessibility:** online retail is a leading target for ADA suits in the US and falls under the European Accessibility Act for EU consumers since June 2025; build to WCAG 2.2 AA (focus order in checkout, error messages, contrast on swatches, carousel controls).

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Storefront: home, collection, product detail, search and results, cart, checkout, order confirmation, account, order tracking, returns portal.
- Content: about, journal or guides, size and fit tools, shipping and returns, FAQ, store locator or pop-up pages.
- Marketing: landing pages by campaign, email and SMS templates (welcome, cart, post-purchase), social ad and shopping feed assets, creator briefs.
- Packaging and unboxing: shipper box, inserts, labels, thank-you cards, returns kit.
- Brand and system: logo, typography, color, photography guidelines, UI kit for theme customization, copy guidelines.

## Voice and content

- Friendly and concise; product names and attributes are specific, and benefits are stated in the customer's terms.
- Shipping, returns and sizing copy is blunt and reassuring: "Free returns within 30 days. Prepaid label in the box."
- Avoid fake urgency, shaming opt-out text ("No thanks, I hate saving money") and vague eco claims.
- Microcopy in cart and checkout gets as much attention as hero copy; errors give a fix.
- Write alt text and product descriptions that help both search and screen-reader users.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.scandinavian-nordic` + `archetype.everyperson` + `tier.mid-market`: warm whites, one muted accent, airy grid, soft shadows used sparingly, product photography leading. Fast and familiar; differentiate through photography, copy and packaging.
- **Adjacent:** `style.mid-century-modern` + `archetype.creator`, borrowed from furniture and home goods: earthy palette, rounded geometric type, curated editorial collections. Suits home, lifestyle and design-led brands that sell taste.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.maximalist-eclectic` + `archetype.jester` (for `audience.teens-gen-z` and snack, accessory or novelty brands): dense color, layered patterns, sticker-like badges, playful microcopy. High memorability in a sea of minimal shops. Risk: slower pages, harder scanning and checkout confusion; keep product galleries, cart and checkout calm, fast and conventional.

## Metrics that matter

- Conversion rate by device and source; add-to-cart rate; cart-to-checkout and checkout-completion rate.
- Average order value, revenue per visitor, repeat purchase rate and customer lifetime value.
- Product page: time to first image, review engagement, size-guide use, return rate by SKU.
- Performance: Largest Contentful Paint and Interaction to Next Paint on product and checkout pages.
- Support: contacts per order, "where is my order" share, refund and chargeback rate.
- Email and SMS: opt-in rate, revenue per recipient, unsubscribe and complaint rates.
