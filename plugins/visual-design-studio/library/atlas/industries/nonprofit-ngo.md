# Nonprofits and NGOs

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Individual donors:** give in under a minute on a phone, know where the money goes, get a receipt, feel their gift mattered, stop or change a recurring gift without a phone call.
- **Major donors and foundations:** see strategy, evidence, financial health and governance; get a report they can forward to a board.
- **Supporters and activists:** sign, share, show up, write to a representative; need a clear single action and a reason it works.
- **Volunteers and fundraisers:** sign up, get tools, track their peer-to-peer page, receive thanks.
- **Beneficiaries and communities:** reach services in their language on low-end devices, without stigma; appear in content only with informed consent.
- **Partners, journalists and regulators:** find facts, figures, filings and a press contact fast.

## Trust signals

- Financials a donor can find in two clicks: annual report, audited statements, program-expense ratio shown honestly with context, not as a vanity metric.
- Registration details on every donation surface: charity or EIN number, registered name, regulator links (US 501(c)(3) status, UK registered charity number).
- Specific impact statements with units and dates ("$40 pays for 12 weeks of clean-water maintenance for one family") with a link to the method.
- Named leadership, board, auditors and independent ratings (Charity Navigator, GuideStar/Candid seal, UK Fundraising Regulator registration) shown only when current.
- Visible dignity in imagery: named people, their words, consent-based photography, no pity framing.
- Closing the loop: receipts, impact updates and a "what happened to my gift" email within 90 days.

## Category conventions

- Hero photograph of a person in need or in action, a single bold donate button, and a donation amount selector with three suggested values plus "other".
- Monthly-giving upsell on the donate form, pre-selected amounts, a progress thermometer or goal bar.
- Documentary photography, warm color grading, one signature brand color (yellow, orange, blue or green) plus a big sans-serif.
- Impact numbers in large type; stories from the field; an annual-report PDF.
- Email sequences: welcome, thank-you, appeal, year-end giving push (a large share of annual giving arrives in Q4).

## Where to differentiate

- **Radical transparency:** itemized cost breakdowns, project-level reports with photos and coordinates, failures reported (charity: water's proof and maps set the standard).
- **Dignity-first storytelling:** first-person voice, strengths not deficits, illustration where photography would expose or exploit.
- **A product-grade donation flow:** Apple Pay and Google Pay, saved details, 3 fields or fewer on step one, accessible error handling, instant receipt.
- **Distinct visual identity:** most nonprofits converge on the same stock-photo hero; a custom illustration system, type voice or data visuals stand out.
- **Participation design:** peer fundraising pages, supporter walls, campaign toolkits that look good by default.
- **Honest urgency:** real deadlines and real matching-gift terms rather than fake countdowns.

## Category clichés

- Sad-child close-up with a "Just $1 a day" ask; sepia and desaturated suffering.
- Hands holding a globe, sprouting seedling in cupped hands, a heart made of anything.
- Drop-shadowed donate buttons in five competing colors; false-scarcity countdown timers.
- Stat blocks with no sources; "making a difference" and "together we can" as headlines.
- White-savior framing: volunteers from the funder country as the hero of the image.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Charitable solicitation (US):** most states require registration before soliciting and some prescribe disclosure wording on solicitations and donation pages; online giving reaches donors everywhere, so the footer or donate-page legal block often carries registration statements. Confirm per state.
- **Donor receipts (US):** donors need a written acknowledgment from the charity to deduct single gifts of $250 or more, and the charity must give a quid-pro-quo disclosure when a payment over $75 is partly for goods or services (IRC 6115). Gala tickets, premiums and auction items need a "fair market value" line in the receipt and on the form. Tax-deductibility statements should name the organization's status and say "to the extent allowed by law".
- **UK:** the Fundraising Regulator's Code of Fundraising Practice (a revised Code took effect 1 November 2025; verify) requires fundraising to be legal, open, honest and respectful; Gift Aid declarations have prescribed wording and a clear tick-box; charities generally must show their registered name and charity number on websites and fundraising materials (verify thresholds).
- **Recurring gifts:** disclose the amount, frequency and how to cancel on the form itself; state auto-renewal laws and card-network rules apply. Make cancel as easy as sign-up.
- **Privacy and marketing consent:** GDPR and PECR (UK/EU) govern fundraising email, SMS and profiling of donors; US rules include CAN-SPAM and TCPA. Pre-ticked consent boxes fail in the EU/UK.
- **Imagery and safeguarding:** informed consent for photographing beneficiaries, special care for children, and sector codes such as the Dóchas Code of Conduct on Images and Messages. Not law everywhere, but funders and press enforce it.
- **Accessibility:** ADA and WCAG 2.2 AA for donation flows; organizations receiving federal funds face Section 504 obligations; the EAA covers e-commerce-style donation and ticketing services in the EU from June 2025.
- **Political activity:** US 501(c)(3) organizations may not endorse candidates; campaign pages must be separated and labeled accordingly.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Identity: logo, color and type system that works in one color, on black-and-white print and at favicon size; photography and illustration guidelines with consent rules.
- Website: home with one primary action, donation form (one-time and monthly), campaign pages, impact and financials, stories, get-involved, press room.
- Fundraising kit: peer-to-peer page templates, social tiles, event signage, merchandise.
- Email and direct mail: appeal letter and reply device, thank-you and receipt, year-end campaign, monthly-donor newsletter.
- Annual or impact report designed as a web page and PDF; data visualizations with sourced figures.

## Voice and content

- Specific, human, active: name the person, the place, the cost and the result. Reading grade 6-8.
- Ask clearly and once per section; thank before asking again.
- Use people-first language and the terms communities use for themselves; never describe people as "beneficiaries" in public copy if they would not.
- Put the number and the unit together, with a date and a source link.
- Error and payment copy is calm: "Your card was declined. Nothing was charged. Try another card."

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.editorial-magazine` + `archetype.caregiver` + `tier.value`: documentary photography, one strong brand color, big type, human stories. Works for most appeals; differentiate with transparency and a better donation flow.
- **Adjacent:** `style.illustrative-narrative` + `archetype.everyperson`, borrowed from children's publishing and editorial illustration: custom characters and scenes tell stories where photographing people would expose them. Pair with `style.civic-plain` forms for the donation flow.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.grunge-punk-zine` + `archetype.outlaw`, as used by direct-action and campaigning groups: photocopy texture, stencil type, raw cut-and-paste. High recall with younger activists. Risk: readability and accessibility suffer, major donors and institutional funders may read it as unprofessional, and legal disclosures still need a plain layer.

## Metrics that matter

- Donation form conversion rate, average gift, monthly-donor share, first-gift to second-gift retention.
- Monthly donor churn and cancellation-flow completion; recurring-gift failure recovery rate.
- Cost to raise a dollar by channel; email list growth and unsubscribe rate.
- Campaign action completion (signatures, shares, volunteer sign-ups).
- Trust: independent ratings, donor complaints, reports downloaded, press mentions of accuracy.

## Reference anchors

- charity: water for proof-of-impact reporting and a recognizable yellow identity
- Doctors Without Borders / Medecins Sans Frontieres for direct, unsentimental field communication
- Amnesty International and Greenpeace for campaign graphics and activist tone
- UNICEF and World Wildlife Fund for global-scale identity systems and fundraising platforms
- Khan Academy for plain, mission-first donation messaging
- Charity Navigator and Candid (GuideStar) for transparency benchmarks
- Dóchas Code of Conduct on Images and Messages for ethical imagery
