# Automotive and Mobility

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Shoppers:** shortlist models, understand real cost (price, finance, insurance, charging, running costs), configure, compare, test-drive, trade in, and buy without a day lost at a dealership.
- **EV-curious buyers:** range, charging time, home charging, incentives, battery warranty and resale value anxieties.
- **Owners:** service booking, recalls, remote controls (lock, climate, charge), digital key, roadside help, subscriptions.
- **Drivers in motion:** navigation, media, calls, climate and driver-assistance status, with eyes on the road.
- **Riders and commuters (mobility services):** find, unlock, pay, trust the driver or vehicle, feel safe, get help.
- **Fleet and commercial operators:** uptime, telematics, driver compliance, total cost of ownership.
- **Dealers and technicians:** inventory, pricing, leads, diagnostics, parts.

## Trust signals

- Transparent pricing: on-the-road price including fees, finance APR with representative example, lease terms with total due at signing.
- Honest range and efficiency numbers with the test standard named (EPA estimate, WLTP) and typical-use ranges.
- Safety ratings (NHTSA, IIHS, Euro NCAP) shown with year, model variant and rating scale.
- Warranty, recall and service history in plain language; software-update policy for connected cars.
- Clear driver-assistance language that does not overstate capability.
- Real charger availability, live status and pricing; a map that shows what works.
- Dealer or showroom reviews, delivery timelines, and a named human contact.

## Category conventions

- Cinematic hero imagery of the vehicle in motion or in landscape; dark or neutral backgrounds; type set small and wide for premium models.
- 3D configurator with exterior, wheels, interior, options and running price; 360-degree and AR views.
- Model lineup carousel, spec comparison table, "build and price", "find a dealer", "book a test drive".
- Owner apps: vehicle status card, remote controls, charging status, service booking, digital key.
- In-car UI: large touch targets, a persistent status strip, voice assistant, night mode; physical controls for critical functions.
- Motorsport and performance cues for sport trims: carbon texture, red or yellow accents, italic type.

## Where to differentiate

- **Purchase journey:** one flow from online configuration to financing, trade-in, delivery date and handover, with state saved across web, app and showroom.
- **In-car interface craft:** fast response, low latency, minimal glance time, sound and haptic feedback, restrained animation that never distracts.
- **Software as brand:** consistent type, motion and iconography from website to app to dashboard to charger.
- **Charging experience:** route planning with charger reliability, plug-and-charge, transparent per-kWh pricing.
- **Tone:** confident and specific; engineering stories (aerodynamics, batteries, materials) rather than adjectives.
- **Sound and motion identity:** startup sound, door chime, indicator tone, animated logo.
- **Heritage vs future:** choose consciously; many brands blur the two and look generic.

## Category clichés

- Car on an empty mountain road at sunset; vehicle silhouettes with light streaks.
- "Born to move", "Drive your dreams", "Beyond driving"; unqualified "autonomous" or "self-driving".
- Glossy chrome gradients and lens flares; red sports car in a tunnel of light.
- Touchscreen-only controls for climate and wipers; flat dark UIs with low-contrast text.
- A configurator whose final price excludes mandatory fees.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Driver distraction (US):** NHTSA's voluntary Visual-Manual Driver Distraction Guidelines (2013) recommend that tasks require glances of no more than about 2 seconds and 12 seconds total eyes-off-road time, and that text entry, video and similar functions be locked out while driving. Most OEM and phone-projection guidelines align with them. Verify the current NHTSA status.
- **Controls and telltales:** FMVSS No. 101 (US) and UN Regulation 121 (UNECE) specify identification, location and colors of controls, displays and warning telltales, including required symbols and red/yellow/green meanings. Custom icons for safety-critical telltales must meet these.
- **Driver-assistance claims:** SAE J3016 defines levels 0-5 and is the shared vocabulary. California law (SB 1398, signed 2022) bars manufacturers and dealers from naming or describing partial automation in ways that imply the car drives itself, and the California DMV pursued Tesla over its "Autopilot" and "Full Self-Driving" marketing (verify the outcome). Name features and write copy conservatively; show driver-responsibility prompts.
- **Pricing and finance ads:** US Truth in Lending (Reg Z) and Consumer Leasing (Reg M) require "clear and conspicuous" disclosure when ads state payments or trigger terms (down payment, term, APR, total due at signing). Fine print in tiny gray type does not satisfy it. The FTC's CARS Rule was vacated by the Fifth Circuit on 27 January 2025, but the FTC Act and state dealer-advertising laws still apply; verify.
- **Window stickers:** the US Monroney label (Automobile Information Disclosure Act) shows price, equipment and fuel economy; online listings should mirror its facts.
- **EV claims:** EPA label (US) and WLTP (EU/UK) test cycles must be cited for range and efficiency; the EU AFIR requires card payment and price transparency at new public fast chargers (verify scope).
- **Data and connected services:** GDPR, the EU Data Act (applies from September 2025) on access to connected-product data, CCPA/CPRA and state privacy laws shape consent screens for telematics, location and in-car microphones.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 AA for shopping and owner apps; in-car accessibility is largely unregulated but large text, voice control and contrast are expected. The EAA covers e-commerce and ticketing services in the EU.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Brand: logo and badge system (grille, steering wheel, wheel center), typeface, color palette including paint colors, motion and sound logo.
- Website: home, model pages, configurator, comparison, offers and finance, inventory, dealer locator, test-drive booking, owners area, sustainability.
- Owner app: onboarding, vehicle status, remote controls, charging, trips, service, digital key, payments.
- In-car UI: home, navigation, media, climate, driver-assistance display, settings, voice; design for day and night with mandated telltales.
- Mobility service: map, booking, unlock, trip, safety center, receipts, driver tools.
- Showroom and events: environmental graphics, digital totems, brochures, launch films.

## Voice and content

- Lead with the person's goal ("Drive 480 km on one charge, tested to WLTP"), then the product detail.
- Short, concrete sentences in the car: one clause, verb first, under 4 seconds to read.
- Charging and range language is consistent: kWh, kW, percent, miles or km, with the test standard.
- Safety messages use plain imperatives ("Keep your hands on the wheel").
- Finance copy: representative APR example, total amount payable, and what is not included.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.quiet-minimal` + `archetype.explorer` + `tier.premium`: big photography, restrained type, generous negative space, minimal controls (the EV-era look of Polestar, Tesla and Lucid). Safe in the premium segment; differentiate through interaction quality and sound.
- **Adjacent:** `style.art-deco` + `archetype.ruler` for heritage and coachbuilt luxury, borrowed from watchmaking and haute-couture houses: geometric ornament, metallic accents, symmetrical compositions, a nod to 1930s automotive design. Works for brands with genuine history; keep the interface itself modern.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.retro-futurism-synthwave` + `archetype.magician`: neon horizon grids, chrome type, 1980s sci-fi optimism, for niche EV, tuner or limited-edition launches. Risk: dates quickly, can trivialize safety and autonomy claims, and in-car use needs high-contrast, low-glow variants for legibility and night driving.

## Metrics that matter

- Configurator completion, test-drive bookings per visit, lead-to-sale conversion, online order share.
- Price transparency: finance application completion, complaints about hidden fees.
- App: monthly active owners, remote-feature usage, service bookings per active owner, charging-session success rate.
- In-car: task completion time, glance time in testing, voice-command success rate, crash/latency incidents.
- Brand: consideration versus peers, NPS after delivery and after first service.

## Reference anchors

- Polestar and Volvo for Scandinavian-minimal EV brand and UI
- Tesla and Rivian for software-led ownership experiences
- Porsche and Mercedes-Benz for configurator craft and brand-system consistency
- Mini and BMW Motorrad for heritage-plus-modern brand layering
- Apple CarPlay and Google Android Auto design guidelines for driver-safe UI
- NHTSA driver-distraction guidelines and UNECE R121 as interface constraints
