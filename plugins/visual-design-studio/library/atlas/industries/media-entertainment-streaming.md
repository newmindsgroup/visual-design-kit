# Media, Entertainment and Streaming

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Viewers and listeners:** decide what to watch or hear in under two minutes, resume exactly where they left off, trust recommendations, and avoid spoilers.
- **Households:** shared accounts with different tastes; kids need safe profiles; parents need controls.
- **Cord-cutters and bundle shoppers:** compare services, price, ads tier, what's included, and when a title leaves.
- **Superfans and cinephiles:** depth, extras, credits, lists, community, physical editions.
- **Readers and news audiences:** trust, speed, paywall clarity, corrections, comments, alerts.
- **Creators, labels and rights holders:** artwork control, billing, metadata, analytics, payment transparency.
- **Advertisers and partners:** premium, brand-safe inventory and clear formats.
- **TV-room, mobile and car users:** the same service across 10-foot, handheld and voice interfaces.

## Trust signals

- Accurate metadata: runtime, year, cast and crew, languages, subtitles and audio description, rating and content advisories.
- Predictable behavior: playback starts in seconds, quality adapts, resume works across devices, downloads are honest about expiry.
- Transparent subscription terms: price, tiers, ads, simultaneous streams, renewal date, cancel path in the app.
- Editorial integrity: bylines, dates, corrections policy, sponsored-content labels, separation of editorial and advertising.
- Rights clarity: where a title is available and why it leaves; credits and contributor payments visible.
- Parent trust: kids profiles, PIN-protected adult content, ratings that match regional bodies.

## Category conventions

- Dark UI with poster or still-image rows, a large billboard hero with autoplay trailer, and a persistent search.
- Cards with 2:3 or 16:9 artwork; "Continue watching", "Top 10", "Because you watched" rows; genre tiles.
- Focus-based navigation for TV (D-pad, big focus rings, 10-foot type of at least 28-32px equivalent); touch navigation for mobile.
- Brand color as a single signal (red, purple, blue, green) against black or deep blue.
- Music and audio: album-art-first grids, mini-player, lyrics view, waveform scrubber, playlists with cover mosaics.
- News and publishing: homepage modules by story priority, article template with large headline serif, newsletters.

## Where to differentiate

- **Discovery as craft:** mood-based browsing, trusted curators, human-written lists, clear explanations for recommendations.
- **Artwork and thumbnail system:** consistent safe zones, localized key art, no misleading frames; art direction as a differentiator (A24 and Criterion approach).
- **Editorial voice:** film essays, interviews, liner notes and "behind the scenes" bring audiences deeper than the catalog.
- **Interface motion:** transitions that preserve context, fast focus response on TV, fluid mini-player.
- **Accessibility as product:** best-in-class captions (size, edge, color, background), audio description, dyslexia-friendly options, reduced motion.
- **Social without noise:** watch parties, shared lists, reviews that feel like a community (Letterboxd).
- **Brand sound and motion:** an unmistakable launch sting, ident and UI sounds.

## Category clichés

- Black screen with red or purple glow and a "Play" triangle button on every surface.
- Family on a couch lit by a TV; popcorn bucket, film reel, clapperboard and 3D glasses icons.
- Everything labeled "Trending", "Hot" and "New"; auto-playing audio on hover.
- Hero trailer that starts muted and cannot be paused; infinite carousels of near-identical thumbnails.
- "Unlimited entertainment, anytime, anywhere" taglines.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

- **Captions and audio description:** US CVAA and FCC rules require captions on IP-delivered video that aired with captions on TV, and set caption-quality standards (accuracy, synchronization, completeness, placement). The EU Accessibility Act (from 28 June 2025) covers access to audiovisual media services, and the AVMSD requires progress on accessibility; Ofcom has on-demand captioning duties in the UK. Design effects: user-controllable caption styling, accessible players, audio-description tracks and keyboard and screen-reader support. Reference: BBC Subtitle Guidelines, Netflix Timed Text Style Guide.
- **Ratings and minors:** MPA and TV Parental Guidelines (US), BBFC (UK), FSK (Germany) and others; the AVMSD requires protection of minors from harmful content (Article 6a) and parental-control tools. Display ratings and descriptors on detail pages and in trailers.
- **Photosensitive epilepsy:** WCAG 2.3.1 and ITU-R BT.1702 (the Harding test in the UK) limit flashing; autoplay trailers, intros and UI transitions must be checked.
- **Subscriptions:** ROSCA and state auto-renewal laws (California requires a simple online cancellation route for online sign-ups; verify); the FTC's click-to-cancel rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025 but enforcement under ROSCA continues. The EU adds a "withdrawal function" for distance contracts through Directive (EU) 2023/2673 (applies from 19 June 2026; verify). Price, renewal date and cancellation must be clear before payment.
- **Advertising and sponsored content:** FTC rules on deceptively formatted advertising and native advertising require clear labeling ("Sponsored"); the AVMSD regulates product placement disclosure in Europe.
- **Children's data:** COPPA applies to child-directed content (the 2019 FTC and New York AG settlement with Google/YouTube over children's data is the reference case); the UK Children's Code applies to services likely to be accessed by children.
- **AI-generated content:** EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties (labeling deepfakes and synthetic media) have applied since 2 August 2026, with a transition for some marking duties (verify). Disclose synthetic voices, de-aged actors and AI imagery in credits where required.
- **Contractual constraints:** credit-block and billing-block rules in talent and distribution contracts dictate type size and order on posters and end cards; follow them even when they break the grid.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Identity: logo, brand sting, color and type system, ident, social avatars, store icons.
- Apps for mobile, web, TV (tvOS, Android TV, Roku, Fire TV, console), car: home, browse, detail, search, player, profile picker, downloads, settings, account and billing.
- Artwork and thumbnail system: templates, safe zones, localization rules, trailer and teaser style, press kit.
- Editorial and marketing: campaign key art, landing pages, newsletter, social video templates, billboard and out-of-home.
- Creator and partner tools: dashboards for uploads, analytics and payouts.
- Accessibility pack: caption style defaults, audio description UX, remote navigation maps.

## Voice and content

- Describe titles in plain, evocative language in under 40 words; avoid spoilers and superlatives from marketing.
- Navigation and settings labels are short nouns and verbs; the interface speaks only when it matters.
- Error states for playback: say what happened and what to do ("Playback paused. Check your connection and try again").
- Pricing and plan copy leads with price, ads, streams and quality; no hidden "plus fees".
- Localize titles, synopses and key art by market, not just subtitles.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.glass-spatial` + `archetype.magician` + `tier.mid-market`: dark depth, blurred translucent panels, large cinematic artwork, focus-driven TV navigation. Fits OTT apps; differentiate with curation and sound.
- **Adjacent:** `style.editorial-magazine` + `archetype.sage`, borrowed from publishing (MUBI, Criterion Channel, long-form magazines): serif headlines, essays and curated programs make the service feel like a publication. Strong for art-house, documentary and premium audio.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.maximalist-eclectic` + `archetype.jester`: layered color, collage artwork, chaotic energy and irreverent copy, as in cult youth networks and late-night programming. Risk: noisy interfaces slow discovery on TV, may fail contrast and motion accessibility, and clash with licensed artwork you cannot alter. Keep playback, settings and billing calm and standard.

## Metrics that matter

- Time to first play, start-up latency, rebuffer rate, completion rate and resume rate.
- Browse-to-play conversion; search success rate; share of plays from recommendations versus search.
- Subscriber metrics: conversion from trial, churn, reactivation, tier mix, cancellation-flow completion.
- Accessibility: caption usage, audio-description use, complaints.
- Editorial: article completion, newsletter open rates, subscriptions from content.
