# Fashion and Apparel

## Audience and jobs to be done

- **Style seekers:** find inspiration, see how a piece looks and moves, understand how to wear it and what it signals.
- **Purchase-ready shoppers:** pick the right size and fit, check fabric and care, see delivery and returns terms.
- **Loyal customers and collectors:** access drops, waitlists, archive pieces and early releases; expect recognition and service.
- **Gift buyers:** need guidance on size, price range, packaging and exchange policy.
- **Press, stylists and buyers:** lookbooks, line sheets, imagery and wholesale portals.
- **Values-driven shoppers:** want materials, factory and impact information they can verify.

## Trust signals

- Accurate sizing: size charts per garment, model measurements and size worn, fit notes from reviews.
- Material and care detail: fabric composition, weight, origin, care label, and what the garment does after washing.
- Clear returns and exchanges: window, cost, condition, and a prepaid label; resale or repair programs.
- Authentic imagery: unretouched detail shots, real customers in reviews, video of the garment in motion.
- Supply-chain honesty: factories, certifications with scope (for example GOTS, Fair Trade, OEKO-TEX) and clear boundaries for claims.
- Heritage and craft evidence: atelier, materials, dates, founders, collaborations.

## Category conventions

- Full-bleed campaign imagery, runway or lookbook stories, seasonal collections and drops.
- Minimal navigation (Women, Men, New, Sale), sticky header, large product grid with hover-swap images.
- High-contrast serif or neo-grotesque wordmarks in black or white; neutral backdrops that let product color lead.
- Product pages with a long vertical gallery, a size selector, a "complete the look" module and fabric accordions.
- Instagram, TikTok and creator-led content; shoppable video; email teasers for launches.
- Packaging: dust bags, tissue, boxes and hang tags as part of the brand.

## Where to differentiate

- **A recognizable photographic point of view:** casting, locations, lighting and cropping that could not belong to another brand.
- **Typography as identity:** a custom or carefully chosen display face, consistently set; the move from serif to sans and back is a visible brand decision across the industry.
- **Fit and sizing tools that work:** body-data-informed recommendations, model comparison and fit-by-review beat generic charts and reduce returns.
- **Editorial depth:** journal, interviews, care guides and styling stories that give reason to return.
- **Material transparency:** show fiber, mill, factory and repair information as part of the aesthetic.
- **Digital craft:** transitions, cursor, image treatment and sound that feel curated, with performance preserved.

## Category clichés

- All-black or all-white minimal sites with a centered thin sans wordmark, indistinguishable between brands.
- Models posed against a gray studio backdrop with identical expressions; heavy retouching and uniform body types.
- "Timeless", "effortless" and "refined essentials" copy; sustainability claims with leaf icons and no evidence.
- Sale banners with red strike-through prices and constant discount pop-ups on premium brands.
- Parallax scroll-jacking and slow full-screen intros that block shopping.
- Fix: choose a specific casting and location language, show real fabric and construction, and make claims you can document.

## Regulatory and compliance notes

These shape design; apparel labeling rules are mostly about the product, but online claims follow advertising law.

- **Textile and care labels (US):** the Textile Fiber Products Identification Act requires fiber content, country of origin and manufacturer identification; the FTC Care Labeling Rule requires care instructions; wool and fur have separate labeling acts. Online product pages generally must show fiber content and origin information too; design fabric accordions and label graphics accordingly.
- **Made in USA and origin claims:** the FTC rule requires "all or virtually all" US content for unqualified claims; qualified claims ("assembled in the USA with imported fabric") must be clear and conspicuous.
- **Environmental and ethical claims:** the FTC Green Guides (under review) and the UK CMA Green Claims Code treat vague or unsupported "sustainable", "conscious" or "eco" claims as potentially misleading; EU Directive 2024/825 bans generic environmental claims without recognized excellent performance and unverified sustainability labels from September 2026. EU rules on ecodesign and digital product passports are expected to reach textiles; verify the timetable.
- **Fiber names and labeling (EU):** the EU Textile Products Regulation sets permitted fiber names and labeling; check each market's label language requirements.
- **Retouching and model rules:** some countries require disclosure of retouched body images in advertising or restrict very thin models (for example Norway and France); verify the target markets before campaigns.
- **Influencer and review rules:** FTC Endorsement Guides and the 2024 reviews rule require disclosure of gifted or paid posts and ban fake reviews; the UK ASA and CMA apply similar standards.
- **Pricing and returns:** was/now pricing and fees follow the rules in the e-commerce entry; EU withdrawal rights apply.
- **Children's apparel:** CPSIA and flammability and drawstring rules apply to children's products; Prop 65 warnings apply in California for some materials.
- **Intellectual property and counterfeits:** collaborations and trade dress require licenses; do not imitate others' logos or patterns.
- **Accessibility:** ADA and the European Accessibility Act apply to online stores; image-heavy sites need meaningful alt text, caption support for videos and keyboard-friendly galleries.

Confirm requirements with counsel for the specific product and jurisdiction.

## Key deliverables and screens

- Website: home with campaign, collection and category pages, product pages, lookbook and editorial, stores and stockists, about and sustainability, journal, account and returns.
- Commerce flows: size selector and fit finder, cart, checkout with wallets, drop and waitlist mechanics, order tracking.
- Campaign assets: seasonal lookbook, hero film, social templates, ad sets, email sequences, press kit, line sheets.
- Retail and physical: store graphics, packaging, hang tags, labels, shopping bags, invitations.
- Brand: logo and wordmark, monogram, type system, color, photography direction, motion, tone of voice, naming for collections.

## Voice and content

- Confident, spare and specific: name the fabric, the cut and the intended use.
- Describe fit honestly ("runs small; size up for layering").
- Avoid superlatives and filler ("must-have", "iconic") unless earned; use captions and credits like a magazine.
- Sustainability and ethical copy states what is true, with numbers and scope, and what is not yet done.
- Tone ranges from quiet luxury to irreverent streetwear; keep policies (returns, sizing, shipping) plain in either.

## Recommended directions

- **Category-expected:** `style.fashion-editorial-didone` + `archetype.lover` + `tier.luxury`: high-contrast Didone or refined sans, generous white space, full-bleed photography, restrained palette. Instantly legible as fashion; success depends on photographic quality and typography.
- **Adjacent:** `style.web-brutalist` + `archetype.outlaw`, borrowed from art, music and internet culture: stark system type, grids exposed, plain links, large raw images. Suits streetwear, avant-garde labels and drop-based brands.
- **Category-breaking:** `style.eco-natural-sustainable` + `archetype.explorer` (for materials-led or slow-fashion labels): earthy palette, fabric macro photography, repair and resale as primary navigation, supply-chain maps shown plainly. Differentiates through proof rather than mood. Risk: heightened scrutiny of green claims and a look that can feel worthy rather than desirable; every claim must be documented and the imagery must still be aspirational.

## Metrics that matter

- Conversion rate and revenue per visitor by device; add-to-bag rate on product pages.
- Return rate by SKU and size, and size-related return reasons.
- Full-price sell-through, markdown depth and drop sell-out time.
- Email and SMS list growth and revenue; loyalty and repeat purchase rate.
- Social engagement quality and shoppable-content conversion.
- Performance on image-heavy pages (Largest Contentful Paint) and accessibility audit scores.

## Reference anchors

- Harper's Bazaar under Alexey Brodovitch for Didone typography and photographic layout in fashion publishing
- Vogue and *i-D* for editorial casting, covers and typographic voices
- COS, Uniqlo and Zara for minimal, systematic digital retail at scale
- Balenciaga and Burberry for logo and type shifts (sans-serif wordmarks, then Burberry's 2023 return to serif) as lessons in brand typography
- Patagonia for activism and repair-led brand communications
- SSENSE and Net-a-Porter for multi-brand luxury e-commerce design
