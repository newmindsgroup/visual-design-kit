# 70s Psychedelic / Groovy

## Essence

Two overlapping decades of color and curve. The 1960s psychedelic poster scene in San Francisco (Wes Wilson, Victor Moscoso, Rick Griffin and Stanley Mouse and Alton Kelley for the Fillmore and Avalon ballrooms, from 1966) used vibrating colors and melting letterforms; Peter Max, Milton Glaser's 1967 Bob Dylan poster and Heinz Edelmann's *Yellow Submarine* (1968) carried them into pop culture. The 1970s softened it into warm earth tones, fat soft serifs (Cooper Black, ITC Souvenir and ITC Bookman Swash), rainbow stripes and sunbursts on packaging, record sleeves and kitchens. Today it signals friendliness, sensuality and good times for food, beauty and music brands.

## Visual signature

- Soft, heavy display serifs with rounded terminals (Cooper Black lineage), tightly tracked and often outlined or echoed with offset contours.
- Concentric arcs, waves, rainbows, sunbursts and flowing bands that wrap around content; shapes that behave like liquid.
- Warm earth palette: avocado, harvest gold, burnt orange, chocolate, cream, with plum, hot pink or teal as the psychedelic accent.
- Organic, symmetrical flower and mushroom motifs, eyes, hands, suns, moons; hand-lettered quotes and slogans.
- Grain, halftone and screen-print misregistration; paper-warm backgrounds.
- Wavy or melting type (1960s Fillmore style) used as a feature in headlines only.

## Composition and layout

- Wrap the hero in concentric arcs or a sunburst, and set one fat headline across them with a contour echo; keep supporting copy small and on a cream panel.
- Cut sections with wavy SVG dividers (2-3 colors) instead of straight lines; stack 3-4 colored bands behind imagery to create a rainbow.
- Use rounded containers for everything (cards, buttons, images), with generous 24-32px padding and consistent radii.
- Product and menu layouts: big circular crops of food or objects, hand-drawn price tags, slogans in script or soft serif.
- Pair a sensuous headline with a plain, readable body in Nunito; avoid mixing more than two display faces.
- Print: warm uncoated stock, 3-4 spot inks, overprint blends for gradients, halftone sunrays; album-sleeve proportions work well for square social posts.

## Imagery and iconography

- Illustration: flat, rounded vector with thick outlines or screen-print offsets; suns, flowers, mushrooms, birds, eyes, hands, planets; faces in profile.
- Photography: warm grading, flash portraits, film grain, soft focus, sun flares; lifestyle shots in natural settings; apply a cream-orange duotone for headers.
- Icons: filled rounded icons at 24-32px with 2.5px rounded strokes; circle or blob containers.
- Patterns: wood grain, flowers, op-art swirls, checkers; at 6-10% contrast behind panels.

## Motion

Slow, rolling and warm. Background waves drift across 20-40s, rainbow bands slide in with sine easing over 600-1200ms, and buttons squish slightly on hover (scale 1.04, 200ms). A melting or wavy text effect (SVG turbulence or `feDisplacementMap`) may be used once per page on a headline, not body text. Avoid hard cuts, strobing and fast pulsing, which turn the 1960s psychedelic vibe into a seizure risk. With `prefers-reduced-motion`, stop drifts and distortions and render the static composition.

## Fits and risks

- **Fits:** snacks, soda, cannabis-adjacent, wine and coffee (`industry.food-beverage-cpg`, `channel.packaging`), restaurants and bars (`industry.restaurants-hospitality`), beauty, fragrance and fashion (`industry.beauty-cosmetics`, `industry.fashion-apparel`), music, film and festivals (`industry.media-entertainment-streaming`, `industry.events-conferences`). It expresses `archetype.lover`, `archetype.creator` and `archetype.jester` personalities.
- **Risks:** pure 1960s vibrating color pairs (orange on blue, pink on green) cause chromatic vibration and poor readability; wavy lettering hurts legibility; and nostalgia can read as costume if not tied to the product's story. Drug-culture imagery needs care with age gating and platform ad policies.
- **Wrong for:** `industry.legal-professional-services`, `industry.fintech-banking` and `industry.government-civic` (seriousness and precision), and `audience.neurodivergent` when wavy, high-chroma patterns cannot be calmed.

## Do and don't

- Do commit to a warm 4-color palette and use one fat display face with a quiet text face.
- Do echo contours and arcs consistently; curves should share centers and spacing.
- Do keep text on solid cream or chocolate panels with verified contrast.
- Don't put saturated complementary colors directly against each other for text or thin lines; separate with Cream or Chocolate.
- Don't apply melt, wave or ripple effects to body copy, tables or form labels.
- Don't use cheesy peace signs, tie-dye clipart or fake stoner humor as a shortcut.

## Clichés and AI tells

- The lazy version: rainbow gradient, a tie-dye texture, a retro sunburst, "GROOVY" in Cooper Black, and a smiley flower, on every product.
- Generated 70s posters with melted, unreadable lettering, muddy browns and glossy 3D flowers.
- Fix: choose a precise period reference (a 1973 cookbook, a specific record sleeve, a kitchen tile), apply a coherent warm palette, set the display type properly (outline, echo, tight tracking), and keep the copy clear and specific.

## Accessibility notes

- All primary text pairs pass: Chocolate on Cream 10.58:1; Cream on Plum 8.58:1. Cream on Burnt orange #B44303 (4.62:1), Avocado #5F6F12 (4.59:1), Hot pink #C0215E (4.78:1) and Teal #0B6F78 (4.86:1) pass for body but sit near the limit; use 16px+ and weight 600.
- The brighter orange #D9591B falls to 3.21:1 on Cream and Avocado #7C8B2E to 3.09:1; the darkened versions in the palette are the minimum for text.
- Avoid vibrating pairs such as Burnt orange against Teal or Hot pink against Avocado at similar luminance; add a Cream keyline.
- Wavy and displaced display type must have a plain-text equivalent in the DOM and must not be used below 40px.
- Motion should be slow and removable; provide the reduced-motion static composition and avoid any flicker.
