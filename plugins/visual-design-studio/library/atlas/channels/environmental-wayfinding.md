# Environmental Graphics and Wayfinding

## Formats and specs

Codes differ by country and jurisdiction, and many are updated. The figures below summarize the US 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design (section 703, signs) from secondary sources; items marked "verify" were not confirmed against the primary text (access-board.gov). Local codes (for example California CBC 11B, Texas TAS, UK BS 8300, EU national rules) add or change requirements. Confirm with an accessibility consultant or code official.

## Structure and hierarchy

- **Four sign types:** identification (what this is), directional (where to go), informational (maps, rules, "you are here"), regulatory (safety, legal). Design a family with consistent dimensions, mounting systems, colors and typography.
- **Decision points:** place directional signs at every point where a person must choose: entrances, elevator lobbies, corridor intersections, stairs. Repeat confirmation signs after the choice. Limit each sign to 3-5 destinations, grouped by direction and listed by priority; arrows lead the line.
- **Naming and numbering:** use one name per place across signs, maps, apps and staff language; avoid internal jargon; use consistent numbering logic (for example floor-wing-room).
- **Message hierarchy:** destination name (largest), supporting info (smaller), symbol, arrow; sentence case or title case for lines longer than a word (all caps hurts legibility); limit to two weights.
- **Maps:** orient north-up or direction-of-travel up consistently; show "you are here"; use 5-7 colors max; add icons for key services; provide tactile or high-contrast versions.
- **Zoning and color-coding:** code floors, wings or routes by color plus number or letter (never color alone); pair with architectural cues like materials and lighting.
- **Experiential graphics:** wall murals, window film, floor graphics and installations build brand and story; keep them out of the sight lines of directional signs.

## Craft defaults

- Typography: sans-serif with open apertures, large x-height and clear distinction between I/l/1 and O/0. OFL options: Atkinson Hyperlegible, Public Sans, Inter (with tabular numerals), Overpass (derived from Highway Gothic), Lexend; commercial: Frutiger, Clearview, Transport, Helvetica Now, Univers. Track +2% to +5% in caps; avoid condensed widths below 4 in cap height.
- Color: high contrast between text and ground (target 7:1 where practical); use the building's materials and lighting in the choice; test under actual lamps (LED, fluorescent) and daylight; avoid red-green pairings.
- Pictograms: consistent stroke and grid (for example 24x24 grid scaled to 100 mm+), recognized symbols for toilets, exits, elevators, accessibility.
- Placement: eye level (about 60 in for general viewing, 48-60 in for tactile), perpendicular to the walking direction for hanging signs (clear height at least 80 in / 2030 mm over circulation, verify), in good light and not behind glare or glass reflections.
- Materials: aluminum, powder-coated steel, acrylic, laminate, vinyl, etched glass, wood; specify matte, non-glare finishes; consider impact, cleaning, graffiti and UV fade.
- Lighting and electronics: backlit signs need uniform illumination; digital directories need brightness control and fallback content.
- Languages and scripts: bilingual or multilingual signs need hierarchy and sufficient size for each script; check right-to-left reading direction.
- Inclusive design: tactile maps, audio beacons or apps, large-print guides, plain language, and consultation with disabled users.

## Production and handoff

- Phases: audit and programming, route and decision-point analysis, naming and message schedule, design intent (sign family), fabrication drawings, mockups on site, bidding, fabrication, installation oversight, post-installation check. The `layout` and `typography` skills support grid, scale and type choices; `print-packaging-social` covers large-format print production.
- Deliverables: sign-type drawings with dimensions and materials, message schedule (location, type, copy, arrows, symbols), mounting and clearance specs, color and finish samples, file formats for fabrication (vector PDF, AI or DWG), and maintenance guide.
- Mockups: full-size at real height and distance in the site, day and night; test with users who have low vision; check lines of sight and clutter.
- Regulatory: confirm accessibility (ADA, local), fire and life safety (exit signs, evacuation maps), building permits, heritage restrictions and sign-ordinance rules with the authority. Confirm requirements with counsel or a code consultant.
- Lead times: custom fabrication 4-10 weeks; allow for approvals and corrections; keep a spare parts and updates strategy (changeable inserts, digital).

## QA checklist

- [ ] Every decision point has directional information; no dead ends or contradictory signs.
- [ ] Place names, numbers and symbols are consistent in signs, maps, apps and staff language.
- [ ] Letter heights meet the viewing distance table or rule; tested at full size on site.
- [ ] Tactile signs: raised characters, Grade 2 Braille, mounting heights, no protruding objects.
- [ ] Contrast measured (light reflectance or WCAG ratio) under real lighting; non-glare finishes.
- [ ] Emergency, fire and accessibility signs reviewed by the authority having jurisdiction.
- [ ] Color is never the only signal; pictograms follow recognized standards.
- [ ] Message schedule matches fabrication drawings and installed signs; maintenance plan agreed.
