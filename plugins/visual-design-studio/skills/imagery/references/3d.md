# 3D for brand, product and web

Version-Timestamp: 2026-10-20 02:07:10 UTC

3D earns its cost when it shows something a camera cannot (a product before it exists, the inside of a mechanism, every color of every SKU, an interactive configurator) or when a brand owns a distinctive material world. It fails when it is a generic glossy blob on a landing page that costs 10MB and a second of main-thread time.

## When 3D helps

| Use | Why 3D | Alternative to consider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product not yet manufactured | Marketing can start before samples exist | Labeled concept renders only; no claims beyond the spec |
| Many SKUs, colors or finishes | One model, many renders with identical light and angle | Photography if only a few variants |
| Exploded views and mechanisms | Shows internal parts and assembly | Technical illustration for print |
| Configurators and AR try-on | User changes color, size and options live | Pre-rendered image sequence if the option count is small |
| Brand worlds and campaigns | Owned materials, objects and environments | Illustration or set-built photography |
| Data and space (maps, buildings, networks) | Spatial relationships | 2D diagrams for clarity and accessibility |

Do not use 3D when real photography is cheaper and more credible (food, people, existing products), when the audience is on low-end devices, or when it only decorates.

## Tools

| Tool | Strengths | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blender | Free and open source (GPL); modeling, sculpting, Geometry Nodes, Cycles (path tracing) and EEVEE (real-time) renderers; glTF export built in | Default for studios without a licensed pipeline; AgX is the default view transform since Blender 4.0 |
| Cinema 4D (Maxon) | Motion design standard; MoGraph cloners and effectors; Redshift renderer | Fast for abstract brand motion and product animation |
| Spline | Browser-based, collaborative, quick interactive web scenes; exports to web embeds, code and glTF | Good for prototypes and light interactive scenes; check runtime size |
| Houdini | Procedural modeling, simulation (fluids, particles, cloth) | VFX-grade; steep learning curve |
| KeyShot | Fast photoreal product rendering from CAD | Common in industrial design teams |
| Substance 3D Painter and Designer | PBR texturing and procedural materials | Industry standard for materials |
| three.js, React Three Fiber, Babylon.js | Web runtimes for custom interactive 3D | Full control, engineering effort |
| `<model-viewer>` (Google) | Web component for glTF with AR, camera controls and a poster | Fastest dependable path for product viewers |

## Materials basics (PBR metallic-roughness)

- **Base color:** the surface color without lighting. Keep non-metals between roughly sRGB 30 and 240; pure black (0) and pure white (255) do not exist in real materials and look fake.
- **Metallic:** 0 for non-metals, 1 for metals; values in between only for transitions such as dust or rust edges.
- **Roughness:** 0 is mirror, 1 is chalk. Real surfaces vary; use roughness maps with subtle variation (fingerprints, wear, micro-scratches) to avoid the plastic CG look.
- **Normal and height maps** for surface detail that geometry does not need to carry.
- **Index of refraction:** glass about 1.5, water about 1.33, diamond about 2.42.
- **Specialized layers:** clearcoat for car paint and lacquer, sheen for fabric, subsurface scattering for skin, wax, soap and milk, transmission for glass and liquids.
- **Brand color in 3D:** match under neutral light first, then check under the scene light; saturated brand colors often need lower base-color saturation to read correctly after lighting.

## Lighting and camera basics

- Start with an HDRI environment for realistic reflections, then add area lights for shape. Large soft lights for products; a rim light for separation from the background.
- Use the same lighting rig and camera for every SKU in a range, saved as a template scene.
- Product focal lengths 50-100mm (full-frame equivalent) avoid distortion; wide lenses only for environments.
- Use a filmic view transform (AgX or ACES) so highlights roll off naturally; output sRGB for screens.
- Add depth of field sparingly; use real camera values (for example f/4 at 85mm) rather than heavy blur.
- Contact shadows and ambient occlusion where objects meet surfaces; floating products are the most common 3D tell.

Render settings for stills: denoising (OpenImageDenoise or OptiX) with 128-512 samples is a common starting point; output 16-bit PNG or TIFF for delivery and multilayer EXR for compositing.

## Web 3D

Format: **glTF 2.0** (the Khronos standard), usually as a single binary `.glb`.

Compression:

| Technique | What it does | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draco (`KHR_draco_mesh_compression`) | Strong geometry compression | Needs a decoder (WASM or JS) loaded at runtime |
| Meshopt (`EXT_meshopt_compression`) | Fast-decoding compression for geometry, morph targets and animation | Pair with gzip or Brotli on the server |
| KTX2 with Basis Universal (`KHR_texture_basisu`) | GPU-compressed textures: smaller downloads and much lower GPU memory | Usually the biggest win; JPEG and PNG textures decompress to full size in memory |
| Quantization | Smaller vertex attributes | Usually included in the tools below |

Tooling:

- `gltfpack -i model.glb -o model.min.glb -cc -tc` (meshoptimizer): `-cc` applies meshopt compression with additional compression, `-tc` converts textures to KTX2 with Basis Universal; `-si 0.5` simplifies to about half the triangles.
- glTF Transform CLI: `gltf-transform optimize in.glb out.glb` with options for geometry compression and texture compression (WebP, or KTX2 through its `uastc` and `etc1s` commands). Check `--help` for the installed version.

Budgets (common starting points; test on a mid-range Android phone):

| Item | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Hero product viewer, compressed GLB | 1-3MB (under 1MB for mobile-first pages) |
| Triangles | 50k-100k for mobile, up to about 300k for desktop-only experiences |
| Textures | 1024-2048px maximum per map, KTX2; reuse texture sets across variants |
| Draw calls | Under about 50-100 |
| Device pixel ratio | Cap rendering at 1.5-2 |

Performance rules:

- Show a rendered **poster image** first; it is usually the LCP element. Load the 3D runtime and model after the page is interactive or on user intent.
- Pause the render loop when the canvas is offscreen (IntersectionObserver) or the scene is static (render on demand, for example `frameloop="demand"` in React Three Fiber).
- Provide a static image fallback when WebGL is unavailable.
- AR: GLB for Android Scene Viewer and WebXR; USDZ for iOS AR Quick Look. `<model-viewer>` handles both with `ar` and `ios-src`.

## Accessibility and reduced motion

- With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, disable auto-rotate, camera drifts, parallax and scroll-driven camera moves; show the poster or a still angle with manual controls.
- Provide controls that work without a mouse: buttons or keyboard for rotate, zoom and reset, and a pause control for anything moving longer than 5 seconds (WCAG 2.2.2).
- Give the canvas a text alternative (`role="img"` with an `aria-label`, or a visible description) and never put essential information only inside the 3D view; repeat specs and options as text.
- Avoid large, fast camera moves, zoom pulses and spinning; they trigger vestibular symptoms.
- Interactive targets in configurators: at least 24x24 CSS px, 44x44 for touch, with visible focus.

## Workflow

1. Brief: purpose, outputs (stills, animation, web, AR), the product facts that must be exact, and which surfaces are unknown.
2. Block out with simple shapes; agree camera angles and composition.
3. Lookdev on a turntable: materials and lighting approved on one hero angle.
4. Render tests at final resolution; inspect at 100% for noise, fireflies, aliasing and material errors.
5. Finals and compositing.
6. For web: clean the model (apply transforms, real-world scale in meters, Y-up, sensible origin), retopologize or decimate, bake details into normal maps, export glTF, compress, test on device, measure download and frame rate.
7. Record source files, CAD versions, texture licenses and render settings.

## Common failures

- Plastic look → roughness variation, real IOR, imperfections.
- Floating product → contact shadow and ambient occlusion.
- 4K textures on a phone → 2K maximum, KTX2.
- 20MB GLB with no poster → poster first, compressed model, lazy load.
- Auto-rotating hero ignoring reduced motion → static fallback and pause control.
- Invented product details on unseen surfaces → mark them unknown and avoid those angles until real data exists.
