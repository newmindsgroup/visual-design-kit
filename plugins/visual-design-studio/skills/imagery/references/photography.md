# Photography art direction

Version-Timestamp: 2026-10-20 02:07:10 UTC

Brand photography is recognizable when its light, lens, framing, color and casting are consistent enough to describe in one sentence. Write that sentence first, turn it into a brief and a shot list, and judge every image, commissioned or stock, against it.

## Art direction brief

| Element | Specify | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose and usage | Channels, placements, crops needed | Website hero (16:9 and 4:5 mobile crop), social (9:16, 4:5), print brochure (A4 portrait) |
| Subject | Who or what, doing what | Clinic staff mid-task with patients, hands busy, not looking at camera |
| Setting | Real location type, time of day, season, props | Small independent clinics, weekday morning, real equipment, no stock-office plants |
| Light quality | Hard or soft; source size | Soft, large window source; no on-camera flash |
| Light direction | Front, 45 degrees, side, back or rim, top | Key from 45 degrees camera left, gentle fill from a bounce |
| Color temperature | Kelvin and whether mixed sources are allowed | Daylight 5000-5600K; tungsten practicals allowed in background as warm accents |
| Contrast ratio | Key to fill | 2:1 for open and friendly; 4:1 for drama; 8:1 for noir |
| Lens and focal length | Full-frame equivalents | 35mm for context, 50mm for natural portraits, 85-135mm for close portraits, 100mm macro for details |
| Depth of field | Aperture range | f/2-2.8 for people with soft backgrounds; f/8-11 for products and architecture; focus stacking for packshots |
| Camera height and angle | Eye level, low, high, top-down | Eye level for respect and honesty; low angle for heroic sport; 90-degree top-down for flat lays |
| Composition | Grid, negative space, subject placement | Subject on the right third, left 40% clean for headline |
| Motion | Shutter choices | 1/500s or faster to freeze; 1/15-1/30s with panning for motion blur |
| Grade | Look, reference stills or LUT | Warm, slightly lifted blacks, natural skin; no teal-orange split |
| Casting | Ages, ethnicities, bodies, disability, real people or models | Mix of real staff and street-cast models reflecting the clinic's community |
| Wardrobe | Palette, patterns, logos | Solid mid-tones from the brand palette; no fine stripes (moiré); no visible third-party logos |
| Hair, makeup, styling | Level of polish | Natural, everyday; visible skin texture |
| Retouching level | Light, standard or high-end | Standard (see below) |

Write the one-sentence summary at the top: "Real clinic moments in soft morning window light, shot at eye level on a 35-50mm lens, warm natural grade, people busy with each other rather than posing."

## Shot list

| ID | Scene | Subject | Framing | Orientation and crops | Lens | Light | Props | Talent | Usage | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A01 | Reception check-in | Receptionist and patient | Medium shot | Horizontal 16:9 with left copy space; vertical 4:5 | 35mm | Window key left, bounce fill | Real check-in desk, tablet | 2 | Website hero | Must |
| A02 | Detail | Hands passing a receipt | Close-up | Square and 4:5 | 100mm macro | Same as A01 | Printed receipt | 2 hands | Social, brochure | Must |
| A03 | Back office | Coder reviewing a claim | Medium close-up | Horizontal and 9:16 | 50mm | Side window, 4:1 | Laptop with neutral UI | 1 | Product page | Nice |

Coverage rules:

- For every hero setup shoot horizontal and vertical, with copy space on each side, plus a tight detail and a wide context frame.
- Plan 8-15 setups per shoot day as a common starting point, fewer for complex lighting or children and animals.
- Shoot real product UI only if it is final; otherwise shoot screens off or with neutral content and composite later.
- Capture a color reference (color checker card) in each lighting setup.
- For video, capture 10-20 seconds of B-roll per setup with the same art direction.

## Lighting setups

- **Window light plus bounce:** natural, low cost; place subject 45 degrees to the window, white bounce opposite.
- **Three-point:** key, fill and rim (back) light for separation; rim at 1-2 stops above key for drama.
- **Overhead softbox:** flat lays and food, often with a black flag to add shape.
- **Product on white:** large diffused sources from both sides and above, a white sweep, exposure set so the background reaches pure white without clipping the product.
- **Glass and liquids:** backlight through diffusion for glow; strip lights for clean edge highlights; black cards to define edges.
- **Reflective surfaces:** large diffusion and black flags; control what the product reflects.

## Product and e-commerce standards

- Consistent angle set per product: front, three-quarter, side, back, detail, scale (in hand or in context), lifestyle.
- Same camera height, focal length (often 85-100mm to avoid distortion) and light for every SKU in a range.
- Main marketplace images: many marketplaces require a pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255) with the product filling most of the frame; verify each marketplace's current image policy.
- At least 2000px on the long edge for zoom.
- Packshots must be the real, current product: correct label, color and size. Never imply features or contents the product does not have.

## Stock selection

Selection criteria (all must pass):

- Matches the brief's light, lens and grade; or can be graded to match.
- Looks unposed: people engaged with each other or a task, not grinning at the camera (unless direct address is the intent).
- Specific setting with real, plausible objects.
- Representation is natural and respectful, not tokenistic.
- No visible third-party logos or watermarks, and no recognizable artwork or buildings without clearance.
- Not overused: run a reverse image search; if it appears on dozens of competitor and template sites, choose another.

Clichés to reject: business handshake, team high-five, woman laughing alone at a salad, people pointing at a transparent screen, lightbulb idea, chess pieces, puzzle pieces, call-center headset smile, doctor with crossed arms and stethoscope, hooded hacker, robot hand touching a human hand, glowing brain.

Licensing:

- **Royalty-free:** one fee, broad reuse; check the media and print-run limits of the standard vs extended license.
- **Rights-managed:** priced by use, territory and time; exclusivity can be bought.
- **Editorial use only:** cannot be used in advertising or to promote a product.
- **Free libraries (for example Unsplash, Pexels):** commercial use allowed under their own licenses, but no model or property releases are guaranteed, and selling unaltered copies is prohibited. Avoid identifiable people in advertising from these sources.
- Keep the license record with each image (source, ID, license type, date, user).

## Retouching standards

| Level | Includes | Excludes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Exposure, white balance, crop, dust, stray hairs | Skin work |
| Standard | Temporary blemishes, flyaway hair, clothing wrinkles, background cleanup, color match across the set | Changing body shape or permanent features |
| High-end | Frequency separation, dodge and burn, compositing, sky replacement, product cleanup | Plastic skin, misrepresenting the product |

Rules:

- Keep skin texture visible at 100% zoom.
- Do not alter body shape, skin tone or permanent features without the subject's consent and a clear reason. Some countries require labels on commercial images where bodies were retouched (France since 2017 for images altering a model's silhouette; Norway since 2022 for paid ads with body retouching). Confirm requirements with counsel for each market.
- Product retouching must not overstate performance; advertising regulators (for example the UK ASA) have ruled against retouched cosmetics ads.
- Color-manage the pipeline: calibrated display (D65 white point), a color checker in each setup, 16-bit working files, sRGB export for screens, CMYK conversion with the printer's profile at the end.

## Model and property releases

- **Model releases** are needed for commercial use of any identifiable person; a parent or guardian signs for minors. Include: names, date, shoot, description of use (media, territory, duration), compensation, signatures.
- **Property releases** for private interiors, recognizable private buildings in some jurisdictions, artwork, and pets with identifiable owners; remove or clear trademarks and logos in frame.
- **Privacy:** in the EU and UK, photos of identifiable people are personal data under GDPR; record the legal basis, retention period and how people can withdraw consent.
- **Metadata:** write creator, copyright, usage terms and release status into IPTC fields so the record travels with the file.
- Confirm release wording and data handling with counsel for the specific market.

## Delivery specs

- Masters: RAW plus 16-bit TIFF (Adobe RGB or ProPhoto for print workflows).
- Web derivatives: AVIF or WebP plus JPEG fallback, sRGB, sized to 1x and 2x of the largest display slot; art-directed crops per breakpoint, not one crop scaled.
- Print: 300ppi at final size, converted with the printer's profile.
- File naming: `brand-shoot-[yyyymmdd]-[setupID]-[frame].ext`.
- Alt text drafts for each delivered image.

## Common failures

- Brief says "authentic, premium, natural" and nothing measurable → write light direction, Kelvin, lens and grade.
- Only horizontal frames shot → plan vertical crops on set.
- One hero image scaled for every breakpoint → art-directed crops.
- Stock images from three different grades on one page → grade to one look or replace.
- Retouched skin like plastic → standard level, texture preserved.
- Free-library images of identifiable people in ads → licensed images with releases, or a shoot.
