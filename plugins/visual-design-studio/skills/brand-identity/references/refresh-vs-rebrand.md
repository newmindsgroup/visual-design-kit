# Refresh vs rebrand

Version-Timestamp: 2026-10-20 02:07:10 UTC

The most expensive mistake in identity work is throwing away recognition the brand already owns. Audit equity before drawing, change only what the problem requires, and plan the migration as carefully as the design.

## The change spectrum

| Level | What changes | Typical trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maintenance | Files cleaned, color specs corrected, templates rebuilt | Inconsistent execution, broken assets |
| Refresh (evolution) | Logo redrawn with the same idea, palette tuned, new type, updated imagery | Dated look, digital failures (small sizes, dark mode), new channels |
| Redesign | New mark and system, same name and positioning | Identity no longer fits the business or audience |
| Rebrand (revolution) | Name and/or positioning change, new identity | Merger, acquisition, legal conflict, reputation damage, a fundamentally new business |

## Valid and weak triggers

- **Valid:** merger or acquisition; repositioning or new audience; expansion into categories or markets where the current brand misleads; trademark conflict; reputation crisis; functional failure (the mark fails at 16px, has no one-color version, breaks in dark mode, cannot be embroidered or fabricated); the brand looks like a competitor.
- **Weak on their own:** new leadership wanting a mark; boredom inside the company (staff tire of a brand long before customers do); following a trend; "we need to look more modern" without saying for whom.

## Equity audit

1. **Inventory every asset:** logo and its parts, colors, typefaces, graphic devices, characters, packaging structure and color blocking, taglines, sounds, photography style, store or vehicle livery.
2. **Collect evidence of recognition.** The strongest method is a masked-asset test: show each asset without the brand name to category buyers and ask which brand it belongs to. Record the share who name the brand (fame) and the share who name a competitor (confusion). Use a properly sized quantitative sample for decisions; small qualitative tests (10-20 people) only flag problems. If no research budget exists, say so and treat every equity judgment as a hypothesis.
3. **Score each asset** on the fame-by-uniqueness grid from Jenni Romaniuk's *Building Distinctive Brand Assets*:

| | Low uniqueness | High uniqueness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High fame** | Shared with competitors: do not rely on it alone | Use or lose: keep and protect |
| **Low fame** | Ignore or test as a new asset | Investment potential: use more consistently |

4. **Functional audit** of the current identity: small-size legibility, one-color and reversed versions, contrast of brand color pairs (compute them), dark mode, motion, non-Latin scripts, file quality, template coverage.
5. **Touchpoint audit:** photograph or screenshot 30-60 real touchpoints (site, app, social, packaging, signage, vehicles, uniforms, documents). Inconsistency here often looks like an identity problem but is an execution problem.
6. **Stakeholder and customer language:** what words people use for the brand today, and which they should use tomorrow.

Output a keep / refine / replace decision per asset with the reason.

## Evolution or revolution

| Choose evolution when | Choose revolution when |
| --- | --- |
| Strong use-or-lose assets exist | Equity is weak, negative or tied to a past the business is leaving |
| Positioning is unchanged | Positioning or the business model changed |
| The problem is functional or dated execution | A merger or name change needs a new shared identity |
| Customers are loyal and habitual (packaged goods, banking) | The brand must signal a break to win a new audience |

Continuity rules for an evolution:

- Keep at least two of: the core shape idea, the primary color, the name's letterform character.
- Change no more than one or two major assets per cycle.
- Compare old and new side by side at the same size in the same applications; the change should be clearly better, and still recognizably the same brand from 3m away.

Public precedents worth studying:

- **Mastercard (2016):** the interlocking circles redrawn and simplified; in 2019 the name was dropped from the symbol for some uses. Evolution that kept the use-or-lose asset.
- **Burger King (2021):** returned to a bun-shaped mark closer to its 1969-1999 logo, with new type and a food-led color palette. Evolution by reclaiming dormant equity.
- **Airbnb (2014):** a revolutionary new symbol, mocked at launch, that became an owned asset through consistent use.
- **Gap (2010):** a new logo replaced within about a week after customer backlash. A revolution with no rationale shared and no equity analysis.
- **Tropicana (2009, North America):** a packaging redesign that removed the orange-with-straw image and was reverted after sales fell. Packaging structure and imagery were use-or-lose assets.

## Migration plan

1. **Old-to-new map:** logos, colors, fonts, tokens, templates, file names, URLs, social handles, app icons. One row per asset with owner and status.
2. **Rollout model:** big-bang launch (one date, high impact, high cost) or phased (digital first, then print, signage and fleet as they wear out). Most organizations phase physical assets.
3. **Touchpoint priority:** high visibility and low cost first (site, app, social avatars, email signatures, templates), then sales materials, then packaging (aligned with print runs and stock), then signage and vehicles.
4. **Coexistence period:** set the length and the rules for mixed environments. Never let two versions of the logo appear on the same piece.
5. **Packaging run-down:** coordinate with stock levels to avoid write-offs; plan a transition pack if needed.
6. **Legal:** file trademarks for new marks before launch; keep old registrations as long as the old marks are in use.
7. **Digital:** redirects, metadata (Open Graph images, favicons, app store assets), cached images on partner sites, CDN purges.
8. **Communication:** internal launch first (staff are the first audience), then a short public story that says why the brand changed in customer terms. A logo reveal alone is not a story.
9. **Measurement:** track recognition, search volume for the brand, sentiment and sales against a pre-launch baseline.
10. **Rollback:** keep the previous masters, templates and records archived and restorable.

## Pretesting and backlash

- Test with customers before launch, with the masked-asset method for recognition and a few real applications rather than logos on white.
- Prepare a one-page rationale that names the problem the change solves.
- Expect early negative reactions; judge by behavior (sales, recognition) over weeks, not comments in the first days.

## Common failures

- Redesigning before auditing equity → audit first; protect use-or-lose assets.
- Fixing an execution problem with a new logo → rebuild templates and governance instead.
- Changing name, logo, color and packaging at once for a loyal-habit category → change one or two assets per cycle.
- Launch with a logo and no applications → launch with the system working on real touchpoints.
- No coexistence rules → mixed old and new marks on the same shelf, site or vehicle.
