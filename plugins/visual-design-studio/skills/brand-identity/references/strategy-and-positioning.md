# Strategy and positioning

Version-Timestamp: 2026-10-20 02:07:10 UTC

Strategy is the part of identity work that decides what the design must make people believe. Keep it short enough to design from: one positioning sentence, a one-page platform, a perceptual map and a translation table. Everything else is evidence behind those four.

## Brief-to-strategy workflow

1. **Collect the brief variables.** What is being branded (company, product, service, campaign), who buys and who uses, markets and languages, price tier, channels, deadline, what must not change, and who decides. Ask only the questions whose answers change the work; give options and a recommended default for each.
2. **Desk research (half a day to two days).** Category size and direction, the 8-15 competitors buyers actually compare (direct, indirect and the do-nothing alternative), review sites and forums for the words customers use, and the client's own sales, support and search data if available.
3. **Stakeholder interviews.** 5-8 people, 30-45 minutes each: founders or leadership, sales, support, product. Ask what customers say when they choose them, what they say when they leave, and what the brand must never become.
4. **Audience evidence.** Reuse real research when it exists. If none exists, label any persona or need as an assumption and propose the cheapest test (5-8 customer interviews, a review mining pass, a survey). Never invent quotes, numbers or findings.
5. **Competitor visual audit.** Capture each competitor's logo, primary colors (hex), typefaces or type genre, imagery style, headline claims and tone. Put them on one board at equal size.
6. **Synthesis.** Write the category code (what everyone does), the whitespace (what nobody owns that buyers value), and the proof the client can actually deliver.
7. **Platform and positioning.** Draft two materially different positions, test each with the checks below, recommend one, record why the other lost.
8. **Translate to design.** Turn attributes into design levers (table below) and write the creative brief that the territories will be judged against.

## Positioning statement

Template (after Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm*):

> For **[target audience]** who **[need or tension]**, **[brand]** is the **[frame of reference]** that **[single most important benefit]** because **[reason to believe]**. Unlike **[main alternative]**, it **[key difference]**.

Fictional example: "For independent clinics who lose hours to insurance paperwork, Ledgerly is the billing service that gets claims paid on the first submission because certified coders review every claim before it is filed. Unlike billing software, it takes the work off the clinic's desk."

Run five checks before anything is drawn:

| Check | Question | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swap test | Could the top competitor sign this sentence unchanged? | Yes: the position is category code, not a position |
| Truth | Can the client prove the reason to believe today? | The proof is a roadmap item or an aspiration |
| Relevance | Does the target audience rank this benefit in its top three? | Only the founders care about it |
| Specificity | Does it name a real audience and a real alternative? | "Everyone", "businesses", "the market" |
| Sacrifice | What does the brand give up to own this? | Nothing: it is trying to be all things |

## Brand platform

One page. Every line must be usable by a designer or writer tomorrow.

| Element | Length | Content and test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | 1 sentence | Why the organization exists beyond profit; would staff repeat it unprompted? |
| Positioning | 1-2 sentences | As above; passes the swap test |
| Audience priority | 1-3 segments, ranked | Who the design serves first when segments conflict |
| Value proposition | 1 sentence plus 3 benefits | Functional, emotional and (where true) social benefit |
| Reasons to believe | 3-5 facts | Each verifiable; cite the source |
| Personality | 3-5 attributes as "X, not Y" | "Confident, not arrogant"; the "not" half stops misreadings |
| Archetype | 1 primary, 1 secondary at most | From the atlas `archetype.*` entries |
| Voice principles | 3-4 principles with do/don't lines | Hands off to the content and voice work |
| Messaging hierarchy | Tagline territory, core message, 3 pillars, proof under each | Pillars map to site sections and campaign lines |
| Mandatories and no-go zones | Bullets | Legal claims, locked assets, cultural sensitivities |

## Personality sliders

Score the current and the target brand from 1 to 7 on each slider with stakeholders, then design for the target. A move of 2+ points on any slider means the identity must visibly change on that dimension.

| Slider (1 ↔ 7) | Moves toward 1 | Moves toward 7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Classic ↔ Modern | Serif or engraved forms, heritage color, symmetry | Grotesque or geometric sans, saturated or unexpected color, asymmetry |
| Serious ↔ Playful | Straight cuts, restrained palette, slow motion | Rounded or irregular shapes, bright palette, bouncy motion |
| Exclusive ↔ Accessible | Wordmark-led, white space, few colors, small type | Symbol-led, warm color, larger type, plain words |
| Quiet ↔ Loud | Low contrast, small logo, generous margins | Full-bleed color, oversized type, high contrast |
| Precise ↔ Human | Geometric construction, tabular figures, grids | Hand-drawn details, humanist type, candid photography |
| Local ↔ Global | Place-specific motifs, local script or dialect | Neutral systems, Latin-script-first with multiscript planning |

## Competitive perceptual mapping

1. Pick two axes buyers genuinely trade off. Interview language is the best source. Avoid "quality vs price" by default; it puts everyone in the top right.
2. Plot the 8-15 competitors plus the client (current and target). Use dots sized by market presence if known, never invented.
3. Look for whitespace, then ask why it is empty: no demand, impossible economics, or simply unclaimed. Only the last is an opportunity.
4. Make a second, purely visual map: plot competitor primary colors on a hue wheel and tally logo types (wordmark, symbol, combination) and type genres (geometric sans, grotesque, serif, script). The crowded hue and the dominant type genre are what the new identity should usually avoid.

Example axes by category:

| Category | Axis 1 | Axis 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B2B SaaS | Built for specialists ↔ built for everyone | Do-it-yourself tool ↔ done-for-you service |
| Food and beverage | Indulgent ↔ virtuous | Heritage ↔ new-wave |
| Fintech | Guided and protective ↔ self-directed and powerful | Institutional ↔ peer-like |
| Healthcare | Clinical authority ↔ personal companion | Episodic care ↔ continuous care |
| Hospitality | Scripted service ↔ local and spontaneous | Escape ↔ connection |

Output a one-paragraph category code: "Everyone in [category] uses [color], [type genre] and [imagery cliché], and claims [claim]." Then decide which codes are entry tickets (needed for credibility, keep them) and which are clichés (break at least one, deliberately).

## Archetypes

Use archetypes as a consistency tool, not a costume. The atlas holds one entry per archetype with visual translation, voice, fits and clichés.

| ID | Core drive | Typical visual cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `archetype.innocent` | Safety, simplicity, optimism | Light palette, soft rounded forms, clean white space |
| `archetype.sage` | Understanding, truth | Rational serif or sans, editorial grids, restrained color |
| `archetype.explorer` | Freedom, discovery | Landscape imagery, earthy or sky color, rugged textures |
| `archetype.outlaw` | Disruption, liberation | High contrast, black, distressed or raw type |
| `archetype.magician` | Transformation | Deep color, light effects, reveal motion |
| `archetype.hero` | Mastery, courage | Bold sans, dynamic angles, strong primaries |
| `archetype.lover` | Intimacy, sensory pleasure | Didone or script, warm tones, close crops, texture |
| `archetype.jester` | Joy, play | Bright clashing color, irregular shapes, wordplay |
| `archetype.everyperson` | Belonging, honesty | Friendly sans, plain words, real people |
| `archetype.caregiver` | Protection, service | Soft color, rounded humanist type, warm photography |
| `archetype.ruler` | Control, stability | Symmetry, serif caps, deep color, gold or silver |
| `archetype.creator` | Expression, craft | Custom type, visible process, unexpected materials |

Rules:

- Choose one primary. A secondary may color the voice (Sage primary with Jester wit) but must not split the visual language.
- Check tensions: Ruler with Outlaw or Innocent with Outlaw rarely holds together without a deliberate concept.
- Pair with tier: Hero at value tier is sport energy; Hero at luxury is quiet mastery. Run `python3 ../../../library/scripts/atlas.py combine archetype.hero tier.luxury` to see conflicts and pairings.
- Do not default to Hero or Sage for every B2B brief. The swap test applies to archetypes too.

## From strategy to design

Translate every personality attribute into concrete levers before sketching. The table becomes the scoring rubric for territories.

| Attribute | Shape | Type | Color | Imagery | Motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Precise, not cold | Geometric construction with one humanist detail | Grotesque with open apertures; tabular figures | Deep teal or ink, one warm accent | Real people at work, natural light | 160-240ms, standard easing, no bounce |
| Warm, not sentimental | Rounded corners at 25-50% of stroke | Humanist sans or soft serif | Warm neutrals, terracotta or saffron | Candid, close, unposed | Gentle deceleration, 240-320ms |
| Bold, not loud | Heavy, simple silhouette | Condensed or extended display, tight spacing | One saturated color at large scale | Graphic crops, strong shadows | Fast in, slow settle |
| Premium, not exclusive | Fine detail with generous space | High-contrast serif or refined sans | Restrained palette, rich dark neutral | Still life, material texture | Slow, long fades |

## Creative brief for the identity

One page, approved before territories are presented:

1. Objective and what success looks like in 12 months.
2. Primary audience and the one thing they should feel or believe.
3. Single-minded proposition (one sentence).
4. Personality attributes with "not" halves.
5. Mandatories: name, locked assets, legal, scripts and markets, required applications.
6. Deliverables and channels, with the first three applications for testing.
7. Evaluation criteria with weights (for example: distinctiveness 25%, fit to proposition 25%, versatility 20%, small-size legibility 15%, longevity 15%).

## Common failures

- Strategy deck of 60 slides that no designer can use → one-page platform plus the translation table.
- Values like "innovation, integrity, excellence" → behaviors and "not" halves that would change a design decision.
- Positioning based on an unproven claim → label it a hypothesis and plan the test.
- Perceptual map axes chosen to make the client look unique → axes from buyer language, plotted honestly.
- Archetype picked after the logo to justify it → archetype and translation table come first and score the territories.
